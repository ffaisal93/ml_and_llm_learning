## Study one question actively

Try a 60–90 second explanation before opening the answer. For a derivation, write assumptions and symbols; for code, work a small input and an edge case; for a design, define the main invariant and one failure sequence. Then compare with the worked solution.

Score one point each for answering the actual question, explaining the mechanism, checking an example, handling an edge case, and responding to a changed constraint. Record the exact gap and a concrete repair task. Reattempt weak questions after a delay with different numbers.

Do not turn an under-specified exercise into a fabricated numerical answer. Say what information is absent, state your assumptions and show a conditional calculation or algorithm. Behavioral answers should use your real experience, not a made-up story.

## If the interview is tomorrow

Use the first group as a diagnostic instead of attempting to memorize 547 answers overnight. Prioritize the questions you cannot currently explain and the categories matching the role. Work at least one attention/loss implementation, one model-debugging case, one evaluation/design tradeoff and two real project discussions. Leave time to rest.
