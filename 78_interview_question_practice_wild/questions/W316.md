## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">L6 behavioral conducted by a senior Director. Discussion-style — interviewer probes past leadership stories (tree-level and forest-level) and weaves in policy-vs-efficiency dilemmas that feel like trolley-problem variants. No standard &#x27;tell me about a time&#x27; prompts; expect to defend judgments live.

Requirements
60-minute discussion-style behavioral, not a checklist of standard prompts.
Cover past projects at two levels: project-detail (&#x27;the tree&#x27;) and strategic / cross-team (&#x27;the forest&#x27;).
Be ready for policy-vs-efficiency dilemmas presented as hypotheticals — e.g. &#x27;a new safety policy slows down the simulator pipeline; how do you weigh it against shipping velocity?&#x27;
The interviewer is typically a senior D / VP-level engineer; expect direct push-back and follow-up on every claim.
Notes
Structure stories with cause → choice → outcome → reflection. Senior interviewers credit the reflection layer specifically — what would you do differently, what did the experience change in your decision framework.
Tree vs forest. Have two of each kind of story prepared:
Tree-level: concrete engineering decision, with numbers, that you owned end-to-end.
Forest-level: cross-team or organizational decision where you influenced direction without holding the authority.
Policy-vs-efficiency dilemmas. Frame the response around explicit trade-off axes (safety, velocity, cost, fairness, downstream maintenance). Pick a stance and defend it; refusing to commit is a documented red flag at the L6 bar.
Influence without authority. Be ready to describe a specific time you changed another team&#x27;s roadmap. The interviewer will ask &#x27;what would have happened if you hadn&#x27;t pushed&#x27; — have a credible counterfactual.
Pushback handling. When the interviewer challenges a choice, restate the trade-off in their language before defending. This signals you can update on new information rather than reciting a memorized story.
Don&#x27;t oversell. Senior interviewers notice puffed-up impact claims; quantify outcomes conservatively and acknowledge collaborators by name.
Specific Waymo themes that have surfaced: decisions weighing rider safety against fleet utilization; ML model promotion gates; managing cross-team review for safety-critical changes; deprecating a beloved feature for compliance reasons.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Resolve a policy-versus-efficiency tradeoff. Distinguish a mandatory constraint from a process that can be improved through an authorized change.
2. Clarify the purpose, owner and actual requirements of the policy.
3. Find compliant alternatives or propose a reviewed exception with evidence about benefits, risks and controls.
4. Document the decision and measure the result; do not frame secretly bypassing a requirement as good ownership.

### Small example / sanity check

A slow data-access process may be improved with a scoped approved dataset rather than copying unrestricted private data to a convenient location.

### Common trap

Treating speed as justification for ignoring an applicable security or privacy constraint.
