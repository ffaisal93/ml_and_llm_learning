## Worked solution

1. Explain logistic regression from scores to gradients. Logistic regression models binary probability with a linear score passed through a sigmoid.
2. Compute z=xᵀw+b and p=sigmoid(z); p/(1-p)=exp(z), so the log odds are linear.
3. Fit weights by minimizing binary cross-entropy, preferably calculated from logits for stability.
4. The score gradient is p-y, giving weight gradient Xᵀ(p-y)/N under mean reduction. Choose the classification threshold using validation costs and metrics.
5. Explain multicollinearity and stable least squares. Correlated predictors can make coefficient estimates unstable even when predictions remain useful.
6. Write the least-squares objective ||Xw-y||²; inspect rank or singular values of X.
7. Use QR or SVD-based least squares rather than explicitly inverting XᵀX. Centering and scaling can help conditioning.
8. Use ridge if shrinkage is appropriate, and distinguish predictive stability from interpretation of individual coefficients.
9. Compare random forests and gradient boosting. Random forests average diversified trees; boosting adds models sequentially to improve an objective.
10. A forest typically trains trees on bootstrapped samples with feature randomness, reducing variance through averaging.
11. Boosting fits successive updates informed by current errors or loss gradients; learning rate, tree depth and stopping determine its capacity.
12. Compare time-aware validation, calibration, inference cost and sensitivity to hyperparameters rather than selecting by reputation.
13. Why use AdamW, warmup and gradient clipping? They address different training problems: adaptive update scale, early-step instability and unusually large gradients.
14. Adam maintains first and second moment estimates, with bias correction after zero initialization.
15. AdamW applies decoupled weight decay rather than treating decay exactly as another adaptively scaled loss gradient.
16. Warm up learning rate over initial steps and clip the global gradient norm when appropriate. Inspect logs and ablations; these are tuning tools, not correctness fixes.

### Small example / sanity check

A logit of 0 means p=0.5; a logit of log(3) means p=0.75 and odds 3:1.

### Common trap

Calling sigmoid a hard threshold, or assuming a threshold of 0.5 is optimal for every cost structure.
