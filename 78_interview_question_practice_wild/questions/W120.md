## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Explain logistic regression from scores to gradients.

Logistic regression models binary probability with a linear score passed through a sigmoid.

1. Compute z=xᵀw+b and p=sigmoid(z); p/(1-p)=exp(z), so the log odds are linear.
2. Fit weights by minimizing binary cross-entropy, preferably calculated from logits for stability.
3. The score gradient is p-y, giving weight gradient Xᵀ(p-y)/N under mean reduction. Choose the classification threshold using validation costs and metrics.

#### Explain multicollinearity and stable least squares.

Correlated predictors can make coefficient estimates unstable even when predictions remain useful.

1. Write the least-squares objective ||Xw-y||²; inspect rank or singular values of X.
2. Use QR or SVD-based least squares rather than explicitly inverting XᵀX. Centering and scaling can help conditioning.
3. Use ridge if shrinkage is appropriate, and distinguish predictive stability from interpretation of individual coefficients.

#### Compare random forests and gradient boosting.

Random forests average diversified trees; boosting adds models sequentially to improve an objective.

1. A forest typically trains trees on bootstrapped samples with feature randomness, reducing variance through averaging.
2. Boosting fits successive updates informed by current errors or loss gradients; learning rate, tree depth and stopping determine its capacity.
3. Compare time-aware validation, calibration, inference cost and sensitivity to hyperparameters rather than selecting by reputation.

#### Why use AdamW, warmup and gradient clipping?

They address different training problems: adaptive update scale, early-step instability and unusually large gradients.

1. Adam maintains first and second moment estimates, with bias correction after zero initialization.
2. AdamW applies decoupled weight decay rather than treating decay exactly as another adaptively scaled loss gradient.
3. Warm up learning rate over initial steps and clip the global gradient norm when appropriate. Inspect logs and ablations; these are tuning tools, not correctness fixes.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Connect the model, objective and evaluation split

A model class states what relationships can be represented; the objective states which errors training penalizes; the split estimates where those relationships generalize. These are separate choices. For binary logistic regression,

$$
z_i=x_i^\top w+b,\qquad p_i=\sigma(z_i),\qquad
L=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\log(1+e^{z_i})-y_i z_i\right]+\lambda R(w).
$$

Compute the softplus term stably from logits. The derivative of the unregularized per-example loss with respect to its score is $p_i-y_i$. A decision threshold changes actions but does not refit the probability model.

Inspect a simple baseline and training/validation curves before diagnosing high bias or variance. Low training error and poor validation performance may indicate overfitting, entity leakage, an inappropriate split or a genuine distribution change. The remedies differ: regularization does not repair a broken future-information boundary.

Fit scaling, imputation and encoders within training folds. For repeated users, grouped validation can be necessary; for forecasting, preserve the direction of time. Include class imbalance, calibration and subgroup errors rather than reporting only one accuracy number. A model can improve ranking while becoming worse as a probability estimate, and a statistically better estimate can still produce worse decisions at an unsuitable operating threshold.

### Work a small example

A logit of 0 means p=0.5; a logit of log(3) means p=0.75 and odds 3:1.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Calling sigmoid a hard threshold, or assuming a threshold of 0.5 is optimal for every cost structure.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Explain multicollinearity and stable least squares.** If one column is exactly twice another, several coefficient pairs produce the same predictions. The individual coefficients are not identifiable without an extra convention.

Does dropping one correlated variable always improve test prediction? No; compare held-out performance and the intended interpretation.

**Compare random forests and gradient boosting.** A hundred independent high-variance trees can become stable when their errors are weakly correlated; a hundred almost identical trees average away much less error.

Why do forests often need less tuning? Their averaging is relatively robust, though feature leakage and distribution shift still matter.

**Why use AdamW, warmup and gradient clipping?** A gradient [3,4] has norm 5. Clipping its norm to 1 yields [0.6,0.8], preserving direction before the optimizer's own transformation.

Why unscale mixed-precision gradients before clipping? Otherwise the threshold is applied to artificially scaled gradients.
