## Question

Answer four short conceptual questions on the fundamentals of deep learning for computer vision and sensor data. Each answer should be precise and fit in a few sentences, with the key formula where one exists.

### Questions

1. CNN vs MLP. When and why is a convolutional network preferred over a plain multilayer perceptron for image or sensor-grid input? Address parameter count and spatial structure.
2. Residual connections. What does a residual block compute, and why do residual connections make very deep networks trainable?
3. Activation functions. Can y = 0.01x serve as an activation layer? State what this implies about non-linearity and explain what makes Leaky ReLU useful. Compare it with sigmoid, tanh and ReLU.
4. Gaussian distribution. Give the density of N(mu, sigma^2), the 68-95-99.7 rule, and the distribution of a sum of independent Gaussians. Explain why Gaussian noise is a common assumption for sensor measurements.

## Worked solution

### CNN versus MLP

A 3×3 convolution from 3 to 32 channels has $3\cdot3\cdot3\cdot32+32=896$ parameters independent of image size. A dense map from a 224×224×3 image to 32 features has $224\cdot224\cdot3\cdot32+32=4,816,928$ parameters. Shared local filters respect spatial neighborhoods and translation equivariance, useful for images/sensor grids where nearby measurements relate. Convolutions need depth/dilation for global context; translation assumptions can be wrong for channels/coordinates with unique meaning, and pooling can discard precise location.

### Residual connections

A residual block computes $y=x+F(x;\theta)$, or $y=P(x)+F(x)$ with a projection when shapes differ. Its Jacobian includes $I+J_F$, giving gradients a direct path and making learning a correction to an existing representation easier. This reduces optimization degradation at depth, not a guarantee against vanishing/exploding gradients; normalization, initialization and residual scaling still matter.

### Activations

$y=.01x$ is a valid linear layer but supplies no nonlinearity: stacked affine maps with it collapse to one affine map. Leaky ReLU is $\max(x,\alpha x)$ with $0<\alpha<1$; its negative-side slope avoids completely dead negative units while positive values retain slope one. Ordinary ReLU has zero negative slope and can die. Sigmoid maps to (0,1), useful for binary probabilities but saturates at both tails and is not zero-centered; tanh maps to (-1,1), is centered but also saturates. Leaky ReLU is unbounded and still requires appropriate activation scale; its kink uses a chosen subgradient.

### Gaussian measurements

$$f(x)=\frac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\left[-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right],\quad\sigma>0.$$

About 68%, 95% and 99.7% lie within 1, 2 and 3 standard deviations. For independent $X_i\sim N(\mu_i,\sigma_i^2)$, $\sum_iX_i\sim N(\sum_i\mu_i,\sum_i\sigma_i^2)$. Correlation adds covariance terms to the variance; Gaussian marginals alone do not establish independence. Many small additive measurement disturbances motivate a Gaussian approximation through central-limit behavior, but bias, clipping, correlated drift and heavy-tailed faults violate it. Check sensor residuals rather than assuming every outlier is impossible.
