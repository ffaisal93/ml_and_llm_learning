## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">You are talking with the manager of a team you may join. The conversation decides whether your background fits the open role and the team&#x27;s current needs. It is a two-way evaluation: you are expected to present yourself concisely and to ask informed questions.

What to prepare
1.
Background pitch (5-10 minutes). Walk through your career at resume-highlight level: roles, scope, and the thread that connects them to this role. Prepare a 30-second version as well.
2.
Project summaries. Describe two or three projects at elevator-pitch level: the problem, your specific contribution, and the measurable outcome.
3.
Questions for the manager (3-5). Ask substantive questions about the team&#x27;s structure, current roadmap and open problems, the scope of the role, and the growth path from your level to the next.
4.
Motivation. Give a concise answer to &quot;why are you looking&quot; and &quot;what would make this role compelling&quot;.
Constraints
The conversation lasts about 45 minutes, so the pitch, the projects and your questions must all fit.
The manager decides whether the background fits the specific needs of the open role, so tie every answer to the role&#x27;s scope.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Present your background in ninety seconds. Connect your real experience to the role through a small number of relevant capabilities and outcomes.
2. Start with your current focus and the kinds of problems you solve.
3. Choose one or two concrete projects, naming your role, difficult decision and measured result.
4. Finish with why the target work fits your interests and what you want to contribute, without inventing credentials or metrics.

### Small example / sanity check

An MLE introduction can connect data-quality work, reliable serving and one measured deployment result instead of listing every library used.

### Common trap

Reciting the entire resume chronologically or making claims you cannot defend under follow-up.
