## Question

An open-ended product round: pick a part of Reddit's onboarding flow and propose improvements. The interviewer cares about the framework (user segmentation, goal definition, hypothesis generation, measurement) more than the specific ideas. Thinking-out-loud exercise.

Expected
Define which onboarding flow is in scope (new-user first-session, signed-out-to-signed-in, mobile-vs-web, default-feed personalization).
Pick one user segment and articulate its first-session goal.
Identify 2-3 friction points against that goal.
Propose 2-3 improvements, each tied to a hypothesis about why it moves the metric.
Define measurement (success metric, guardrail, decision criterion).

For an ML Platform candidate, the strongest framing tilts toward a personalization/cold-start-recommender lens. Avoid feature-list answers; narrate segmentation -&gt; goal -&gt; hypothesis -&gt; metric.

## Worked solution

### Focus on a new user's first useful feed

Choose mobile first-session onboarding for a new signed-in user who arrives without following any communities. Their goal is to find a few relevant, welcoming discussions quickly, not to complete a long questionnaire. This is a proposed experiment, not a claim about the current Reddit UI or a measured outcome.

Three hypotheses connect friction to action. First, a long interest survey delays value: offer three optional broad interests with an immediate skip and show a provisional feed. Second, unfamiliar community names make selection difficult: show sample posts and a short explanation before asking for a follow. Third, a single accidental tap can overpersonalize cold start: blend selected interests with diverse, safety-filtered exploration and make “less like this” visible. A simple rules/popularity baseline can establish whether a learned cold-start ranker adds value.

The recommender consumes only permitted first-session signals—selected topics, language/region and explicit skips—then updates a short-lived session profile. User→candidate community/post features produce a small, diverse candidate set; do not infer sensitive interests from sparse ambiguous behavior. Keep a nonpersonalized fallback and a clear way to reset choices. Exploration yields labels, but clicks caused by position or sensational content are not equivalent to satisfaction.

Use seven-day retained newcomers who had a meaningful first-session interaction as the primary experiment metric, with an explicit definition such as saving/following or sustained reading rather than raw scroll count. Secondary metrics are time to first useful action, relevant-community follow rate and survey abandonment. Guardrails include p95 first-feed latency, harmful-content exposure, hides/reports and early uninstall/exit. Randomize users before onboarding, predefine duration/sample size and a minimum useful lift, and compare against the current flow with stable instrumentation. Segment by language/acquisition channel without choosing the winner from a lucky subgroup.

A decision criterion might be a statistically credible retention improvement above a predefined practical margin with no material harm/latency regression; the exact margin needs baseline traffic/variance. Start with the smallest UI+rules change and shadow the ML scorer before online ranking. A list of features without segment→friction→hypothesis→metric reasoning would miss the product question.
