## Question

This problem has four parts on the data behind a machine-learning project: where it comes from, whether it may be used, how it is cleaned, and how to keep evaluation honest.

Task

Choose one machine-learning project you have built and answer the following.

Part 1 — Provenance and labels

For your project, state:
1. The source of the data (collected, purchased, scraped, generated by a model, or inherited), its volume, and the time window it covers.
2. Your right to use it: licence or terms of service, consent, and how personal data was handled.
3. Who produced the labels, against what guideline, and how label quality was measured (for example inter-annotator agreement or an audited sample), with the numbers.
Part 2 — Cleaning and splitting
1. Describe how you removed exact duplicates and near-duplicates, and how many examples each step removed.
2. Specify how you split train, validation and test, and by which unit (row, user, document, session, or time). Justify the unit.
Part 3 — Augmentation leakage (quantitative)

A dataset of N base examples is augmented into K near-identical copies per example, giving N·K rows. The rows are then split at random, with a fraction f of them going to test.
1. Derive, as a function of K and f, the probability that a test row has at least one sibling (a copy of the same base example) in the training set. Evaluate it for K = 5 and f = 0.2.
2. Suppose every base example carries a random binary label, so no model can truly exceed 50% accuracy. What accuracy would a 1-nearest-neighbour model report on this split, and why?
3. Give the correct procedure, and state where augmentation belongs relative to the split.
Part 4 — Contamination and scale
1. Describe how you would detect overlap between a training corpus and a public evaluation set, including the matching method and its window size.
2. If the dataset had to grow 100×, which part of the pipeline would break first, what would you automate, and what would you keep under human review?

## Worked solution

### Personal provenance must come from records

No candidate project, license, label audit or removal counts were supplied. A completed personal answer must name the source, collection window, raw/retained volume, permission basis, consent restrictions, PII retention/deletion policy, label producers, guideline version and audited agreement/error counts. For each claimed count, retain the corresponding manifest or audit. A useful statement is “Of the audited [number] examples, [number] were adjudicated incorrect”; neither slot can honestly be filled from this question.

Track the cleaning funnel by unique source id: raw → invalid/unpermitted → exact duplicate → near duplicate → retained. Normalize only semantically irrelevant differences before hashing; use shingle similarity with human-reviewed thresholds for near duplicates. Keep duplicate-family ids. Split at the real unit of independence: patient/user/session/document family, or time for future prediction. Fit preprocessing on training data and preserve a frozen final test set. A row split is defensible only when rows are independent for the intended deployment.

### Derive augmentation leakage

Condition on a particular row being in test. Under independent row assignment with test probability $f$, its other $K-1$ siblings are all in test with probability $f^{K-1}$. Therefore

$$P(\text{training sibling}\mid\text{test row})=1-f^{K-1}.$$

For $K=5,f=.2$, the leak probability is $1-.2^4=.9984$, or **99.84%**. A 1-NN classifier that recognizes copies predicts perfectly on leaked examples. On an unseen base example whose label is an independent fair bit, its expected accuracy is $.5$. Thus expected reported accuracy is

$$(1-f^{K-1})\cdot1+f^{K-1}\cdot .5=1-\tfrac12f^{K-1}=.9992.$$

This is **99.92%** apparent accuracy despite 50% true unseen-base accuracy. It assumes siblings are reliably nearest and base labels are independent; augmentation that changes label semantics or neighbor geometry changes the result. If the split fixes exactly $m$ test rows out of $NK$ rather than independent assignments, the exact all-siblings-test probability is $\binom{m-1}{K-1}/\binom{NK-1}{K-1}$, approximately $f^{K-1}$ at large $N$.

Split base examples or their full duplicate groups **before** augmentation. Generate training augmentations only from training bases. If augmented test views are useful for robustness, keep them in their test family and aggregate uncertainty by base id. Never tune augmentation on final test results.

### Contamination and 100-fold growth

For text, normalize whitespace/Unicode conservatively, exact-hash full documents, and compare rolling 13-token shingles against evaluation prompts and reference answers; use MinHash/LSH to propose near-document matches and verify overlap. Thirteen is an explicit starting choice, not a universal threshold: use smaller task-aware matching for short answers, and inspect false matches from formulas/boilerplate. Include transformed formats, answer-only copies and semantic paraphrases in a targeted human audit. Report overlap counts, confidence and quarantined families; absence of an exact match is not proof of no contamination.

At 100×, first measure the bottleneck: exhaustive pairwise near-duplicate comparisons grow quadratically and are a likely first failure. Replace them with partitioned hashing/LSH and distributed verification; stream parsing and quality checks, maintain versioned manifests and sample audits. Automate schema validation, permissions enforcement, duplicate candidate generation, split-family checks and reproducible transforms. Keep humans on permission ambiguities, label-policy disagreement, rare languages, high-impact errors and deduplication threshold audits. Scale the independent audit sample to its desired confidence, not blindly to the full corpus size.
