## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">This problem has four parts on the data behind a machine-learning project: where it comes from, whether it may be used, how it is cleaned, and how to keep evaluation honest.

Task

Choose one machine-learning project you have built and answer the following.

Part 1 — Provenance and labels

For your project, state:

1.
The source of the data (collected, purchased, scraped, generated by a model, or inherited), its volume, and the time window it covers.
2.
Your right to use it: licence or terms of service, consent, and how personal data was handled.
3.
Who produced the labels, against what guideline, and how label quality was measured (for example inter-annotator agreement or an audited sample), with the numbers.
Part 2 — Cleaning and splitting
1.
Describe how you removed exact duplicates and near-duplicates, and how many examples each step removed.
2.
Specify how you split train, validation and test, and by which unit (row, user, document, session, or time). Justify the unit.
Part 3 — Augmentation leakage (quantitative)

A dataset of N base examples is augmented into K near-identical copies per example, giving N·K rows. The rows are then split at random, with a fraction f of them going to test.

1.
Derive, as a function of K and f, the probability that a test row has at least one sibling (a copy of the same base example) in the training set. Evaluate it for K = 5 and f = 0.2.
2.
Suppose every base example carries a random binary label, so no model can truly exceed 50% accuracy. What accuracy would a 1-nearest-neighbour model report on this split, and why?
3.
Give the correct procedure, and state where augmentation belongs relative to the split.
Part 4 — Contamination and scale
1.
Describe how you would detect overlap between a training corpus and a public evaluation set, including the matching method and its window size.
2.
If the dataset had to grow 100×, which part of the pipeline would break first, what would you automate, and what would you keep under human review?</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
2. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
3. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
4. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.
5. Audit human-labeled training data before scaling it. More labels help only if they carry the intended signal and match the task definition.
6. Inspect the sampling frame, instructions, class balance, duplicates, annotator disagreement and gold-label checks.
7. Separate ambiguous examples from clear errors; compare disagreement across languages, subgroups and content types.
8. Run a controlled data-quality intervention against a fixed model and evaluation set before attributing improvements to architecture.

### Small example / sanity check

Improving annotation guidelines while holding the model fixed can show a clearer causal contribution than changing data and architecture together.

### Common trap

Calling the dataset “clean” without saying what was checked.
