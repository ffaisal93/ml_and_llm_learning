## Question

### Description

Three methods dominate the fine-tuning of language models toward human or automatic feedback: PPO, DPO and GRPO. They differ in what data they consume, which extra models they keep in memory, and whether they sample from the policy during training. Compare them precisely enough that someone could implement each loss and pick the right method for a project.

The three methods are:

PPO: Proximal Policy Optimization, as used in RLHF with a learned reward model.
DPO: Direct Preference Optimization, the form with a frozen reference policy.
GRPO: Group Relative Policy Optimization, the critic-free method introduced with DeepSeekMath.
What to answer
1. Losses. Write the exact training loss each method minimizes, and define every symbol. For PPO include the clipped surrogate, the value-function loss, the entropy bonus and the KL penalty to the reference policy.
2. DPO derivation. Show how the DPO loss follows from the KL-regularized reward-maximization objective, and explain why no explicit reward model is needed.
3. Requirements. For each method, list what it needs: a reward model or reward function, a value model, a reference model, preference pairs, or several samples per prompt. Say whether it trains online (sampling from the current policy) or offline.
4. Intuition, advantages and disadvantages. One short intuition per method, then its main strengths and weaknesses.
5. When to use which. Recommend a method for each case below and justify it.
(a) A dataset of human preference pairs and a small compute budget.
(b) Math or code tasks whose answers a program can check.
(c) A learned reward model with a need for online exploration, or a multi-step control task such as robotics.
Expected depth
Notation is free, but each loss must be correct up to sign and constant factors, and must say whether it is minimized or maximized.
Use one worked number for DPO and one for GRPO to show the loss or the advantage on a small case.
A compact comparison table at the end is expected.

## Worked solution

### PPO: minimize a clipped actor–critic objective

For rollout policy $\pi_{old}$, trainable actor $\pi_\theta$, frozen reference $\pi_{ref}$, sampled token/action $a_t$ in state/prefix $s_t$, define $r_t=\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)/\pi_{old}(a_t|s_t)$. Let $\hat A_t$ be a detached return/GAE advantage, $\hat R_t$ a detached return target and $V_\phi$ a learned critic. One explicit minimized RLHF loss is

$$L_{PPO}=-E_t\min(r_t\hat A_t,\operatorname{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_t)+c_vE_t(V_\phi(s_t)-\hat R_t)^2-c_HE_tH(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t))+\beta E_tD_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t)\|\pi_{ref}(\cdot|s_t)).$$

All coefficients are nonnegative; entropy encourages exploration, KL discourages reference drift, critic estimates expected reward. Apply masks/normalization to valid action tokens consistently. The scalar reward comes from a learned reward model or environment/function; this specified RLHF setup uses a learned reward model. Some implementations put KL into rewards/advantages instead: do not count it both there and in the explicit loss above. Old actor and reference serve different roles. Clipping limits a surrogate incentive, not a hard KL guarantee. Intuition: improve rewarded sampled choices without taking an uncontrolled step; the cost is rollout, critic/reward/reference memory and tuning.

### Derive DPO from the regularized reward objective

For each prompt x maximize $\sum_y\pi(y|x)R(x,y)-\beta\sum_y\pi(y|x)\log[\pi(y|x)/\pi_{ref}(y|x)]$, subject to probabilities summing to1. Differentiating with a Lagrange multiplier yields $\pi^*(y|x)=\pi_{ref}(y|x)e^{R(x,y)/\beta}/Z(x)$. Rearranging gives $R(x,y)=\beta\log[\pi^*(y|x)/\pi_{ref}(y|x)]+\beta\log Z(x)$.

Under Bradley–Terry $P(y_w\succ y_l|x)=\sigma(R_w-R_l)$, prompt-specific logZ cancels. Replace the optimal policy by the trainable one and minimize

$$L_{DPO}=-E_{(x,w,l)}\log\sigma\left(\beta\left[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_w|x)}-\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l|x)}{\pi_{ref}(y_l|x)}\right]\right).$$

Response log probabilities sum answer-token terms, with prompt/padding excluded. No separately trained reward model/critic is needed because reward differences are parameterized by these reference-relative policy scores. For ratios winner2 and loser.5 with beta=.5, margin=.5log4=.693147, so loss=-log(2/3)=**.405465**. At equal reference-relative scores it is log2. Intuition: increase the winner's advantage over loser relative to reference. Static pairs make it cheap/offline, but pair errors, length bias and lack of new exploration remain.

### GRPO: an explicit critic-free outcome-reward variant

For G responses $y_i\sim\pi_{old}(\cdot|x)$, score rewards R_i and set detached $A_i=(R_i-\bar R)/(s_R+\delta)$ using population group standard deviation. With response length T_i, token ratios r_it, and declared reference penalty K_it, minimize

$$L_{GRPO}=-E\frac1G\sum_i\frac1{T_i}\sum_t\left[\min(r_{it}A_i,\operatorname{clip}(r_{it},1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_i)-\beta K_{it}\right].$$

A commonly used nonnegative sampled KL estimator is $K=\pi_{ref}(a|s)/\pi_\theta(a|s)-\log[\pi_{ref}(a|s)/\pi_\theta(a|s)]-1$; specify the sampling/reduction convention because variants differ. Rewards `[0,0,1,1]` have mean.5/std.5 and advantages approximately `[-1,-1,1,1]`; equal rewards give zero centered learning signal (KL may still update). Intuition: learn from siblings that outperform their group, avoiding a critic while paying for multiple rollouts and risking noisy/zero-variance rewards. This is the outcome-supervision formulation associated with [DeepSeekMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300); process-reward advantages are another variant.

### Pick by the available signal

| Method | Feedback/data | Extra models/state | Sampling | Best fit and main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPO | Online scalar reward; often learned reward model | Critic, frozen reference, rollout actor snapshot | Fresh rollouts with limited reuse | (c) Learned reward/exploration or multi-step control; expensive critic/rollout stability |
| DPO | Preferred/rejected pairs | Frozen reference; no explicit critic/reward model | Offline | (a) Human pairs/small budget; limited coverage and preference bias |
| GRPO | Reward function/model and G answers/prompt | Reference in this variant; no critic | Online groups | (b) Verifiable math/code; checker exploitation, long-rollout cost, uninformative groups |

Online here means new rollouts sampled between policy-update cycles, often with several minibatch updates against an old snapshot; it does not mean sampling anew for every gradient operation. Validate task success and regression slices independently of the optimized reward/loss.
