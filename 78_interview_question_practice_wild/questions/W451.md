## Question

Expected Handshakes in 10x10 Grid Dice Rolls

Given a 10 × 10 grid where each cell represents a person. Each person rolls a dice and they shake hands with their neighbors if they roll the same number. What is the expected total number of handshakes?

Example

### Input

```text
10 10
```

## Worked solution

### Count neighbor edges, not people

Assume independent fair six-sided dice, one handshake per unordered pair of **orthogonally adjacent** people, and no wraparound. The grid has 10×9 horizontal plus 9×10 vertical edges, totaling 180. For each edge e, indicator I_e=1 when endpoints match; $P(I_e=1)=6(1/6)^2=1/6$. Linearity of expectation gives $E[\sum_e I_e]=180/6=30$. Shared people do not require independent handshake indicators for this expectation.

“Neighbors” is otherwise ambiguous. If diagonals also count, add 2×9×9=162 diagonal edges: expectation 342/6=57. If dice are not fair, match probability is $\sum_{j=1}^6p_j^2$ for identical independent dice, or $\sum_jp_jq_j$ for different endpoint dice. Correlated rolls require their joint match probability. A 1×1 grid has zero edges; for general m × n with orthogonal neighbors the answer is $[m(n-1)+n(m-1)]/6$. Counting four neighbors per person and dividing by two without correcting corners/boundaries would overcount. Tests enumerate grid edges and fair-die pairs for tiny grids and confirm the 10×10 results under both stated neighbor definitions.
