## Question

For $d>0$ and $\sigma>0$, maximize $f(x) = \exp\!\big(-(x-d/2)^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})\big) + \exp\!\big(-(x+d/2)^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})\big)$. Derive stationarity and the midpoint second derivative; classify the boundary; characterize nonzero maxima. Analyze $d = \sigma$ and $d = 4\sigma$.

## Worked solution

### Reduce stationarity to a fixed-point equation

Set a=d/2. Write $f(x)=2e^{-(x^2+a^2)/(2\sigma^2)}\cosh(ax/\sigma^2)$. Its log derivative is

$$\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}=\frac{-x+a\tanh(ax/\sigma^2)}{\sigma^2}.$$

Thus every stationary point satisfies $x=a\tanh(ax/\sigma^2)$, and x=0 always qualifies. Direct differentiation gives

$$f''(0)=2e^{-a^2/(2\sigma^2)}\frac{a^2-\sigma^2}{\sigma^4}.$$

If d<2sigma, tangent slope $a^2/\sigma^2<1$ and the positive-side fixed-point difference is strictly negative: zero is the unique stationary point and global maximum. If d>2 sigma, zero is a local minimum and there is exactly one positive stationary point x* in (0, a), with its negative mirror. Strict concavity of tanh on the positive half-line gives uniqueness; both nonzero points are global maxima since f tends to zero at infinity.

At the boundary d=2 sigma the second derivative vanishes, but zero is still the unique global maximum. Expanding the log gives $\log f(x)=\log f(0)-x^4/(12\sigma^4)+O(x^6)$, a flat quartic maximum, not a minimum or a plateau of maximizers.

For d=sigma, a=sigma/2 and the maximum is x=0 with value $2e^{-1/8}\approx1.7650$. For d=4 sigma, a=2 sigma: solve $u=2\tanh(2u)$ to obtain $u=x/\sigma\approx1.99865$, so maxima lie near ±1.99865 sigma, slightly inside the two Gaussian centers, not exactly at them. Bisection on a positive bracket away from zero is safer than Newton initialized at zero, which remains at the wrong stationary point in the bimodal case. The midpoint's value there is $2e^{-2}\approx.2707$, whereas either peak is about 1.00034.
