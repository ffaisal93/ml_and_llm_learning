## Question

For d&gt;0 and sigma&gt;0, maximize f(x)=exp(-(x-d/2)²/(2*sigma²))+exp(-(x+d/2)²/(2*sigma²)). Derive stationarity and the midpoint second derivative; classify the boundary; characterize nonzero maxima. Analyze d=sigma and d=4*sigma.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Set a=d/2. The target-density sum is 2*exp(-(x²+a²)/(2*sigma²))*cosh(a*x/sigma²), an even function, so x=0 is stationary.
2. Its second derivative at zero is 2*exp(-a²/(2*sigma²))*(a²-sigma²)/sigma^4. If $d<2\sigma$ the midpoint is the unique maximum; if $d>2\sigma$ it is a local minimum.
3. The stationarity equation is x=a*tanh(a*x/sigma²). At d=2*sigma, $\tanh(z)<z$ for $z>0$ implies no positive stationary root and the midpoint remains the global maximum, despite zero second derivative.
4. For $d>2\sigma$ there are symmetric maxima ±x_star with $0<x_*<a$. Solve the positive root numerically by bracketing while excluding zero; as separation grows it approaches the positive target center from inside.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Name the random experiment and condition on the right event

Write the sample space and independence assumptions before counting. Bayes' rule reverses conditioning:

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0.
$$

A $90\%$ detection rate is not a $90\%$ probability that an alert is genuine. Prevalence and false-positive rate determine the latter. Work expected counts in a hypothetical population when the formula feels abstract.

For waiting-time and random-walk exercises, first-step analysis often gives a solvable recurrence:

$$
h(s)=1+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s)h(s'),\qquad h(s)=0\text{ at absorbing states}.
$$

Specify what counts as one step: two particles moving once each may cost two coin flips but only one elapsed second. At a forced horizon, all surviving probability mass must be assigned to the final stopping time.

For expected counts, indicator variables and linearity do not require independent indicators. For uncertainty or variance, dependence does matter. Check normalization, limiting cases and a small enumeration. Simulation is useful validation but not a replacement for a requested exact derivation; conversely, a neat-looking fraction should not be asserted merely because a numerical estimate is close to it.

#### Put the result in mathematical notation

Writing $a=d/2$,
$$f(x)=2e^{-(x^2+a^2)/(2\sigma^2)}\cosh(ax/\sigma^2),\qquad
f''(0)=2e^{-a^2/(2\sigma^2)}\frac{a^2-\sigma^2}{\sigma^4}.$$
Stationary points satisfy $x=a\tanh(ax/\sigma^2)$. At $d=2\sigma$, the second derivative vanishes but $\tanh z<z$ for $z>0$ still makes the midpoint the unique maximum. For $d>2\sigma$, nonzero maxima lie strictly inside the two target centers.

### Work a small example

For d=sigma aim at zero. For d=4*sigma, x_star is about 1.99865*sigma, slightly inside the target center 2*sigma.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Calling the zero second derivative at the boundary sufficient to classify the point, or asserting the optimum jumps exactly to a target center.
