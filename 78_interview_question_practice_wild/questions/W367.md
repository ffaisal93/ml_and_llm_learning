## Question

Number Logic — Find the Next Term

For each integer or fraction sequence below, infer the generating rule and give the next term. Work by hand, without a calculator. A rule is only acceptable if it reproduces every given term.

Sequences

```text
1. 19 18 20 60 15 14 16 ?
2. 58 8 50 10 40 15 ?
3. -12 -11 -7 2 18 43 ?
4. 1  3/2  4  9½  23  55½ ?
5. 7 6 8 24 6 5 7 ?
6. 5 3 8 9 11 27 ?
7. 3 1 2 6 12 144 ?
8. 2 1 3 4 7 11 18 ?
```

### Requirements

Give the next term and state the rule in one sentence for each sequence.
Explain a systematic order in which to test candidate rules (differences, ratios, interleaved streams, recurrences, products, named lists) and how you decide a rule is right.
Sequences 1, 5 and 6 hide their rule in the positions of the terms (groups, a repeating operator cycle, interleaved streams); say how you detect each.

## Worked solution

### Compact rules that reproduce every given term

| Sequence | Next | One-sentence rule and verification |
|---|---:|---|
|1|48|Repeat operators −1,+2,×3,÷4:19 → 18 → 20 → 60 → 15 → 14 → 16 → 48.|
|2|25|Odd-position terms subtract the intervening even term (58−8=50, 50−10=40, 40−15=25); the even stream8,10, 15 has increments2,5,… increasing by3.|
|3|79|Add successive squares1,4, 9, 16, 25, 36:−12→−11→−7 → 2 → 18 → 43 → 79.|
|4|134|From the third term use a_n=2a_(n−1)+a_(n−2):4=2(3/2)+1, 19/2=2(4)+3/2, 23=2(19/2)+4,111/2=2(23)+19/2, then2(111/2)+23=134.|
|5|21|Repeat −1,+2,×3,÷4:7 → 6 → 8 → 24 → 6 → 5 → 7 → 21.|
|6|14|Odd positions5,8, 11, 14 add3; even positions3,9, 27 multiply by3.|
|7|10368|After seeds3,1, 2 each term is the product of the previous three: 6, 12, 144, then6·12·144=10368.|
|8|29|After seeds2,1 each term sums the previous two: 3, 4, 7, 11, 18, 29.|

Sequence2's even-stream rule can be made fully explicit as e_1=8, e_m=e_(m−1)+(3m−4) for m≥2; it produces every supplied even term without guessing an unstated future one. The next requested term is odd and already determined by40−15. Sequence4 uses exact halves, avoiding decimal calculator dependence; 6·12·144=72·144=10080+288=10368 by hand.

### A systematic search and the positional clues

First compute small integer differences and ratios and look for constant/higher differences or named simple lists (sequence3 exposes squares). Next test short repeating operator cycles and grouped blocks; sequence1's19/18/20/60 then15/14/16 suggests the same within-group−1,+2,×3 progression with÷4 between groups, and sequence5 confirms that cycle independently. Then split odd/even or mod-r positions: sequence6 immediately becomes arithmetic versus geometric streams. Try short additive/product recurrences (4, 7, 8), checking each eligible index from the start, and consider named sequences only if they explain all terms without extra exceptions.

Reject a candidate that fits only the final pair, changes operations ad hoc or fails one earlier value. A finite prefix has infinitely many continuations (one can interpolate a polynomial or append a special case), so these are simple consistent intended-style rules, not mathematically unique necessities. State the rule with the number; a bare list of guesses does not demonstrate it.
