Requirements

Explain a systematic order in which to test candidate rules (differences, ratios, interleaved streams, recurrences, products, named lists) and how you decide a rule is right.
Sequences 1, 5 and 6 hide their rule in the positions of the terms (groups, a repeating operator cycle, interleaved streams); say how you detect each.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. 1: repeat operators -1,+2,*3,/4, giving 48. 2: alternating subtraction and subtraction-amount stream gives 58-8=50,50-10=40,40-15=25, so next is 25.
2. 3: successive differences 1,4,9,16,25 lead to +36 and 79. 4: from the third term use a_n=2*a_(n-1)+a_(n-2), giving 2*55.5+23=134.
3. 5: the same -1,+2,*3,/4 cycle gives 21. 6: odd positions 5,8,11,14 rise by 3 and even positions 3,9,27 multiply by 3, so next is 14.
4. 7: after the first three seeds, each term is the product of its previous three: 3*1*2=6,1*2*6=12,2*6*12=144; next is 6*12*144=10368. 8: each term is the preceding two summed, giving 29.
5. Test differences/ratios, periodic operators, interleaved positions and short recurrences, and verify all supplied terms. A finite prefix admits infinitely many continuations; these are compact consistent rules, not a proof of unique mathematical necessity.

### Small example / sanity check

The next-term list under these rules is [48,25,79,134,21,14,10368,29]. Show the rule, not only the number.

### Common trap

Fitting only the final two terms or claiming a plausible continuation is the only possible sequence.
