## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Tell the story of a time you strongly disagreed with a technical or product decision made by your team or a more senior person. Interviewers use this question to see how you handle conflict: whether you raise concerns with evidence, stay respectful, and move the work forward whatever the outcome.

What to deliver

Prepare a spoken answer of about three minutes built on one real situation from your own work. Answer all of these parts, in order:

1.
Situation: What was the decision, who made it, and why did you disagree? State your role and what was at stake.
2.
Evidence: What facts, data or experiments did you gather to test your own view? Did you consider that you might be wrong?
3.
Action: Whom did you talk to, in which order, and how did you phrase the disagreement? What alternative did you propose?
4.
Resolution: What was decided in the end? If the decision went against you, how did you commit to it? If it went your way, how did you treat the people who disagreed?
5.
Result and learning: What happened afterward, and what would you do differently? Describe results honestly; do not invent numbers.
Example of a strong opening

&quot;Our team planned to launch a ranking model after an evaluation that only used overall accuracy. I owned the evaluation pipeline, not the launch decision, and I was worried that the model did much worse on a small user segment. I computed per-segment metrics before raising the concern.&quot;

The opening names the decision, the speaker&#x27;s role and the concern, and it shows evidence-gathering before escalation.

Example of a weak opening

&quot;I disagreed with my manager, so I told everyone the plan was wrong and we argued until he gave in.&quot;

It has no evidence, no alternative, and no collaborative resolution.

Constraints
Use one concrete story, not a general philosophy of disagreement.
Spend about 60% of the time on your actions, because the interviewer is evaluating your behavior.
Use &quot;I&quot; for your own actions and &quot;we&quot; for team outcomes.
Do not blame colleagues; describe their reasoning fairly.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. How do you handle disagreement over an ML design? Make the disputed assumptions testable and connect the decision to the shared objective.
2. Restate the other person's concern fairly and identify whether the disagreement concerns facts, constraints or preferences.
3. Propose a small experiment or decision table with agreed success criteria, cost and deadline.
4. Document the decision, support execution after it is made and revisit only when meaningful new evidence appears.

### Small example / sanity check

A debate over retrieval complexity can become a measured comparison of answer quality, p95 latency and maintenance cost on a fixed query set.

### Common trap

Describing a colleague as incompetent or presenting “I won the argument” as the result.
