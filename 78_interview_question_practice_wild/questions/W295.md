## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Solve a timed set of short probability and expected-value problems, giving each answer as a number accurate enough to pick the closest of several multiple-choice options.

Format
14 independent parts. Budget about 90 seconds per part.
For each part, give the value as an exact fraction or a decimal to 3 significant figures, plus a one-line justification.
Fast estimation is acceptable when the exact computation is slow, as long as the estimate is within about 5% of the true value.
Parts
1.
A fair die is thrown twice. What is the probability that the second face differs from the first?
2.
A standard 52-card deck is shuffled and the top 10 cards are discarded unseen. What is the probability that the new top card is red?
3.
Two fair dice are thrown. What is the probability that their sum is 11 or 12?
4.
A fair coin is flipped 3 times. What is the probability that all three outcomes are the same?
5.
61 coins are placed at random into 15 boxes. What is the probability that at least one box holds more than 4 coins?
6.
A bag holds 10 red, 10 yellow and 10 blue balls. Balls are drawn uniformly at random without replacement until one colour is used up (only two colours remain in the bag). What is the expected number of balls left in the bag?
7.
A fair die is rolled repeatedly and the running sum is kept, stopping as soon as the sum exceeds 100. What is the probability that the last roll was a 2?
8.
Two particles start on opposite vertices of a regular octagon. Every second, each particle independently moves one edge clockwise or anticlockwise with probability 1/2 each. What is the expected number of seconds until they are on the same vertex? How many coin flips is that?
9.
A particle starts on a vertex of a regular decagon. Every second a fair die is rolled: on 1 or 2 it moves one edge clockwise, on 3 or 4 one edge anticlockwise, on 5 or 6 it stays. What is the expected number of seconds until it first reaches the vertex opposite its start?
10.
A particle starts at lattice point (5, 5) of the grid of points (x, y) with 0 &lt;= x, y &lt;= 10. Each step it moves up, down, left or right with probability 1/4 each. What is the expected number of steps until it first reaches a point with x or y equal to 0 or 10?
11.
Start with USD 10 and wager USD 1 per independent round, stopping at USD 0 or USD 20. Find the probability of reaching USD 20 for win probabilities (a) 0.5 and (b) 0.6.
12.
Six fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of (a) more heads than tails, (b) at most 3 heads?
13.
A machine outputs integers uniformly from 1 to 10. You and a friend each take the sum of two independent outputs. What is the probability that the two sums are equal?
14.
An integer is chosen uniformly from 1 to 1,000,000. What is the probability that its decimal representation contains the digit 7 at least once?
Input

None: each part is self-contained.

Output

Fourteen numeric answers (parts 8, 11 and 12 have two values each), each with a short justification.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. 1: P(second die differs)=5/6. 2: after unseen discards, any remaining position is exchangeable with an original card, so red probability=1/2. 3: sums 11/12 have 3 ordered outcomes, giving 1/12. 4: HHH or TTT gives 2/8=1/4. 5: 15 boxes with at most 4 each hold at most 60 coins, so the 61st forces the event with probability 1.
2. 6: Let E(a,b,c) be expected remaining balls until a color empties. Base E= a+b+c when any component is zero; otherwise E=[aE(a-1,b,c)+bE(a,b-1,c)+cE(a,b,c-1)]/(a+b+c). Memoization gives E(10,10,10)=310/77≈4.025974.
3. 7: Let u(0)=1 and u(s)=sum_(d=1..6,s>=d)u(s-d)/6 for reachable sums through 100. Last roll 2 has probability [u(99)+u(100)]/6≈0.0952381. 8: relative position divided by two is a four-cycle lazy walk; first-step equations give 8 seconds and 16 total flips. 9: a ten-cycle walk moves with probability 2/3, so hitting the opposite vertex takes 5*(10-5)/(2/3)=37.5 seconds.
4. 10: Solve h(x,y)=1+mean(neighbor h), with boundary h=0; the center of the 0..10 grid gives about 29.239374 steps. 11: gambler’s ruin gives i/M=1/2 for the fair case and [1-(q/p)^i]/[1-(q/p)^M]≈0.982954 for i=10,M=20,p=0.6.
5. 12: Binomial(6,1/2) gives P(H>3)=11/32 and P(H<=3)=21/32. 13: sum-count frequencies 1,2,...,10,9,...,1 give matching-sum probability sum(count²)/10000=0.067. 14: six-digit zero-padded counting gives 1-(9/10)^6=0.468559; exchanging 0 for 1000000 leaves this count unchanged.

### Small example / sanity check

The numerical checks solve the bag recurrence and 81 interior-grid hitting-time equations, rather than guessing a “nice” closed form for the grid. A repeated title remains a separate question entry so neither 14-part practice set is lost.

### Common trap

Treating discarded unseen cards as biased, assuming the last die is uniform at a threshold crossing, or counting particle seconds as just one flip per second.
