## Question

What are bias and variance? Provide detailed descriptions and real-world examples.

## Worked solution

### Bias is systematic error; variance is sensitivity to the training sample

Fix an input x and imagine collecting many training datasets D from the same population. Let $\hat f_D(x)$ be the model trained on D and $f(x)=E[Y\mid X=x]$ the true conditional mean. Bias is $E_D[\hat f_D(x)]-f(x)$; variance is the spread of $\hat f_D(x)$ around its average.

Under squared error and independent test noise,

$$E_{D,Y}[(Y-\hat f_D(x))^2]=
\underbrace{(E_D\hat f_D(x)-f(x))^2}_{\mathrm{bias}^2}+
\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_D(\hat f_D(x))}_{\mathrm{variance}}+
\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid x)}_{\mathrm{irreducible\ noise}}.$$

These are properties of a learning procedure and data distribution, not synonyms for “training error” and “test error.” The familiar decomposition applies directly to squared error; classification error needs different treatment.

### Real examples

For house prices, a model using only floor area with one straight line may systematically miss location premiums and nonlinear size effects. It can have similar, poor training and validation error: a high-bias symptom. Add justified location/features or nonlinear terms, reduce excessive regularization, and see whether held-out error improves.

A deep unpruned tree trained on a small set of past loans may memorize unusual borrowers. Training error is tiny, validation error is much larger and different training resamples yield very different predictions: a high-variance symptom. More representative data, shallower trees, regularization or bagging can stabilize it.

### Diagnose instead of guessing

Use a leakage-safe split and plot training/validation error against capacity and training-set size. High errors that converge at a poor level suggest representation/optimization/label problems. A persistent gap that narrows as data grows suggests variance. Repeat splits or bootstrap fits to inspect prediction stability. A distribution shift can also produce a large gap; first check whether training and validation represent the same target population.

Reducing capacity or applying L2/dropout/early stopping often lowers variance while adding bias. Adding useful features or capacity often lowers bias but can increase variance. The best choice minimizes representative validation error subject to deployment constraints. More data cannot fix an excluded critical feature, and a larger model does not fix corrupted labels. Irreducible noise is the uncertainty remaining given the available x; collecting a genuinely informative new measurement can reduce that conditional noise.
