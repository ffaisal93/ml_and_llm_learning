## Question

You are given transactions(transaction_id INT, vendor_id INT, amount_charged INT, transaction_type VARCHAR, refund_transaction_id INT NULL, timestamp DATETIME) where transaction_type is PURCHASE or REFUND (a REFUND's refund_transaction_id points to the original purchase transaction_id) and vendors(vendor_id INT, city VARCHAR, state VARCHAR, country VARCHAR).

Write a SQL query to: compute each vendor's net total charged amount in the last 2 years (relative to CURRENT_DATE), where PURCHASE is positive and REFUND is negative; return the Top 3 vendors by net total amount (descending).

Suggested output columns: vendor_id, net_amount.

Assumptions/constraints
Refund rows are counted based on the refund timestamp and must be within the 2-year window to be included.
amount_charged is a positive integer.
transaction_id is unique.
Example

Current date assumed 2025-04-01. Output: vendor_id=3 net_amount=500; vendor_id=2 net_amount=300; vendor_id=1 net_amount=150 (101 and 103 cancel out, +102 = 150).

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

Aggregate at the intended vendor grain before ranking, with refunds and time boundaries defined.

1. Filter transactions using a reproducible as-of timestamp and a half-open date interval.
2. Normalize charge/refund signs or subtract refund amounts according to the schema, then group by vendor id.
3. Sort totals descending with an explicit tie rule; use ROW_NUMBER for exactly three rows or DENSE_RANK when tied ranks should be included.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Establish row grain before writing a join

Say what one output row represents: an event, user, vendor, parent type or time interval. Then inspect the cardinality of every join. Joining two one-to-many relations can multiply rows and inflate both totals and averages; `DISTINCT` is not a general repair because legitimate equal-valued records can also exist.

For mergeable averages store a sum and count:

$$
\bar x_{\rm pooled}=\frac{\sum_g \operatorname{sum}_g}{\sum_g \operatorname{count}_g}.
$$

Do not average subgroup means unless the intended weighting gives each group equal weight. Likewise, daily distinct users cannot be added to obtain monthly distinct users.

Use half-open time windows, such as `timestamp >= start AND timestamp < end`, and name the timezone. For point-in-time training, feature **availability time** must not exceed prediction time; event occurrence and availability can differ.

Choose tie semantics deliberately: `ROW_NUMBER` produces a fixed number of rows under a deterministic secondary order, while `DENSE_RANK` preserves equal primary scores. A predicate on a nullable right-table column in `WHERE` can undo the unmatched-row preservation of a `LEFT JOIN`. Test duplicate events, absent dimension rows, nulls, tied maxima and an empty denominator. These cases explain correctness more convincingly than running only one ordinary example.

### Work a small example

A vendor with charges 100 and refunds 30 has net charge 70, not gross charge 100.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Joining a one-to-many vendor table before aggregation and multiplying transaction totals.
