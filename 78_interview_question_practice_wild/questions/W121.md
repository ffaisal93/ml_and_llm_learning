## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Treat A and B as labeled samples, define deployment class priors and split held-out observations before fitting any density or boundary.
2. A transparent baseline estimates each class mean and variance and compares log prior minus log sigma minus (x-mu)²/(2*sigma²), under a Gaussian assumption. Use variance floors for tiny/constant samples.
3. If shapes are not plausibly Gaussian, compare a one-dimensional KNN, histogram/KDE or validated discriminative model; tune hyperparameters inside training folds.
4. Return posterior/score uncertainty as well as a class if overlap is substantial. No deterministic rule can recover the generating class with certainty when distributions overlap.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Connect the model, objective and evaluation split

A model class states what relationships can be represented; the objective states which errors training penalizes; the split estimates where those relationships generalize. These are separate choices. For binary logistic regression,

$$
z_i=x_i^\top w+b,\qquad p_i=\sigma(z_i),\qquad
L=\frac1N\sum_i\left[\log(1+e^{z_i})-y_i z_i\right]+\lambda R(w).
$$

Compute the softplus term stably from logits. The derivative of the unregularized per-example loss with respect to its score is $p_i-y_i$. A decision threshold changes actions but does not refit the probability model.

Inspect a simple baseline and training/validation curves before diagnosing high bias or variance. Low training error and poor validation performance may indicate overfitting, entity leakage, an inappropriate split or a genuine distribution change. The remedies differ: regularization does not repair a broken future-information boundary.

Fit scaling, imputation and encoders within training folds. For repeated users, grouped validation can be necessary; for forecasting, preserve the direction of time. Include class imbalance, calibration and subgroup errors rather than reporting only one accuracy number. A model can improve ranking while becoming worse as a probability estimate, and a statistically better estimate can still produce worse decisions at an unsuitable operating threshold.

### Work a small example

For the supplied separated arrays, x=1.05 is much closer to A’s range than B’s. Equal-distance nearest-mean is a simple fixed rule only under suitable scale/prior assumptions.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Promising perfect correctness for overlapping unknown distributions or estimating an unstable density from a handful of values without caveats.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Implement k-nearest-neighbor classification.** Neighbors with labels [A,A,B] predict A for k=3. With k=2 and labels [A,B], a deterministic tie rule is required.

Why can high-dimensional KNN weaken? Distances become less discriminative and search structures can lose their advantage.

**Explain logistic regression from scores to gradients.** A logit of 0 means p=0.5; a logit of log(3) means p=0.75 and odds 3:1.

When is the linear boundary too restrictive? Feature engineering, kernels or nonlinear models may help; compare on a leakage-free split.

**Check calibration and choose a decision threshold.** Under ideal calibrated probabilities and fixed costs, predict positive when p>C_FP/(C_FP+C_FN). With costs 1 and 9, the threshold is 0.1.

What happens under prevalence shift? Probability calibration and the best operating point may change.
