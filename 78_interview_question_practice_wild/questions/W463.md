## Question

Describe a time when you had to make a data-driven decision.

## Worked solution

### An illustrative decision with a counterfactual

No personal experience is provided. A strong **example** is deciding whether to ship a search reranker. “I owned evaluation, while product owned launch priority. The candidate improved overall click-through, but the actual goal was successful issue resolution within a latency budget. I wanted evidence before adding serving cost.”

“I checked logging/split quality, compared lexical and reranked results on the same time-held-out labeled queries, inspected language/new-user slices and measured tail latency under matched load. I separated a ranking gain from a position/exposure effect, then proposed a small randomized rollout with a predefined resolution metric and latency/complaint guardrails. We chose the simpler baseline for the slow slice and canaried the candidate elsewhere.” State the real sample size, baseline, confidence interval and practical margin in your actual story; none are supplied here.

“The resulting decision was narrower than my initial preference for a full launch. I documented the evidence, the cost of delay and the trigger for revisiting it. If no reliable outcome estimate existed, I said so and treated the pilot as a learning decision.” A data-driven answer should identify what would have changed your mind—for example, no resolution benefit at the planned sample size—not simply cite a dashboard that confirmed your intuition. Explain data limitations and stakeholder reasoning, your own action and the observed follow-up. Never fabricate a percentage to make an evidence story sound quantitative.
