## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Eight short probability and statistics problems, each with a single numeric or closed-form answer. They are meant to be solved quickly and cleanly by hand: recognise the standard distribution or counting argument, then compute.

Answer each of the following. Give exact values (fractions or closed forms) where possible; for item 2 also give a decimal rounded to three places.

1.
Constrained maximum. Let P = (px, py) be the point in the first quadrant (px &gt;= 0, py &gt;= 0) on the unit circle x^2 + y^2 = 1 that maximizes f(x, y) = x^2 * y. Find py.
2.
Continuous density. A random variable X has density f(x) = 0.5 * e^(-|x|) for all real x. Find P(2 &lt;= |X| &lt;= 4).
3.
Conditional cards. A 5-card hand is dealt from a standard 52-card deck and is known to contain at least 3 aces. Find the probability that it contains all 4 aces.
4.
Dice sum. A fair six-sided die is rolled 5 times. Find the probability that the sum of the five rolls is a multiple of 5.
5.
Normalising constant. A discrete random variable takes values 1, 2, ..., 12 with P(X = x) = k * (2x - 1). Find k.
6.
Coin runs. A fair coin is tossed 9 times. A run is a maximal block of consecutive tosses with the same outcome. Find the expected number of runs.
7.
Standard error. X1, ..., X100 are independent standard normal variables and Y is their average. Find the standard deviation of Y.
8.
Dice maximum. Three fair six-sided dice are rolled and the score is the largest face. Find P(score = 2).
Requirements
Show the key step for each item (the formula or counting argument), not only the final number.
State assumptions you use, such as independence of rolls and tosses.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. 1: substitute x²=1-y², maximizing y-y³ on [0,1]; derivative 1-3y² gives y=1/sqrt(3). 2: the two Laplace tails give exp(-2)-exp(-4)≈0.117020, rounded to 0.117.
2. 3: P(4 aces|at least 3)=C(48,1)/[C(4,3)C(48,2)+C(48,1)]=1/95. 4: convolve five die residues modulo 5; favorable count is (6^5+4)/5=1556, so probability 389/1944≈0.200103.
3. 5: sum_(x=1)^12(2x-1)=12²=144, so k=1/144. 6: runs=1+sum_(i=2)^9 I(change), expectation 1+8/2=5.
4. 7: independent standard normals averaged over 100 give variance 1/100 and standard deviation 0.1. 8: P(max=2)=P(all<=2)-P(all<=1)=(2/6)^3-(1/6)^3=7/216.

### Small example / sanity check

For item 3, conditioning changes the sample space: the unconditional probability of four aces is not the requested result. Each answer above includes its distinct counting/distribution argument.

### Common trap

Confusing standard deviation with variance or treating the five dice-sum residues as exactly uniform.
