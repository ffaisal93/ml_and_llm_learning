## Question

Eight short probability and statistics problems, each with a single numeric or closed-form answer. They are meant to be solved quickly and cleanly by hand: recognise the standard distribution or counting argument, then compute.

Answer each of the following. Give exact values (fractions or closed forms) where possible; for item 2 also give a decimal rounded to three places.
1. Constrained maximum. Let $P = (p_x, p_y)$ be the point in the first quadrant ($p_x \ge 0$, $p_y \ge 0$) on the unit circle $x^{2} + y^{2} = 1$ that maximizes $f(x,y) = x^{2}y$. Find $p_y$.
2. Continuous density. A random variable X has density f(x) = 0.5 * e^(-|x|) for all real x. Find P(2 &lt;= |X| &lt;= 4).
3. Conditional cards. A 5-card hand is dealt from a standard 52-card deck and is known to contain at least 3 aces. Find the probability that it contains all 4 aces.
4. Dice sum. A fair six-sided die is rolled 5 times. Find the probability that the sum of the five rolls is a multiple of 5.
5. Normalising constant. A discrete random variable takes values $1, 2, \dots, 12$ with $P(X=x) = k(2x-1)$. Find $k$.
6. Coin runs. A fair coin is tossed 9 times. A run is a maximal block of consecutive tosses with the same outcome. Find the expected number of runs.
7. Standard error. X1, ..., X100 are independent standard normal variables and Y is their average. Find the standard deviation of Y.
8. Dice maximum. Three fair six-sided dice are rolled and the score is the largest face. Find P(score = 2).

### Requirements

Show the key step for each item (the formula or counting argument), not only the final number.
State assumptions you use, such as independence of rolls and tosses.

## Worked solution

### Eight computed answers

1. **p_y=1/√3.** On the first-quadrant unit circle x²=1−y², so f=y−y³ for y∈[0,1]. Derivative 1−3y² vanishes at y=1/√3 and second derivative −6y is negative there. Endpoints give zero while the interior value is positive; x=√(2/3), so this is the global constrained maximum.
2. **e^-2−e^-4≈0.117020, rounded 0.117.** The symmetric Laplace density gives 2∫[2..4](1/2)e^-x dx. Continuous endpoint inclusion does not affect probability; forgetting the second tail would halve the answer.
3. **1/95.** Among hands with at least three aces, four-ace hands number C(4,4)C(48,1)=48 and exactly-three-ace hands number C(4,3)C(48,2)=4,512. Conditional probability is 48/(48+4,512)=1/95; this is not the unconditional four-ace probability.
4. **1556/7776=389/1944≈0.200103.** Each die's residues mod five have counts [1,2,1,1,1], with residue one doubled by faces 1 and 6. In the cyclic generating polynomial write one die as J+x, where J=1+x+…+x⁴ and x⁵=1. In (J+x)^5 every term containing J is uniform across residues (J²=5J). The lone x⁵ contributes an extra one to residue zero. Thus favorable count is (6⁵−1)/5+1=(6⁵+4)/5=1,556. Residues are almost, but not exactly, uniform.
5. **k=1/144.** Normalization requires 1=kΣ[x=1..12](2x−1)=k·12²; all probabilities are positive and sum to one.
6. **E[runs]=5.** There is always the first run; each of the eight adjacent pairs creates another run exactly when outcomes differ, probability 1/2. Linearity gives 1+8/2=5 without needing independence between all change indicators.
7. **sd(Y)=1/10=0.1.** Independent Xi have variance one, so Var((ΣXi)/100)=100/100²=1/100. Take the square root; normality additionally makes Y exactly N(0,0.01), but independence/variance suffice for this standard-error calculation.
8. **7/216.** The maximum of three independent dice equals two when all are ≤2 but not all ≤1: (2/6)³−(1/6)³=(8−1)/216. Counting only permutations of (2,1,1) would miss outcomes with several twos.

All dice/coins and normal observations use the question's independence assumptions; the card hand is uniform without replacement. The runnable checker enumerates all 6⁵ dice sequences for item 4, checks all 2⁹ coin sequences for item 6, verifies the 5-card combinatorial arithmetic and enumerates three-die maxima. These small exact checks validate the nonuniform-residue and run-count arguments, while the derivative/integral and variance calculations provide the analytic answers.
