## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Walk the interviewer through the most technically complex project you have led or made a major contribution to. This is a project deep dive: the interviewer wants to see how you think about trade-offs, scope and impact, and how you lead other engineers, not only what was built. Plan for about 10–15 minutes of story, then follow-up questions on the parts below.

What to answer
1.
Context and your role: what problem the project solved, why it mattered to the business or users, and what you personally owned (as opposed to the team).
2.
Key technical trade-offs: two or three decisions you made, the options you rejected, and why. In hindsight, what would you do differently?
3.
Scoping: how you decided what went into the first version and what was cut or deferred.
4.
Measuring success: the metrics you defined at the start, and the measured outcome (numbers, with the baseline you compare against).
5.
Driving the decision across the team: one significant architectural decision you pushed through, who disagreed, and how you built agreement.
What a strong answer shows
Concrete, measurable impact, not just involvement.
Ownership: clear about what &quot;I&quot; did versus what &quot;we&quot; did.
Principled decisions under ambiguity, with the reasoning stated.
Honesty about mistakes and what changed afterwards.
Enough technical depth that every claim survives one more &quot;why?&quot;.
Constraints
One project, told end to end; do not switch projects between parts.
Answer each part in 1–3 minutes when asked as a follow-up.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain your most technically complex ML project. A strong answer demonstrates decisions and evidence, not a list of technologies.
2. State the user problem, constraints and your own contribution separately from the team's work.
3. Describe the data, baseline, hardest technical failure, alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
4. Quantify the result using a valid comparison, explain limitations and say what you would change next. Use real experiences rather than invented metrics.
5. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
6. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
7. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
8. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.

### Small example / sanity check

“I reduced p95 latency from 800 to 500 ms on a matched workload while keeping task accuracy within the predefined margin” states both benefit and guardrail.

### Common trap

Saying “we built an LLM system” without explaining your choices or how success was measured.
