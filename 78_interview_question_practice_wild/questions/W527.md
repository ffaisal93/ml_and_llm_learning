## Question

Why do you want to change your job in less than a year?

## Worked solution

### Address the short tenure directly

The concern is whether the same mismatch will repeat. Give the real reason briefly, explain what you did to understand or improve the situation, and show why the new role is a deliberate fit. Your employment circumstances are unknown, so choose only a truthful explanation; do not invent restructuring, family needs or broken promises.

### Adaptable answer

“I joined expecting to work primarily on [expected responsibility]. After [actual period/context], the role's ongoing focus became [actual difference]. I discussed [reasonable adjustment] and learned [outcome]. That helped me clarify that I want to build depth in [specific work]. I am exploring this position because [verified responsibility] matches that direction. I recognize the short tenure, so I am being careful to understand ownership, expectations and team needs before making another move.”

If the reason is an actual layoff or contract ending, say that directly instead of using the mismatch story. If it is a personal/location constraint, state the relevant constraint at the level of detail you are comfortable sharing and explain its present status. If you made a poor initial choice, own that without a long defensive narrative.

### Make the fit credible

Name one thing you learned in the current job and one concrete criterion you are checking now. Ask how the new role spends its time, how success is measured and what changed in the team recently. Avoid blaming individuals, sharing confidential details or calling the work beneath you. Do not promise a guaranteed number of years; demonstrate the diligence that makes a longer fit plausible. Keep the main answer around a minute and be ready to discuss the actual timeline consistently with your resume.
