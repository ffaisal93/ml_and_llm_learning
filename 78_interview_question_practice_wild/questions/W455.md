## Question

A short async, pre-recorded video screen (Hireflix). The reported question set:
1. Introduce yourself — and why Ramp?
2. How did you learn a new technology, and how would you explain it to a new intern?
3. How are you using AI in your day-to-day, and has it changed how you approach writing code?
4. Share a recent project.
5. How do you stay up to date with new technologies?
Notes

Because it's pre-recorded there is no back-and-forth — prepare tight, self-contained answers (~1–2 min each). The AI-usage question is a genuine signal at Ramp: give a specific workflow (where AI helps, where you still drive, a time you caught an AI mistake) rather than a generic endorsement. The "explain to a new intern" framing tests communication/teaching.

## Worked solution

### Five self-contained video answers

No resume or personal examples are provided. The following are **illustrative answer structures**, not claims about the reader; substitute actual work and keep each response to roughly one or two minutes with a direct opening.

1. **Introduction/why Ramp:** “My background is in [actual role], especially [specific ownership]. I am looking for [scope]. This role interests me because the advertised work connects reliable financial workflows with user-facing automation.” Cite the actual role/problem that motivates you, not invented company growth or personal product use.
2. **Learning/teaching:** An example is learning transactional outboxes to fix lost notifications. Read a small authoritative example, build a crash/retry experiment, then explain to an intern: “Save the business change and the event-to-send in one transaction; a worker can retry sending, and the consumer still deduplicates.” Ask the intern to trace a crash between commit and delivery to check understanding.
3. **AI workflow:** “I use an assistant for test ideas and small refactors. In an illustrative retry bug, it proposed a memory-only dedup set; I rejected that because restarts lose it. I write a failing crash/retry test, inspect the patch and validate independently before accepting it. I retain ownership of production changes and keep secrets out of unapproved tools.” Use a real caught error in your actual recording.
4. **Recent project:** state user problem, team versus your scope, one rejected alternative, evidence of outcome and one limitation. For example, a versioned status API with stale-data signaling is more concrete than “we used AI/cloud.” Do not turn a target into a result.
5. **Staying current:** follow a small number of primary docs/release notes, choose a problem-driven experiment, compare it with the current approach and share a short reproducible note. Explain one technology you chose not to adopt and why; reading feeds alone is not applied learning.

Record a rehearsal, check audio/timing and make every answer understandable without follow-up. The prompt's reported interview format is not a verified promise of the current hiring process.
