## Worked solution


### 1. Self-attention time complexity

Separate the projections from pairwise mixing. For length $T$ and width $d$, ordinary projections cost $O(Td^2)$; dense query–key and probability–value products cost $O(T^2d)$ across heads. The logical score tensor contains $H T^2$ entries per example. “Quadratic” names the all-pairs sequence interaction, not every operation in a Transformer. Tiled attention can avoid storing all scores without making full attention's arithmetic linear.

### 2. LSTM time complexity

For input width $d$ and hidden width $h$, the four gate/state projections cost $O(T(dh+h^2))$, up to constant factors. Unlike training-time full-sequence self-attention, the hidden-state recurrence introduces a dependency from step $t-1$ to $t$, limiting parallelism across positions. The comparison depends on both sequence length and width; do not compare only the letter $T$.

### 3. Why scale by the square root?

Assuming independent zero-mean unit-variance coordinates, each $q_jk_j$ has variance one, so

$$\operatorname{Var}(q^\top k)=d_k,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(q^\top k/\sqrt{d_k})=1.$$

This controls the initial softmax scale and reduces saturation. It is distinct from subtracting a maximum to prevent exponential overflow, and the assumptions need not remain exact after training.

### 4. Why multiple heads?

Different projections permit distinct matching and value-transfer patterns in parallel. They all can inspect the permitted sequence; heads do not receive disjoint chunks of tokens by definition. Concatenation and the output projection mix their contributions. More heads change per-head width and parameter/compute tradeoffs, so the benefit is an architectural choice to validate, not an automatic accuracy increase.

### 5. Why positions?

Without an order signal, ordinary attention does not know whether a token precedes or follows another except through supplied structure such as a mask. Add position information or encode position-dependent interactions so reversed/rearranged sentences can be distinguished. Vocabulary embeddings alone identify token content, not its particular occurrence's position.

### 6. Sinusoidal-position limitations

Sinusoids provide deterministic features beyond a finite lookup table, but accepting a larger index does not prove useful long-context behavior. Training length, learned attention patterns and frequency resolution affect extrapolation. Test evidence at different positions and distances; one exact-match “needle” task does not establish general long-range reasoning.

### 7. What RoPE changes

Rotate coordinate pairs of $Q$ and $K$ by position-dependent angles. Its key relation is

$$\langle R_mq,R_nk\rangle=\langle q,R_{n-m}k\rangle.$$

The dot product therefore exposes a relative phase. Ordinary RoPE does not add an embedding to $V$. Cache already-positioned keys consistently and advance decode positions; restarting them at zero changes the computation.

### 8. Attention masks

Apply a boolean allowed relation or an additive $0/-\infty$ mask **before** softmax. A forbidden key receives zero probability after exponentiation. Combine causality, valid keys and any segment isolation with an explicit polarity convention. At least one key must remain valid per queried row, or a separate empty-row policy is needed.

### 9. Pre-LayerNorm versus post-LayerNorm

The arrangements are

$$x'=x+F(\operatorname{LN}(x)),\qquad
x'=\operatorname{LN}(x+F(x)).$$

Pre-norm preserves a direct identity route through the residual addition; post-norm places normalization on that combined route. This changes deep-stack gradient behavior, but neither arrangement makes initialization, precision or learning-rate tuning irrelevant. LayerNorm uses feature statistics per token, not BatchNorm-style running batch statistics.

### 10. Training-time parallelism

Given the full teacher-forced input and mask, all positions' projections and attention products can be computed together. A recurrent model must propagate its hidden state along the sequence. Autoregressive **generation** remains sequential across newly sampled tokens unless an additional decoding scheme changes the execution; parallel training does not mean a basic decoder emits an entire unknown answer at once.

### 11. What long-range problem is addressed?

Self-attention can directly connect distant permitted positions instead of compressing all past information through one recurrent hidden-state update per step. That shorter interaction path helps access but does not guarantee the model selects the right evidence. Memory, context truncation and learned positional behavior still constrain practical use.

### 12. Dot-product versus additive attention

Dot-product scores map efficiently onto matrix multiplication. Additive attention uses a learned compatibility function, often with a nonlinear hidden layer. They express somewhat different scoring mechanisms; hardware efficiency and scale control help explain dot-product popularity without asserting additive attention is mathematically invalid or universally worse.

### 13. Why softmax?

For each query, softmax creates nonnegative weights summing to one, so the head outputs a convex combination of values:

$$p_j=\frac{e^{s_j}}{\sum_k e^{s_k}},\qquad o=\sum_jp_jv_j.$$

Its derivative is $\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top$, which becomes small near a one-hot distribution. This links score scale, saturation and learning. Other normalization/mixing rules are possible, but change the operator.

### 14. Separate Q, K and V

Queries express what to look for; keys express matching features; values express transmitted content. Separate query/key projections allow asymmetric compatibility. If the same projection were used for both on the same unmasked inputs, raw pairwise dot-product scores would be symmetric. A separate value projection avoids forcing “how to find me” and “what to return” into the same representation.

### 15. Residual connections

A residual update preserves existing features while learning a correction. Its derivative includes an identity contribution, which can support gradient propagation through depth. Repeated residual additions can still grow activation scales; residual design does not eliminate the need for normalization, initialization and monitoring.

### 16. The feed-forward network

Attention mixes information between positions; a positionwise nonlinear FFN transforms features within each resulting token state. A typical FFN expands width, applies an activation/gate and projects back. Two affine layers without a nonlinearity collapse into one affine map; that is why the nonlinear transformation matters. Gated and ordinary FFNs have different parameter counts.

### 17. Autoregressive masking

The logit predicting the next token must not use that token's future occurrence in the training input. Teacher forcing provides a complete training sequence, so the causal mask enforces the intended information boundary. Shift targets exactly once and test by changing future tokens: earlier logits must stay the same.

### 18. Why deterministic sinusoids originally?

They offered a parameter-free way to encode positions and express relationships using structured frequencies, without learning a fixed-size position table. That design motivation should not be confused with a guarantee of perfect length extrapolation. Learned absolute embeddings and relative/rotary schemes make different tradeoffs.

### 19. Long-range access versus recurrent compression

A head can retrieve a distant value in one attention interaction, whereas a recurrent representation passes information through successive hidden-state updates. But “can attend” is not “will reason correctly.” Validate distraction resistance, multi-evidence tasks and middle-context retrieval at the actual deployment lengths.

### 20. The complete scaled-attention equation

$$\boxed{\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V)=
\operatorname{softmax}_{\rm keys}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\right)V.}$$

For $Q:(T_q,d_k)$, $K:(T_k,d_k)$ and $V:(T_k,d_v)$, scores have shape $(T_q,T_k)$ and output $(T_q,d_v)$. State mask meaning and the normalization axis alongside the equation. A diagonal-only allowed mask returns the corresponding values exactly, making a useful minimal correctness test.


### Apply this question’s exact contract

Remaining oral points: self-attention pairwise work is $O(T^2d)$, with projection/FFN work $O(Td^2)$ depending on widths; an LSTM is O(T*(d*h+h²)) and sequential over T. Multi-head attention gives distinct routing/content subspaces, not disjoint token ranges. Sinusoidal positions avoid a finite learned lookup table but do not guarantee useful extrapolation; RoPE rotates Q/K to expose relative phase. Dot-product attention maps efficiently to matrix multiplication; additive attention scores through a learned nonlinear network and is not universally inferior. Softmax gives nonnegative row-normalized mixing weights, with a saturation tradeoff. Scaled attention is softmax($QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$+M)V. The mask and residual/FFN explanations above cover the remaining repeated why-questions.

### Work a small example

In “The dog chased the ball because it moved,” a pronoun's query may match an object's key while its value carries object attributes. The three vectors serve different jobs even though they start from the same token state.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Calling Q, K and V three different input sequences: self-attention projects the same input; cross-attention uses a different source for K and V.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Why divide attention logits by the square root of the head dimension?** With d_k=64, the unscaled score has standard deviation 8 under these assumptions. Scaling reduces it to 1; dividing by 64 would make the scores unnecessarily small.

Could learned temperature or QK normalization change this calculation? Yes; state how the effective score scale is controlled.

**Trace every shape in multi-head self-attention.** For B=2, T=3, H=4, d_h=8, each score tensor has shape (2,4,3,3); concatenating produces (2,3,32).

What changes for cross-attention? The score matrix becomes (B,H,T_query,T_key), which can be rectangular.

**Where does the causal mask go, and why?** For three tokens the allowed rows are [1,0,0], [1,1,0], [1,1,1]. Changing token 3 must not change the output for token 1.

Why can loss masking alone not isolate packed examples? Loss masking changes which targets train the model, not which tokens it can attend to.

**Why do Transformers need positional information, and what does RoPE do?** For a coordinate pair (1,0), a 90-degree rotation gives (0,1), preserving length. Applying the same rotation to both q and k leaves their mutual dot product unchanged.

What is cached with RoPE? Typically already-rotated keys and ordinary values; the exact representation depends on the implementation.
