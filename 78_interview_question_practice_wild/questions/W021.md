## Worked solution

1. Why are Q, K and V separate projections? Attention is a learned lookup: a query says what to find, a key says what can be matched, and a value carries what to copy.
2. Write Q=XWq, K=XWk and V=XWv, with X shaped (B,T,d_model).
3. Compute scores QKᵀ, normalize each query's row over keys, then mix the value vectors.
4. Separate Q and K allow asymmetric relevance. Separate V lets the features used for matching differ from the features transmitted; the output projection mixes heads.
5. Why divide attention logits by the square root of the head dimension? It controls the initial scale of the dot products so softmax is less likely to become nearly one-hot before learning.
6. Assume independent, zero-mean, unit-variance query and key coordinates. Each product has variance one.
7. A sum of d_k such products has variance d_k and standard deviation sqrt(d_k).
8. Divide by sqrt(d_k), then subtract the row maximum before exponentiation. The divisor controls scale; max subtraction prevents overflow. These are different mechanisms.
9. Trace every shape in multi-head self-attention. Heads are parallel attention computations over smaller feature spaces; they do not each attend to a disjoint piece of the sequence.
10. Project X:(B,T,d) into Q,K,V:(B,T,H*d_h). Reshape and transpose to (B,H,T,d_h).
11. Multiply Q by K transposed on its last two axes to obtain (B,H,T,T). Softmax over the final key axis.
12. Multiply by V to obtain (B,H,T,d_h), transpose to (B,T,H,d_h), concatenate to (B,T,H*d_h), and apply Wo to return to (B,T,d).
13. Why do Transformers need positional information, and what does RoPE do? Without position information, ordinary self-attention cannot reliably distinguish token order. RoPE puts position-dependent rotations into queries and keys.
14. Split each head vector into coordinate pairs and rotate each pair by its position times a frequency.
15. Use rotated Q and K in the attention dot product. The relative rotation depends on the difference between their positions.
16. At cached decoding time, continue the absolute position offset from the prefix. Long-context extension still needs validation; relative structure alone does not guarantee extrapolation.
17. Explain LoRA and calculate its trainable parameter count. LoRA represents a weight update as the product of two thin matrices while leaving the original weight frozen.
18. For W shaped (d_out,d_in), use delta_W=(alpha/r)*B@A with A:(r,d_in) and B:(d_out,r).
19. Train A and B, usually initializing one factor to zero so the initial update is zero without making both gradients permanently zero.
20. The trainable count is r*(d_in+d_out), versus d_in*d_out for the full matrix. Include all targeted matrices when estimating the whole model.
21. Why use AdamW, warmup and gradient clipping? They address different training problems: adaptive update scale, early-step instability and unusually large gradients.
22. Adam maintains first and second moment estimates, with bias correction after zero initialization.
23. AdamW applies decoupled weight decay rather than treating decay exactly as another adaptively scaled loss gradient.
24. Warm up learning rate over initial steps and clip the global gradient norm when appropriate. Inspect logs and ablations; these are tuning tools, not correctness fixes.

### Small example / sanity check

In “The dog chased the ball because it moved,” a pronoun's query may match an object's key while its value carries object attributes. The three vectors serve different jobs even though they start from the same token state.

### Common trap

Calling Q, K and V three different input sequences: self-attention projects the same input; cross-attention uses a different source for K and V.
