## Question

Could you share which companies you are currently in contact with?

(Recruiter-screening question about your active interview pipeline and timeline. Despite the title, the content is a conversational screening prompt, not a coding task.)

## Worked solution

### Answer the recruiting question, not a coding problem

The recruiter wants to understand your timeline, competing processes and role preferences. You can share stages and deadlines without disclosing company names or confidential offer details. Your actual interview pipeline is not supplied, so there is no honest list of employers to fill in here.

### Three truthful response patterns

If comfortable sharing names: “I am speaking with [actual companies] about [role types]. I am at [accurate stages], and the nearest decision deadline is [real date]. My main criteria are [two genuine priorities], which is why I am interested in this team.”

If keeping names private: “I am in a few processes for [role family], mostly at [accurate stage]. I prefer to keep names private, but I can share my timeline: [actual deadline or no deadline]. Could you explain your remaining steps and expected timing?”

If there are no other processes: “This is my current active process. I am focusing on roles involving [genuine interest], and I would like to understand the team's timeline.” You do not need to manufacture competition to justify interest or reasonable scheduling.

### What to avoid

Do not call an introductory conversation an offer or invent an exploding deadline to accelerate the process. Separate a scheduled interview, a verbal indication and a written offer. If a real deadline changes, communicate the date and ask whether the process can accommodate it; do not imply that an extension is guaranteed.

A concise, consistent answer gives enough information for scheduling while preserving privacy. Return to your decision criteria and interest in the role rather than ranking employers for prestige. If the recruiter asks whether they may contact another employer, that is a separate consent question; sharing a name does not authorize contact.
