## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">An applied-ML coding round: build a model on tabular data that predicts two regression targets jointly, where one target is negative-valued (e.g. in [-1e6, -1]) and the other positive ([10, 1e3]). Because the targets have opposite signs and very different magnitudes, naive joint training is dominated by the larger-scale target. Requirements: flip the sign of the negative task during training, standardize/normalize each target separately so their losses are comparable, train a shared model with two heads minimizing MSE(A) + MSE(B), and de-normalize predictions back to the original space (task A must come out negative again).</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Fit a multi-target tabular regressor. Shared inputs do not guarantee that every target benefits from the same representation or error scale.
2. Define per-target labels, missing-label masks, horizons and leakage-free splits.
3. Train independent baselines, then compare a joint model with a weighted sum of target losses.
4. Normalize or weight target scales deliberately and report each target's metric plus the joint business objective.

### Small example / sanity check

A target measured in dollars can dominate an unweighted squared-error objective over a second target measured in fractions.

### Common trap

Reporting only average error while one target improves and the other becomes unusable.
