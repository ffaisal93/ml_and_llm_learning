## Question

An applied-ML coding round: build a model on tabular data that predicts two regression targets jointly, where one target is negative-valued (e.g. in [-1e6, -1]) and the other positive ([10, 1e3]). Because the targets have opposite signs and very different magnitudes, naive joint training is dominated by the larger-scale target. Requirements: flip the sign of the negative task during training, standardize/normalize each target separately so their losses are comparable, train a shared model with two heads minimizing MSE(A) + MSE(B), and de-normalize predictions back to the original space (task A must come out negative again).

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

Shared inputs do not guarantee that every target benefits from the same representation or error scale.

1. Define per-target labels, missing-label masks, horizons and leakage-free splits.
2. Train independent baselines, then compare a joint model with a weighted sum of target losses.
3. Normalize or weight target scales deliberately and report each target's metric plus the joint business objective.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Prefer projections and stable solves to memorized inverses

Least squares finds the projection of the response onto the feature space:

$$
\hat\beta=\arg\min_\beta\|X\beta-y\|_2^2,
\qquad X^\top X\hat\beta=X^\top y.
$$

The normal equations explain the mathematics, but explicitly forming $(X^\top X)^{-1}$ is usually a poor numerical implementation. Squaring the condition number can amplify instability. QR or SVD-based least-squares routines expose rank and near-dependence more reliably.

For two datasets with the same feature definitions, sufficient statistics add:

$$
S=X_1^\top X_1+X_2^\top X_2,\qquad
 t=X_1^\top y_1+X_2^\top y_2.
$$

Solving the pooled problem is not equivalent to averaging the two coefficient vectors. Rows carry different directional information, not only different sample counts.

State centering, intercept and regularization conventions. With ridge, the penalized directions satisfy $(X^\top X+\lambda I)\beta=X^\top y$, but an unpenalized intercept needs separate treatment. Distinguish coefficient identification from predictive usefulness: correlated columns can make individual coefficients unstable while their combined predictions remain fairly stable. Validate forecasts on independent data; impressive training fit, including interpolation of noise, is not evidence of a real predictive relation.

### Work a small example

A target measured in dollars can dominate an unweighted squared-error objective over a second target measured in fractions.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Reporting only average error while one target improves and the other becomes unusable.
