## Question

Evaluate a one-day Value at Risk model and propose a defensible validation plan. State every convention you use; monetary losses are positive numbers in this problem.

### Assumptions

The position has current notional USD 1,000,000.
One-day simple return is R, with a continuous distribution and known cumulative distribution function F.
Ignore funding, borrow fees, margin calls, market impact, and position rebalancing unless a follow-up explicitly adds them.
Use 99% confidence. Define VaR_0.99(L) as the 0.99 quantile of loss L.
Questions
1. For a long position, write the loss random variable and its 99% VaR in terms of a return quantile. Do the same for an equal-notional short position. State exactly when the two VaRs are equal and explain why neither direction is generally larger without distributional assumptions.
2. If the validated long VaR is USD 100,000, what can and cannot be inferred about the short VaR? Contrast a distribution symmetric about zero with a skewed distribution, giving a number for each.
3. Design a Monte Carlo convergence rule for estimating the 99% VaR. Specify an accuracy target, confidence level, repeated-run or bootstrap procedure, and a stopping rule; for a normal return model, estimate the path count that meets your target, and do not give an unsupported universal path count.
4. Design backtesting for daily VaR forecasts. Cover exceedance frequency, independence or clustering, traffic-light interpretation (zone boundaries and what each zone triggers, for 250 daily observations at 99%), rolling windows, and the limitations of backtesting a tail quantile.
5. Describe stress testing, sensitivity analysis, benchmark or challenger models, data-quality checks, change control, and ongoing monitoring. Separate model assumptions from operational risks such as liquidity and margin.
Follow-up: real short-position frictions

Explain how borrow costs, recalls, margin requirements, liquidity, and unbounded price upside can be represented in a broader loss model. Do not claim that these frictions change the mathematical VaR of the simplified return-only model unless they are explicitly included in L.

### Deliverable

Provide equations, a numerical interpretation where justified, a reproducible Monte Carlo validation protocol, and a concise governance plan.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

An estimate is useful only with its sampling assumptions, uncertainty and computational cost.

1. Generate independent draws under the stated model and average the quantity of interest.
2. Estimate standard error as sample standard deviation divided by sqrt(number_of_draws), when finite-variance conditions apply.
3. Use confidence intervals or suitable alternatives, validate against analytic special cases and assess model error separately from simulation error.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Name the random experiment and condition on the right event

Write the sample space and independence assumptions before counting. Bayes' rule reverses conditioning:

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0.
$$

A $90\%$ detection rate is not a $90\%$ probability that an alert is genuine. Prevalence and false-positive rate determine the latter. Work expected counts in a hypothetical population when the formula feels abstract.

For waiting-time and random-walk exercises, first-step analysis often gives a solvable recurrence:

$$
h(s)=1+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s)h(s'),\qquad h(s)=0\text{ at absorbing states}.
$$

Specify what counts as one step: two particles moving once each may cost two coin flips but only one elapsed second. At a forced horizon, all surviving probability mass must be assigned to the final stopping time.

For expected counts, indicator variables and linearity do not require independent indicators. For uncertainty or variance, dependence does matter. Check normalization, limiting cases and a small enumeration. Simulation is useful validation but not a replacement for a requested exact derivation; conversely, a neat-looking fraction should not be asserted merely because a numerical estimate is close to it.

### Apply this question’s exact contract

Define loss sign and VaR quantile convention, estimate the quantile with an explicit interpolation rule, and quantify sampling uncertainty for a quantile rather than applying the standard error of a mean. Backtest exception frequency and clustering on held-out periods. A precise simulation estimate does not validate the assumed return model or establish suitability for financial decisions.

### Work a small example

Reducing Monte Carlo standard error by a factor of two generally requires four times as many independent samples.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Reporting a tiny simulation standard error as proof that the underlying financial or scientific model is correct.
