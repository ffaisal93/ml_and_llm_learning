## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">What did you learn from your experience at Lam?</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain a project failure constructively. Show ownership, evidence and changed behavior, not a story in which someone else is always at fault.
2. State the goal, your responsibility and what failed in observable terms.
3. Explain which assumption or decision was wrong and how you detected it.
4. Describe recovery, what changed afterward and whether the fix actually helped. Keep confidential details appropriately abstract.
5. Handle harsh technical feedback. Separate the useful content of feedback from its delivery and verify what needs to change.
6. Listen, summarize the specific concern and ask for concrete examples when necessary.
7. Check the evidence, acknowledge valid mistakes and propose a bounded correction with a follow-up date.
8. If disagreement remains, explain it respectfully using the shared goal and evidence, and seek the appropriate resolution channel.

### Small example / sanity check

A failed evaluation due to duplicate leakage can become a concrete lesson about entity-aware splits and dataset audits.

### Common trap

Inventing a “failure” that is really only a disguised success or blaming a teammate without examining your own actions.
