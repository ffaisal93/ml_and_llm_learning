## Question

What did you learn from your experience at Lam?

## Worked solution

### The missing personal context matters

The prompt does not say what “Lam” refers to, your role there or what happened. No factual personal retrospective can be inferred. The following is a **conditional example for a technical internship/work placement**, not a claim that you worked at Lam or that Lam has these processes.

“If the placement involved engineering work, I would discuss one lesson about technical rigor and one about collaboration. For example, I learned to distinguish a reproducible measurement from a convincing explanation: I checked units, calibration and test conditions before concluding a change improved performance. I also learned to communicate an uncertain result early, with what I knew and the next experiment, rather than wait until I had a perfect answer.”

Ground that in your actual event: what task you were assigned, which action you personally took, feedback from a colleague and the observable outcome. A useful concrete pattern is an experiment whose apparent improvement disappeared after controlling temperature/input mix; the learning is the revised protocol, not a made-up percentage. Finish with transfer: “On the next project I wrote the measurement assumptions before collecting results and scheduled an early review.”

Replace this example with the actual context and lesson; if Lam is a person, course or another organization, use that meaning instead. Avoid attributing a product, equipment platform, culture or accomplishment to an undefined name. The defensible answer is specific about your real evidence and transparent about what cannot be supplied from this prompt.
