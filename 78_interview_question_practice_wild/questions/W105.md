## Question

Design a data and training system that converts a large, noisy collection of screen observations and user actions into useful supervision for a multimodal UI-navigation agent. The corpus mixes successful, failed, accidental, and incomplete trajectories, so the system must estimate quality without discarding informative failures or reinforcing its own model errors.

Inputs and constraints

```text
100 million trajectories, averaging 20 screen frames at 250 KB each plus action and UI-tree metadata; raw storage is therefore about 500 TB before replication and derived features.
10,000 human-verified seed trajectories spanning common tasks and failure modes.
```

A weekly labeling budget of 5,000 trajectories and a per-iteration compute budget of 2,000 GPU-hours (7.2 million GPU-seconds) shared by scoring, training, and rollouts.
One training-and-refresh iteration per week. Rules and metadata checks run on CPU over all 100 million trajectories. A small thumbnail encoder may score at most 10% of the corpus (10 million trajectories) per iteration, and a vision-language model (VLM) may score at most 200,000 trajectories per iteration.
Planning assumptions for the estimate: the small encoder processes 1,000 frames per GPU-second, and the VLM needs 5 GPU-seconds per trajectory.
Actions use a typed schema: tap(x, y), type(target, text), swipe(start, end), back, and wait. Every action links pre-action and post-action observations.

### Requirements

1. Define ingestion, privacy controls, deduplication, versioning, and a valid-trajectory schema. Distinguish malformed data, unsuccessful attempts, and coherent successful paths.
2. Combine deterministic validators, learned quality models, weak supervision, and selective human review. State how thresholds are calibrated and how label noise is audited.
3. Attach or generate instructions for useful trajectories, measure relevance and novelty, and mix curated, weakly labeled, failed, and synthetic examples during training.
4. Design a weekly loop that trains the navigation policy, evaluates it on frozen tasks, uses it to rescore or relabel candidates, and prevents confirmation bias.
5. Define Plan Coherence Score as a calibrated estimate that the ordered actions are consistent with the instruction and observed state changes. Explain how it is trained and evaluated rather than treating it as an unexplained model output.
6. Use metamorphic tests. Transform a trajectory in a way with a known expected effect, such as deleting a required action, perturbing a tap outside its target, or paraphrasing the instruction. Then verify that the validator's decision changes or stays stable as expected.
7. Cover replay or simulation, retries and alternative rollouts, active-learning selection, data lineage, failure analysis, and safe rollback.
Target metrics
At least 90% precision at 70% recall for rejecting invalid trajectories on a frozen, human-labeled audit set.
Improve task success on a held-out UI/task suite without regressing any safety-critical task family by more than 1 percentage point.
Report coverage as the fraction of a fixed held-out screen-and-action inventory observed in accepted data, not as a fraction of an unknowable set of all possible screens.
Keep every accepted example traceable to source version, filters, labels, model version, and reviewer decisions.
Deliverable

Present the architecture, storage and compute estimates, data schemas, scoring and sampling strategy, training loop, evaluation design, monitoring, privacy controls, and trade-offs. Include a GPU-hour budget table that splits the 2,000 GPU-hours among scoring, training, and rollouts, and show the labeling budget in use.

## Worked solution

### Store trajectories with separate validity and outcome labels

Use an immutable object store for redacted frames and a columnar manifest for trajectories. CPU ingestion validates `Trajectory{id,source_version,session_group,instruction?,privacy_version,frames[],steps[],terminal_outcome}`. Each frame has observation id, timestamp, dimensions, content hash and UI-tree reference. A step has a strictly increasing index, pre/post observation ids, action type, validated payload, time and execution result. Tap coordinates must lie within declared bounds; `type` needs a target and protected text reference; swipe needs two valid points. Hash and sequence checks catch truncated uploads and broken links. A single observation may be both a previous post-state and next pre-state; do not require twice as many unique frames as steps.

Malformed data (missing post-state, impossible coordinates, undecodable frame) is different from a coherent failed attempt or a complete successful path. Store `structurally_valid`, `plan_coherent`, `task_success`, `safety` and confidence separately. A valid failure may teach recovery; an accidentally successful random walk is a poor imitation target. Keep minimal failure segments plus corrective continuations and negative action preferences rather than imitating every failed action.

Obtain permitted source scope, redact secrets/PII from pixels, OCR, trees and typed strings before broadly accessible storage, and reject unrecoverable sensitive examples. Use restricted raw access, encryption, retention limits and deletion indexes keyed by source/user. Never place raw typed passwords in training targets. Group exact hashes and near-duplicate UI/task sessions before splitting; preserve rare app/task variants. Version dedup groups, filters and reviewer decisions so erasure and rollback propagate through derived data.

### A feasible weekly budget

Raw frames: $10^8\times20\times250\,000=5\times10^{14}$ bytes = 500 decimal TB. Three complete replicas would be 1.5 PB before metadata/features; prefer explicitly chosen durability/erasure coding. At most ten million thumbnail-scored trajectories contain 200 million frames: $200\,000\,000/1000=200\,000$ GPU-seconds = 55.56 GPU-hours. VLM scoring 200,000 trajectories costs $200\,000\times5=1\,000\,000$ GPU-seconds = 277.78 hours. Both fit their corpus caps.

| Weekly work | GPU-hours |
|---|---:|
| Thumbnail encoder, maximum 10M trajectories | 55.56 |
| VLM, maximum 200k trajectories | 277.78 |
| Policy training and quality-model calibration | 1,166.66 |
| Replay/alternative policy rollouts | 400.00 |
| Reserve for measured overhead/retries | 100.00 |
| Total | 2,000.00 |

Training and rollout allocations are budget ceilings, not claims of achievable epoch counts; benchmark sequence length, frame resolution and policy throughput first. CPU rules inspect all 100M manifests weekly. Thumbnail scoring reads up to 50 TB of original-sized frames unless cached thumbnails reduce I/O; VLM candidates represent roughly 1 TB raw. Avoid rereading all 500 TB over a constrained GPU link. Cache versioned embeddings and score only candidates chosen with source/task quotas plus an unbiased sample. Expensive scoring of the entire corpus would exceed the stated VLM cap by 500×.

Use 5,000 human reviews as: 1,500 random stratified audits, 2,000 uncertainty/disagreement cases, 1,000 rare/safety/failure-recovery trajectories, 500 double reviews/adjudications. Here each budget unit is a trajectory-review assignment; the last 500 re-review earlier items, yielding at most 4,500 distinct trajectories. Keep a frozen portion of the original 10,000 verified seeds strictly for audit, never refresh it into training.

### Learn and calibrate quality without circular approval

Rules provide high-confidence weak labels for structural violations; verified humans label coherence, outcomes and usefulness under a written rubric. Train the small quality model on cross-fitted labeled/weak examples with source-dependent noise weights, not by treating heuristic labels as ground truth. VLM rationales identify suspect steps, but its score is an input to review, not truth. Estimate weak-label error on the random audit, by language/app/task, and measure reviewer agreement. Use held-out calibration (for example isotonic regression) to map logits to probabilities. Set rejection threshold on validation to achieve invalid-rejection precision ≥90% at recall ≥70%; freeze it and report confusion counts and intervals on the independent audit. If the operating point does not exist, do not claim the target is met; route uncertainty to review.

**Plan Coherence Score** is calibrated $P(\text{ordered actions consistent with instruction and observed transitions}\mid\text{trajectory})$, not task success. Positives include coherent failures caused by environment errors; negatives include reordered actions, unexplained target jumps and instruction mismatches. Use independent human labels, held-out app/task families, reliability curves/Brier score and precision-recall. Compare to order-shuffled and final-screen-only baselines to prove temporal information matters.

Generate candidate instructions from complete trajectories with task templates or a model, verify against intermediate states and terminal evidence, and label ambiguity; do not hallucinate intent from a screenshot. Relevance is agreement with an instruction/task rubric. Novelty is distance from curated task/state/action clusters with quotas for rare inventory items, not merely a high embedding norm. For a chosen initial training mixture use 40% curated successes, 30% high-confidence weak examples, 20% coherent failures/recoveries and 10% synthetic or alternative rollouts; tune by ablation and cap duplicated families. Failure loss must encode rejection/recovery, not next-action imitation of an unsafe act.

### Weekly loop, tests and rollback

Freeze source/label manifests → run CPU filters → sample and score thumbnails → VLM and human selection → calibrate → train candidate → evaluate on frozen independent tasks → bounded replay/rollouts → stage or reject. Use prior-policy rescoring only as a proposal; retain blind random audits, disagreement cases and labels from humans/simulators independent of the policy. Do not relabel a failure successful because the new policy approves its own actions.

Metamorphic tests have known expected effects: deleting a required “confirm” action must lower coherence or mark the trajectory incomplete; shifting a tap outside the known target must fail target consistency; a meaning-preserving instruction paraphrase should preserve the verdict within a set tolerance. Validate transformations because deleting an optional action need not invalidate anything. Test both positives and negatives, and inspect failures rather than optimizing solely for these synthetic edits.

Replay only in resettable isolated app states; external websites can change and irreversible actions require simulation or explicit safe fixtures. Retry environment failures with stable task ids, bounded attempts and recorded seeds; compare first-attempt success and success-at-budget. Test alternate recovery rollouts without overwriting original evidence. Report task success by family, safety violations, cost and coverage $|\text{accepted inventory items}\cap H|/|H|$ for fixed held-out inventory $H$. Promote only on improved task success and no safety-critical family worse by >1 percentage point, using uncertainty appropriate to sample sizes. Monitor freshness, redaction leakage, rejection rates, score calibration, source mix and audit disagreement. Accepted-example lineage records source/version, hashes, filters, weak/human labels, model versions and reviewer decisions. An immutable dataset manifest plus model registry allows rollback to the previous policy and training snapshot without silently mixing versions.
