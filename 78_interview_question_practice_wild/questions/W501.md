## Question

Estimating Merger Synergy Premium in Real-World Deals

What is merger synergy premium and how do we estimate it in the real world?

Example

### Input

```text
StandaloneValueA = 50B, StandaloneValueB = 70B, ExpectedCombinedValue = 130B
```

## Worked solution

### Separate value created from price paid

Net synergy is the combined firm's value minus the values the firms would have had independently on the same valuation date and basis:

$$S=V_{AB}-(V_A+V_B).$$

With the given numbers, $S=130-(50+70)=10$ billion, or 8.33% of the 120-billion standalone total. This is estimated net value creation, not automatically the premium paid to the target's shareholders.

If A acquires B for an equity price $P_B$, the acquisition premium is $P_B-V_B$, often expressed as $(P_B-V_B)/V_B$. Under consistent assumptions, A's shareholders retain approximately $S-(P_B-V_B)$ of the synergy. For example, paying 76 billion for a target worth 70 transfers six billion to target holders and leaves four billion for A before other omitted costs. These figures assume all values refer to compatible equity claims; do not mix an enterprise-value combined estimate with standalone equity values.

### Estimate it in a real transaction

Construct a no-deal forecast for each firm first. Then model incremental after-tax cash flows from cost removal, cross-selling, purchasing scale or financing benefits, subtracting integration expense, customer loss, execution disruption and any dis-synergies. Discount incremental cash flows at rates consistent with their risks and timing. Avoid counting the same saving in both a margin improvement and a separate synergy line, or including growth the firm could achieve without the deal.

Use operating diligence to specify which facilities, contracts or duplicated functions produce savings and when. Estimate revenue synergies more conservatively when they depend on unproven customer behavior. Compare scenarios with different completion probabilities, delays and integration success. Public announcement abnormal returns and comparable transactions provide market cross-checks; they also reflect changed standalone expectations, deal probability and information unrelated to synergy.

A useful interview conclusion is a range of net synergy value, its main assumptions and how the negotiated premium divides that value. There is no observed acquisition price in this question, so the actual target premium cannot be calculated from the three supplied valuations alone.
