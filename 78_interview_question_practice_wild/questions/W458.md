## Question

Please share your proudest intern experience. Describe the background, your contributions, and the outcomes in detail.

## Worked solution

### An illustrative intern story with bounded ownership

No internship history is supplied. The following is an **example**, not a statement about your experience: “My proudest internship project was making a document-import workflow easier to diagnose. Support could see only ‘failed,’ so engineers repeatedly inspected logs. My mentor defined the service boundary; I owned typed error states, a retry-safe worker change and a support-facing status endpoint.”

Explain the hard part: “A timeout could mean the provider accepted the import but the reply was lost. I initially treated every timeout as failure. In a design review I learned to distinguish pending/unknown from definitely rejected, and I added a stable request key and reconciliation check before retrying.” This gives an implementation decision and a learning moment rather than pretending the intern independently designed the whole platform.

Then cover collaboration and outcome: “I agreed the status vocabulary with support, wrote fixtures for duplicate callbacks and crash/retry cases, rolled out behind a flag and documented the runbook. The evidence I can defend is that the failure drills produced one logical import and support could identify the next action from the status page. I do not have an isolated revenue or time-savings number.” Use your actual measured results if available.

End with what made it proud: a small owned change helped both users and teammates, and feedback changed your understanding of distributed failure. A clear before/after example—an ambiguous timeout now says ‘checking provider result’ rather than prompting another import—is more memorable than a list of tools. If your real proudest experience was research, testing or team support, preserve the same background → own contribution → evidence → learning structure without borrowing this project.
