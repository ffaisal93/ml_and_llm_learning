## Worked solution

### Regularization in a short oral answer

Dropout independently masks activations during training; inverted dropout uses $mh/(1-p)$ for $m\sim Bernoulli(1-p)$ and disables masks at inference, preserving activation expectation. At p=.5 a kept value4 becomes8. Conventional unscaled training dropout instead uses `(1-p)` scaling at inference; do not mix conventions. It discourages coadaptation but can hurt fitting when data/capacity are limited.

L1 adds $\lambda\|w\|_1$ and may produce exact zeros through its subgradient interval at zero. L2 adds $\lambda\|w\|_2^2/2$, yielding gradient lambda w and smooth shrinkage. Standardize using training statistics and tune lambda on validation; AdamW's decoupled decay is not identical to inserting an L2 term into Adam's adaptive gradient moments.

### Vocabulary is a systems and representation choice

A vocabulary of V with embedding width D costs VD parameters for one embedding table; an untied output head adds another VD, while tied weights share storage. Increasing V can shorten token sequences and therefore attention/cache/serial decoding work, but enlarges embedding/output logits and creates rarer poorly estimated tokens. Small V offers reusable subwords/bytes and robust coverage but longer sequences, especially for underrepresented languages. For a text becoming T/2 tokens, dense attention pair interactions drop about4×, but vocabulary-softmax cost can rise; measure whole-pipeline latency. Tokenizer changes generally require embedding/head adaptation and matching training/evaluation tokenization, not just swapping a preprocessing setting.

### SFT starts with aligned data

Use a fixed prompt/chat template, explicit role/turn separators, response boundaries, valid tokenizer ids, EOS and document/source-family splits. Deduplicate/quality-filter and remove evaluation contamination. For a sequence, train inputs x[:-1] against x[1:] once; response/EOS targets receive loss, prompt/padding targets can be masked, and masked mean divides by supervised targets. Packed examples need segment-aware attention if independent conditioning is intended. SFT minimizes $-\sum m_t\log\pi_\theta(y_t|prefix)/\sum m_t$. A wrong shift, a missing EOS or future-information mask can look like excellent loss while teaching the wrong task.

### Match post-training to available feedback

DPO uses offline winner/loser pairs and a frozen reference: $-\log\sigma(\beta[\log(\pi_\theta(w)/\pi_{ref}(w))-\log(\pi_\theta(l)/\pi_{ref}(l))])$. It needs neither explicit critic nor reward model for that objective, but depends on comparison quality/coverage.

PPO samples actor rollouts, obtains scalar rewards and critic-based advantages, and clips a policy/old-policy ratio in its surrogate; RLHF usually adds reference KL and a learned reward model. It supports exploration/multi-step interaction but costs rollout/critic memory and careful control.

GRPO samples several responses/prompt, standardizes rewards relative to siblings and uses clipped policy updates without a learned critic. `[0,0,1,1]` gives approximately `[-1,-1,1,1]` advantages; all-equal groups are uninformative. It suits checkable math/code when rewards are reliable but still pays generation cost and can exploit a weak checker. Choose DPO for fixed pairs/limited compute, GRPO for reliable grouped outcomes, PPO when exploration and value modeling help. None guarantees factuality; report independent task/safety/length/calibration regressions before and after each stage.
