## Question

A person commutes between home and work for n days and owns a single umbrella. Rain on each trip is random, and the umbrella moves only when the person carries it. Find the probability that the person never gets wet.

### Setup

1. Each day has two trips: a morning trip from home to work, then an evening trip from work to home.
2. It rains on each trip with probability p, independently of every other trip.
3. The umbrella is at home before the first morning trip.

### Rules

1. If it rains and the umbrella is where the person is, they carry it on the trip and stay dry. The umbrella ends up at the destination.
2. If it rains and the umbrella is at the other place, the person gets wet on that trip.
3. If it does not rain, the person stays dry and leaves the umbrella where it is, even if they will need it later.

```text
4. "Staying dry" means the person is dry on every one of the 2n trips.
```

What to deliver
1. A recurrence, or a matrix form, for P(n, p): the probability of staying dry on all 2n trips.
2. A closed-form expression for P(n, p) in terms of n and p.
3. The value of P(5, 0.3) to four decimal places.
4. The limit of P(n, p) as n grows, for 0 &lt; p &lt; 1, with a one-line reason.

### Examples

```text
n = 1, any p: P = 1 - p(1 - p). The only wet pattern is a dry morning (the umbrella stays home) followed by a rainy evening. At p = 0.3 this gives 0.79.
n = 2, p = 0.5: P = 0.5. All 16 rain patterns over the four trips are equally likely, and exactly 8 of them keep the person dry.
n = 0: P = 1, because there are no trips.
```

### Constraints

p is a real number with 0 &lt;= p &lt;= 1.
n is an integer with n &gt;= 0.
At p = 0 and at p = 1 the answer is 1 for every n: either it never rains, or the umbrella travels on every trip.

## Worked solution

### Track dry paths and umbrella availability

Let $a_t$ be the probability that after t trips the person is still dry and the umbrella is with them, and $b_t$ the probability they are dry but it is at the other location. Initially $(a_0,b_0)=(1,0)$. On a rainy trip only state a survives and keeps the umbrella; on a dry trip availability flips. With q=1-p,

$$\begin{pmatrix}a_{t+1}\\b_{t+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\q&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_t\\b_t\end{pmatrix},\qquad P(n,p)=a_{2n}+b_{2n}.$$

The survival sequence $s_t=a_t+b_t$ has $s_0=s_1=1$ and $s_t=ps_{t-1}+q^2s_{t-2}$. Put $r_\pm=(p\pm\sqrt{p^2+4q^2})/2$. Solving the recurrence gives

$$P(n,p)=\frac{(1-r_-)r_+^{2n}+(r_+-1)r_-^{2n}}{r_+-r_-}.$$

For 0<p<1 both roots have magnitude below one, so the probability tends to zero: eventually an unprotected rainy trip occurs with probability one. At p=0 or p=1 the answer is one, also consistent with the recurrence. Do not use a stationary probability of umbrella possession as the probability of surviving every trip; wet paths must be removed.

```python
import math

def staying_dry(n, p):
    if not isinstance(n,int) or isinstance(n,bool) or n<0 or not 0<=p<=1:
        raise ValueError('n nonnegative integer, p in [0,1] required')
    a,b=1.,0.
    for _ in range(2*n):
        a,b=p*a+(1-p)*b,(1-p)*a
    return a+b
```

The recurrence gives P(1,.3)=.79, P(2,.5)=.5 and P(5,.3)=.2588 (four decimals). Tests enumerate all rain patterns for small n and compare the recurrence/closed form, including n=0 and both endpoints. Matrix exponentiation reduces large-n evaluation to O(log n); the simple loop is O(n) time and O(1) memory.
