## Question

A person commutes between home and work for n days and owns a single umbrella. Rain on each trip is random, and the umbrella moves only when the person carries it. Find the probability that the person never gets wet.

### Setup

1. Each day has two trips: a morning trip from home to work, then an evening trip from work to home.
2. It rains on each trip with probability p, independently of every other trip.
3. The umbrella is at home before the first morning trip.

### Rules

1. If it rains and the umbrella is where the person is, they carry it on the trip and stay dry. The umbrella ends up at the destination.
2. If it rains and the umbrella is at the other place, the person gets wet on that trip.
3. If it does not rain, the person stays dry and leaves the umbrella where it is, even if they will need it later.

```text
4. "Staying dry" means the person is dry on every one of the 2n trips.
```

What to deliver
1. A recurrence, or a matrix form, for P(n, p): the probability of staying dry on all 2n trips.
2. A closed-form expression for P(n, p) in terms of n and p.
3. The value of P(5, 0.3) to four decimal places.
4. The limit of P(n, p) as n grows, for 0 &lt; p &lt; 1, with a one-line reason.

### Examples

```text
n = 1, any p: P = 1 - p(1 - p). The only wet pattern is a dry morning (the umbrella stays home) followed by a rainy evening. At p = 0.3 this gives 0.79.
n = 2, p = 0.5: P = 0.5. All 16 rain patterns over the four trips are equally likely, and exactly 8 of them keep the person dry.
n = 0: P = 1, because there are no trips.
```

### Constraints

p is a real number with 0 &lt;= p &lt;= 1.
n is an integer with n &gt;= 0.
At p = 0 and at p = 1 the answer is 1 for every n: either it never rains, or the umbrella travels on every trip.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

Track whether the umbrella is with the commuter before a trip; carrying it depends on rain.

1. Assume independent rain probability p for each trip and no carrying in dry weather. Define state W=with umbrella and A=away.
2. From W, rain leads to W at the next departure and dry weather to A. From A, either weather leads to W next because the destination has the umbrella.
3. For 0<p<1, stationary P(A)=(1-p)/(2-p), so wet-trip probability is p*(1-p)/(2-p).

### Understand why the approach works

#### Name the random experiment and condition on the right event

Write the sample space and independence assumptions before counting. Bayes' rule reverses conditioning:

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0.
$$

A $90\%$ detection rate is not a $90\%$ probability that an alert is genuine. Prevalence and false-positive rate determine the latter. Work expected counts in a hypothetical population when the formula feels abstract.

For waiting-time and random-walk exercises, first-step analysis often gives a solvable recurrence:

$$
h(s)=1+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s)h(s'),\qquad h(s)=0\text{ at absorbing states}.
$$

Specify what counts as one step: two particles moving once each may cost two coin flips but only one elapsed second. At a forced horizon, all surviving probability mass must be assigned to the final stopping time.

For expected counts, indicator variables and linearity do not require independent indicators. For uncertainty or variance, dependence does matter. Check normalization, limiting cases and a small enumeration. Simulation is useful validation but not a replacement for a requested exact derivation; conversely, a neat-looking fraction should not be asserted merely because a numerical estimate is close to it.

#### Put the result in mathematical notation

Let $A$ mean the umbrella is away before departure. Stationarity gives
$$\pi_A=(1-\pi_A)(1-p),\quad \pi_A=\frac{1-p}{2-p},\quad
P(\text{wet trip})=\frac{p(1-p)}{2-p}.$$
This is the stated independent-rain, carry-only-when-raining model. Changing the carrying policy changes the transition matrix and hence the answer.

### Work a small example

At p=0.5, P(A)=1/3 and the wet-trip probability is 1/6.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Assuming rain and umbrella availability are independent without modeling how past trips move the umbrella.
