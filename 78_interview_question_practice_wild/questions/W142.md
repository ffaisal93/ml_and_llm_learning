## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Plan a four-hour ML take-home exercise.

Budget the work so a reproducible baseline and honest evaluation exist before optional improvements.

1. Spend an initial block clarifying the target, split, metric, data quality and a minimal runnable pipeline.
2. Build and evaluate a baseline, then run one or two prioritized improvements with tracked seeds and configurations.
3. Reserve time for error analysis, clean reruns and a short write-up of assumptions, results and remaining limitations.

#### Fit a baseline on messy tabular data without leakage.

Build a trustworthy split and simple baseline before sophisticated feature engineering.

1. Define the target, prediction time and unit of independence, then split by time or group if required.
2. Fit imputation, scaling and category encodings on training folds only. Start with a dummy baseline and a regularized linear or small tree model.
3. Evaluate slices, missingness patterns and calibration; document features unavailable at decision time and remove them.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Establish what the number actually estimates

Write the population, unit of analysis, outcome window and denominator next to the metric. A per-user mean and a per-impression mean answer different questions. A global mean over unequal batches is

$$
\bar\ell=\frac{\sum_b\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i}{\sum_b n_b},
$$

not generally the unweighted mean of batch means. Workers should reduce numerators and denominators separately when reporting that metric.

Use a comparison with matched examples, mature labels and unchanged definitions. For uncertainty, resample independent units; repeated events within a user or prompt are not automatically independent. A confidence interval measures sampling uncertainty under assumptions, not protection against leaked data or an inappropriate target.

Calibration asks whether predictions around probability $p$ are correct about fraction $p$ of the time. It is not the same as accuracy. A reliability plot should show bin populations, and a calibration-error formula must say whether bins or examples receive equal weight.

When quality regresses, trace data availability, feature freshness, label delays and cohort mix before automatically retraining. For example, a fraud model may look worse on immature labels even when its true behavior has not changed. Report missing/time-out outputs explicitly: dropping them from the denominator can turn an unreliable system into an apparently perfect one.

### Work a small example

A simple model with a valid temporal split and reproducible commands is stronger evidence than an elaborate model evaluated on leaked data.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Using the final hour for one unvalidated architecture change and leaving no runnable result.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Fit a baseline on messy tabular data without leakage.** A “refund issued” field may predict churn extremely well but be recorded after the churn decision, making it unusable for a prospective model.

How would you handle unseen categories? An explicit unknown bucket or encoder behavior that is tested before deployment.
