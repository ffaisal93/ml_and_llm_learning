## Question

You have read the DeepSeek-V3 technical report. Prepare a defence of four of its design choices: for each one, give the numbers that justify it and name what it costs. A summary of the abstract is not an answer.

Use these figures from the report.

Model size: 671 B total parameters, 37 B activated per token, 61 layers.
Attention: 128 heads of dimension 128. MLA uses a joint KV compression rank of 512 plus a decoupled RoPE key of width 64.
Experts: 256 routed experts plus 1 shared expert, with top-8 routing.
Parallelism: expert parallelism over 64 GPUs on 8 nodes, and 16-way pipeline parallelism.
Serving: a 131,072-token context and a bf16 cache.

Use decimal units (1 KB = 1,000 bytes).

Questions to answer
1. Multi-head latent attention (MLA). Compute the KV cache per token and per 131,072-token sequence under MLA and under standard multi-head attention with the same shape. State the reduction factor, and explain at least two costs of MLA.
2. Mixture of experts and node-limited routing. Compute the fraction of parameters activated per token. For one layer, compute two fractions of experts that fire: routed only (8 of 256), and counting the shared expert (9 of 257). Explain why routing is capped at 4 of the 8 nodes. Say what the cap buys and what it costs the router.
3. DualPipe. Take a one-directional pipeline schedule with p = 16 stages. Compute the idle fraction (p - 1) / (m + p - 1) for m = 16, 64 and 256 micro-batches. Explain how DualPipe reduces the bubble. Say what it costs.
4. Critic-free policy optimisation (GRPO). Explain what replaces the critic, one advantage of that choice and one disadvantage. Then quantify the disadvantage. Assume a group of 8 samples with binary rewards. Assume the samples are independent and each passes with the same probability. How often does a prompt yield a group with no learning signal when that probability is 90%, and when it is 99%?
Expected answer

A written answer of roughly 200 to 300 words per question. Every claim has either a computed number or a named cost. State your assumptions where the report does not specify a value.

### Constraints

Quote only figures given above or computed from them.
Mark anything you recall from the report beyond that as recalled.
Label any number you assume as an assumption.
Treat the bubble formula in question 3 as a simplified model, not the exact expression in the report. The DualPipe figure derived from it is an illustration only.

## Worked solution

### 1. MLA: bytes saved and work introduced

Assume the given compressed latent and decoupled RoPE key are cached once per token per layer, both in bf16 at two bytes/value, without metadata, alignment padding, replicas or allocator overhead. MLA stores 512+64=576 values, or **1,152 bytes = 1.152 KB per token per layer**. Standard MHA caches both K and V for each of 128 heads of width 128: 2×128×128=32,768 values, or **65,536 bytes = 65.536 KB**. The ratio is 65,536/1,152=**56.8889×**.

At 131,072 tokens, the per-layer sequence cache is **150,994,944 bytes = 150.994944 MB** for MLA, versus **8,589,934,592 bytes = 8.589934592 GB** for MHA. Across the supplied 61 layers, those become **9,210,691,584 bytes = 9.210691584 GB** and **523,986,010,112 bytes = 523.986010112 GB** respectively. This is logical cache for one sequence; tensor/expert/pipeline placement determines per-GPU residency. Neither total GPU memory nor achievable batch size follows from cache bytes alone, because weights, activations and workspaces also occupy memory.

The gain buys longer-context or larger-batch capacity under the same cache budget, but has named costs: compressed keys/values require learned projection/decompression work or algebraically transformed kernels, and those kernels are more complex than standard MHA. Compression constrains the representable KV information; whether quality is preserved needs an ablation, not arithmetic alone. Decoupled RoPE contributes the extra 64 cached values and requires careful positional handling. Bandwidth savings can shift the bottleneck to compute/launch overhead. No latency speedup number is implied by the 56.9× storage reduction.

### 2. MoE and node-limited routing

The active parameter fraction is **37/671 = 0.0551416**, or **5.51416%** per token, using the supplied total/active definition. The reciprocal is about **18.1351**: total parameter count is that many times the active count, not a guaranteed speedup over a dense model. Embeddings, attention, routing, communication and hardware utilization still cost time, and all expert weights must be stored or transferred somewhere.

Within one layer, routed expert activation is **8/256 = 3.125%**. Counting the always-used shared expert gives **9/257 = 3.50195%**. These are fractions of expert *instances*, not parameter fractions for the whole model; they differ from 37/671 because shared/non-expert parameters and parameter sizes enter differently. Do not substitute one fraction for another.

With expert parallelism on 64 GPUs over eight nodes, the average placement is **eight GPUs/node**, assuming an even layout. Capping a token's routing at four nodes means it touches at most **half of the eight nodes** for routed expert work, reducing cross-node dispatch fanout and making communication scheduling more bounded. It does not by itself quantify bytes or guarantee exactly half the communication: token count, placement, data width and destination overlap are unspecified. Top-8 experts can still be spread across the chosen nodes.

The cost is a constrained router: the globally best unconstrained eight experts might span more than four nodes. It must choose a permitted subset, potentially trading expert specialization/quality for communication locality and creating load imbalance or capacity pressure. Expert placement, load balancing and shared-expert execution remain necessary. The 16-way pipeline is another parallelism axis; the figures do not establish total distinct GPUs without specifying how dimensions overlap.

### 3. Pipeline bubbles and DualPipe

Use only the question's simplified one-direction formula, **f=(p-1)/(m+p-1)**, with p=16. For m=16, f=15/31=**48.3871%**; for m=64, f=15/79=**18.9873%**; for m=256, f=15/271=**5.53506%**. Thus larger micro-batch counts amortize fill/drain idle time, with useful fractions 51.6129%, 81.0127% and 94.4649% in this simplified balanced model. Real stage imbalance, communication, optimizer steps and memory limits are absent from the formula.

DualPipe's design idea is to schedule bidirectional pipeline work and overlap useful forward/backward computation with communication and otherwise idle periods. Opposite-direction work can occupy slots that a one-direction schedule leaves empty. This is a qualitative explanation of the mechanism, not a claim that the formula above exactly describes DualPipe. No exact DualPipe bubble percentage is derivable from p and m alone without its operation durations, overlap assumptions and schedule.

As an explicitly **assumed illustration**, if overlap hid half of the modeled idle time without introducing new overhead, the residual idle fractions would be 24.1935%, 9.49367% and 2.76753%. Those are hypothetical halves of the baseline percentages, not report measurements or the report's DualPipe expression. Even this illustration must not be converted into an equal wall-clock speedup without accounting for total work and overlap feasibility.

The named costs are more complex scheduling/dependencies, additional live activation/buffer pressure from overlapping work, communication coordination and potentially extra model/state residency depending on placement. More micro-batches can increase activation/queueing pressure or reduce kernel efficiency when each is tiny. Evaluate measured end-to-end throughput, peak memory and exposed communication with fixed global-batch semantics; the bubble fraction alone cannot defend every implementation choice.

### 4. GRPO and groups with no relative signal

GRPO replaces a separately learned value critic with a group-relative reward baseline: sample several responses to one prompt, compare their rewards to the group mean and form relative advantages, often normalized by group reward spread. This removes critic training/storage/optimization cost, but requires multiple responses and relies on useful reward variation within a prompt's group. A reward function/verifier still exists; “critic-free” does not mean supervision-free. Exact clipping, KL and normalization details beyond the supplied setting are not needed for this calculation.

For the stipulated independent eight-sample binary-reward group, let q be each response's pass probability. All rewards agree if all pass or all fail, so the probability of **no within-group reward-difference signal** is

$$P_{zero}=q^8+(1-q)^8.$$

At q=0.90, this is **0.43046722 = 43.046722%**. At q=0.99, it is **0.9227446944279202 = 92.27446944%**. The corresponding chances of a mixed group are **56.953278%** and **7.72553056%**. Expected independent groups until a mixed group are about **1.75583** and **12.9441**, respectively, showing a concrete sampling-efficiency cost when prompts become easy. These waiting values assume groups also remain independent with constant q.

With identical rewards, subtracting the group mean yields zero advantages; an implementation needs a safe zero-variance convention rather than division by zero. A KL/regularization term can still act, so “no learning signal” here specifically means no relative reward signal, not necessarily an entirely zero optimizer update. Correlated samples, nonbinary rewards and changing policy probabilities violate the binomial assumption and change these rates. A harder/more varied prompt curriculum or larger group may help, but costs more generation and must not be presented as a free fix.
