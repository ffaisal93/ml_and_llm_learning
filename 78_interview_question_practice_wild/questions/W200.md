## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">You have read the DeepSeek-V3 technical report. Prepare a defence of four of its design choices: for each one, give the numbers that justify it and name what it costs. A summary of the abstract is not an answer.

Use these figures from the report.

Model size: 671 B total parameters, 37 B activated per token, 61 layers.
Attention: 128 heads of dimension 128. MLA uses a joint KV compression rank of 512 plus a decoupled RoPE key of width 64.
Experts: 256 routed experts plus 1 shared expert, with top-8 routing.
Parallelism: expert parallelism over 64 GPUs on 8 nodes, and 16-way pipeline parallelism.
Serving: a 131,072-token context and a bf16 cache.

Use decimal units (1 KB = 1,000 bytes).

Questions to answer
1.
Multi-head latent attention (MLA). Compute the KV cache per token and per 131,072-token sequence under MLA and under standard multi-head attention with the same shape. State the reduction factor, and explain at least two costs of MLA.
2.
Mixture of experts and node-limited routing. Compute the fraction of parameters activated per token. For one layer, compute two fractions of experts that fire: routed only (8 of 256), and counting the shared expert (9 of 257). Explain why routing is capped at 4 of the 8 nodes. Say what the cap buys and what it costs the router.
3.
DualPipe. Take a one-directional pipeline schedule with p = 16 stages. Compute the idle fraction (p - 1) / (m + p - 1) for m = 16, 64 and 256 micro-batches. Explain how DualPipe reduces the bubble. Say what it costs.
4.
Critic-free policy optimisation (GRPO). Explain what replaces the critic, one advantage of that choice and one disadvantage. Then quantify the disadvantage. Assume a group of 8 samples with binary rewards. Assume the samples are independent and each passes with the same probability. How often does a prompt yield a group with no learning signal when that probability is 90%, and when it is 99%?
Expected answer

A written answer of roughly 200 to 300 words per question. Every claim has either a computed number or a named cost. State your assumptions where the report does not specify a value.

Constraints
Quote only figures given above or computed from them.
Mark anything you recall from the report beyond that as recalled.
Label any number you assume as an assumption.
Treat the bubble formula in question 3 as a simplified model, not the exact expression in the report. The DualPipe figure derived from it is an illustration only.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Defend a paper's contribution in a research interview. Separate the claimed contribution from the evidence that actually establishes it.
2. Restate the problem, baseline and proposed mechanism in your own words.
3. Inspect ablations, compute/data parity, uncertainty, benchmark contamination and whether improvements survive meaningful slices.
4. Propose one discriminating experiment and discuss reproduction cost and deployment constraints. Be explicit about what you have not verified.
5. Translate a research technique into a production decision. A benchmark gain must survive the application's latency, data, maintenance and failure constraints.
6. Restate the proposed mechanism and the baseline it would replace.
7. Estimate integration cost and run a representative offline or shadow comparison with fixed success criteria.
8. Evaluate operational behavior, regressions, monitoring and rollback before broader adoption.

### Apply the exact contract

Separate architectural claims (such as latent attention, expert routing and multi-token prediction) from training/engineering claims. Explain how each changes active FLOPs, KV bytes, communication, numerical stability or supervision. An auxiliary multi-token training objective does not by itself prove exact multi-token inference. Compare compute/data-matched ablations and distinguish total expert parameters from active parameters per token; identify what evidence is absent rather than inventing an ablation result.

### Small example / sanity check

A new architecture trained on twice the data cannot attribute its full gain to architecture without a matched-data comparison.

### Common trap

Reciting the abstract or a leaderboard number instead of identifying the causal evidence for the claim.
