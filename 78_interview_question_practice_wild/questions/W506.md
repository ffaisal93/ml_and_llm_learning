## Question

Trading Betting Game (EV / Market-Making)

The core QR/QT interview round: a live betting game over many rounds. Starting from USD 1000, you choose how much to wager across several sub-games (coin flips, dice sums, card products) given randomized odds each round, plus a market-making sub-game on the sum of three cards. The interviewer probes your sizing and reasoning; speed and decision-making under pressure matter more than ending profit.

### Requirements

A timed game (≈25–45 minutes) on an assessment platform, played over multiple rounds with a starting bankroll of USD 1000 (often unlimited rounds within a fixed total time).
Three recurring betting sub-games, each with several wagers whose odds are randomly generated each round:
Coin flips — e.g. H&gt;T, T&gt;H, HTH/THT, HHH/TTT.
Two dice — bet on the sum (=2 or 3, =4, =10, =11 or 12, odd, even, etc.).
Three cards — bet on the product of the first two (&gt;20, &gt;100, odd, even).
A market-making sub-game: a market on the sum of three cards where you can buy or sell at quoted prices that move; you profit by buying below or selling above the true sum.
Each round you have ~2–4 minutes to compute EVs and allocate; the interviewer asks you to justify each decision and follows up on sizing and the probability of profit.
Notes
Whether you end up profitable is widely reported as not the main signal — the interviewer evaluates speed, calibrated sizing, and clear reasoning under time pressure. The stated intent is finding people who spot market opportunities fast in high-pressure settings.
Bet only positive-EV wagers; when an arbitrage (guaranteed profit) appears, size it aggressively / all-in rather than applying Kelly. Interviewers frequently hint that candidates are under-betting good edges.
Kelly is a useful framing but several interviewers explicitly say it "doesn't apply here" for the arbitrage cases — don't over-fit to Kelly. A common pitfall is spending too long computing exact EVs early; pick up the pace after the first round.
A related estimation variant scores you on confidence intervals: each round you give a closed interval [L, U] for a numeric answer and score L/U if the true value lands inside it, 0 otherwise — incentivizing the tightest honest interval.

## Worked solution

### Convert every quote into a payoff before sizing

Clarify whether quoted odds are net profit or total payout, whether stakes are returned, card ranks/deck/replacement rules, position limits and whether trades consume collateral. For a unit stake with net win b and loss one, win probability p gives

$$\mathrm{EV}=pb-(1-p),\qquad p_{\mathrm{break-even}}=\frac1{b+1}.$$

With a fair coin and net payout 1.2, EV is 0.1 per unit staked. Positive EV does not make a single-round win certain. From a 1,000-dollar bankroll, taking repeated correlated risks at full bankroll can still lead to ruin.

### Compute recurring probabilities quickly

For two fair six-sided dice, sums two through twelve have counts `[1,2,3,4,5,6,5,4,3,2,1]` out of 36. Thus sum four is 3/36, sum ten is 3/36, sums two-or-three total 3/36, and parity is one half. Check whether the stated events overlap before combining bets.

For exactly three fair independent flips, HHH and TTT each have probability 1/8, HTH and THT each 1/8, and more heads than tails has probability one half. “HTH occurs before THT” is a different waiting-time game; do not use the fixed-three-flip probability for it. Card-product probabilities require the actual deck/rank/replacement convention. Enumerate ordered draws without replacement or use the stated distribution rather than assuming ranks are uniform after observing a card.

### Size risks and verify arbitrage

For the simple independent repeated binary bet, the log-growth Kelly fraction is $f^*=(bp-(1-p))/b$, clipped to feasible bounds. Uncertain probabilities and correlated wagers justify smaller fractions or a portfolio calculation. A positive edge is not an arbitrage.

A true arbitrage is a feasible set of trades with nonnegative payoff in **every** possible state and a strict gain somewhere (or a guaranteed positive payoff at nonpositive cost). Check fees, fill risk, collateral and maximum loss state by state. Size within capital and platform constraints; an apparent all-in opportunity can disappear when one leg does not fill. This explains why ordinary risky-bet Kelly sizing is not the relevant calculation for a genuinely locked-in profit.

### Market-make the three-card sum

Update the conditional expected sum after each revealed card. Buy below conditional fair value or sell above it after costs, while accounting for inventory risk and the quote provider's possible information. The realized hidden sum is not known merely because an expectation is calculated. Keep a short table of event probability, payout, EV, worst loss and chosen stake; explain the best few opportunities under the time limit instead of optimizing a tiny edge while missing the round. Ending profit is noisy evidence of decision quality; evaluate the reasoning and risk taken, not just one realized path.
