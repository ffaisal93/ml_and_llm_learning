## Question

Two behavioral surfaces appear across Walmart loops: a 45-60 minute HM round during the main onsite, and additional behavioral content woven into every Team Fit conversation afterward. Both lean toward leadership, conflict, data-driven decisions, and operational excellence rather than scripted competency lists.

Representative prompts reported
A time you convinced people to follow your advice without authority over them.
A time you used data to drive a big decision.
A production issue you fixed, end to end, including detection, mitigation, and root cause.
A time you discovered a colleague violating integrity or compliance — what did you do.
How do you handle conflict on a team you do not lead.
How do you motivate a team as a tech leader.
Why Walmart.
A project where you drove operational excellence in your team.
A retrospective on a project that did not go as planned.
Notes
Use STAR and quantify the result whenever possible — Walmart interviewers consistently push for numbers and "what changed because of you, specifically."
Leadership prompts are graded on whether you can describe how the team's behavior changed, not on the persuasion tactic itself.
The compliance/integrity prompt is the trickiest — interviewers want escalation through the correct channel without dramatizing.
The Team Fit phase reuses the same prompts; do not refresh stories per team beyond the "why this team" half.
Several rounds flagged silent interviewers who do not push back even when stories are thin; treat silence as a prompt to deepen.

## Worked solution

### Prepare leadership through changed behavior

Use real stories; the following are **illustrative anchors**, not claimed experience. For influence without authority, bring a reproducible duplicate-processing failure to a partner team, agree an event contract and fund a small joint pilot. The outcome is that both teams adopt stable keys/version checks, not simply that you persuaded someone. For a data decision, show how a forward-time evaluation overturned a random-split model gain. For operational excellence, explain the actual runbook, ownership or deployment-gate change and its observed effect.

A production-issue story must cover detection → mitigation → root cause → prevention: for example, tail latency rose, you paused rollout, found layered retries amplifying an upstream timeout, bounded retries/admission and added a regression load drill. Distinguish your diagnostic/rollout decisions from others' infrastructure work. For a retrospective, own an incorrect dependency estimate and say how planning changed; do not disguise a success as failure.

For suspected integrity/compliance violation, separate observed facts from assumptions, preserve relevant evidence within authorized access, avoid public accusations/confrontation and report through the appropriate manager/compliance/security channel. If the manager is implicated, use the designated independent channel. Contain immediate authorized risk without conducting an amateur investigation or promising confidentiality you cannot guarantee. This is a hypothetical response, not a claim about a colleague or company incident.

As a tech leader, motivate with a clear user goal, achievable scope, autonomy, timely feedback and shared credit; resolve conflict by evidence and decision ownership even without formal authority. For “Why Walmart/this team?”, connect actual interests and verified role scope rather than inventing company facts. Quantify only defensible baselines/outcomes; describe changed team behavior when a percentage is unavailable. Keep the factual stories consistent across Team Fit conversations and adjust only the role-specific motivation. Silence is an opportunity to provide missing evidence, not an invitation to exaggerate.
