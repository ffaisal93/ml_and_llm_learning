## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Applied Scientist round built around an under-defined business problem. The interviewer states a vague, stripped-down prompt and waits; you must clarify goals, propose a plan, and reason through tradeoffs aloud.

From the official module: &quot;A critical skill for this role is dealing with ambiguous and ill-defined problems... This module tests your ability to productively discuss a problem and walk through what you would do and why. The stated problem will be slightly understated / vague / undefined in the beginning.&quot;

Topic areas seen: search/ranking/experimentation, brand-safety scoring, generation-quality assessment.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Structure an ML system-design answer. Start from the decision the system must support, then connect data, modeling, serving and evaluation.
2. Clarify users, target, prediction time, latency, scale and error costs; state assumptions if details are missing.
3. Design data collection and labels, a baseline, offline evaluation, serving interfaces and feedback loops.
4. Estimate capacity and discuss failures, monitoring, rollback and abuse. Spend deep-dive time on the hardest constraint rather than listing every database technology.
5. Prepare a research experiment with limited compute. Spend the budget on experiments that most clearly distinguish hypotheses.
6. State the hypothesis, expected observable effect and a simple falsifying result.
7. Choose a small faithful setting, strong baseline and controlled ablation before scaling.
8. Track compute, seeds and failures, and reserve budget for confirmation rather than spending everything on a broad unreplicated search.
9. Separate correlation, prediction and causation. A useful predictive association need not describe the effect of intervening on a feature.
10. State whether the goal is forecasting, explanation or estimating an intervention effect.
11. List plausible confounders and timing relationships; check selection and measurement mechanisms.
12. Use randomization or a defensible causal identification strategy for causal claims, with explicit assumptions and sensitivity checks.

### Small example / sanity check

For spam detection, the correct latency and false-positive cost differ between pre-publication blocking and later review.

### Common trap

Drawing infrastructure before defining what counts as a correct model decision.
