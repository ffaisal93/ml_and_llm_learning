## Question

Applied Scientist round built around an under-defined business problem. The interviewer states a vague, stripped-down prompt and waits; you must clarify goals, propose a plan, and reason through tradeoffs aloud.

From the official module: "A critical skill for this role is dealing with ambiguous and ill-defined problems... This module tests your ability to productively discuss a problem and walk through what you would do and why. The stated problem will be slightly understated / vague / undefined in the beginning."

Topic areas seen: search/ranking/experimentation, brand-safety scoring, generation-quality assessment.

## Worked solution

### Turn an ambiguous request into a decision

The actual business case is absent, so there is no unique numerical/model answer. Here is a **fictional worked dialogue** for “Improve search.” First ask who searches, what success means, which failure motivates the request, what can change and what latency/data/experiment constraints exist. Offer measurable alternatives: more satisfied purchases, less time to find a relevant result, or fewer zero-result queries. State a provisional assumption: a retail site wants more completed satisfactory orders per search with no worse returns, safety or p99 latency.

If the interviewer says “conversion is down,” do not assume the ranker is at fault. Check logging and sample mix, outages, inventory, price changes, query cohorts and label maturity. Decompose the funnel into search→eligible result→click→cart→paid→retained order. A checkout failure needs a different fix from poor retrieval. Propose a small diagnostic audit: replay representative failing queries, inspect candidate recall and human relevance, and compare recent periods by region/device/new users.

### A concrete conditional plan

Assume the audit finds exact-brand queries losing relevant in-stock products during retrieval. The baseline is lexical search plus current hard availability filters. Add a hybrid lexical/semantic candidate union, then a lightweight learning-to-rank model using query match, content similarity, price, user intent and quality. Do not add a large generator merely because the prompt is vague. Log candidate sets, displayed impressions, positions, assignment, feature/model versions and delayed order/return outcomes. Reconstruct training features as of request time and use forward-time/duplicate-product splits.

Offline require improved recall on brand queries and NDCG on judged relevance without safety/filter violations. Estimate serving cost: an assumed 10,000 QPS×200 ranked products is two million pair scores/s; if the available 100 ms budget has only 20 ms spare, precompute embeddings and bound candidate count or defer a heavier model. A small representative query set can falsify the approach cheaply before a full training project.

Online randomize persistently by user, define conversion/retained orders as primary and return rate, latency, zero results and marketplace concentration as guardrails. Predeclare power/duration and avoid repeatedly stopping at favorable significance. Exposure logging and limited exploration address position/popularity bias; observational increases after rollout cannot alone prove causation. Inventory spillovers may require geo/time switchbacks. Shadow/canary, keep the old retrieval/ranker available and define rollback conditions.

### Reason aloud about changing constraints

If labels are absent, use blinded human judgments and instrument feedback before claiming supervised uplift. If the deadline is one week, fix the demonstrated retrieval/filter defect and run a small safe test rather than propose a six-month platform. If brand safety dominates, define policy labels/action thresholds and review costs; if generation quality is the case, define a task-specific human rubric, factual/brand checks and a holdout, rather than reuse search NDCG blindly.

End with the next decision: “I would first validate whether retrieval is the bottleneck on a representative audit; if it is, I would run this bounded hybrid test. If it is not, I would revisit the funnel evidence.” This is a productive conditional answer with hypotheses, evidence, costs and alternatives. Replace assumptions as the interviewer supplies information; do not present the fictional diagnosis as known business data.
