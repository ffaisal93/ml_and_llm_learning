## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Beat the Odds — Probability Test

Answer a timed set of multiple-choice probability questions, choosing for each the option closest to the true value.

Format
1.
About 30 questions, roughly 90 seconds each. A question can be skipped, but you cannot go back to it.
2.
Each question has about five numeric options; select the one closest to the exact answer. Fast estimation is usually enough.
3.
Wrong answers are penalised, so a blind guess has negative expected value.
4.
Topics: dice, coin and card probability; expected value; random walks on polygons and grids (hitting and return times); gambler&#x27;s ruin; combinatorics; normal-approximation estimates.
Practice questions

For each question, give the probability or expected value.

1.
A fair die is thrown twice. Probability that the second throw differs from the first.
2.
A shuffled 52-card deck has its top 10 cards discarded. Probability that the new top card is red.
3.
Two dice are thrown. Probability that the sum is 11 or 12.
4.
A coin is flipped 3 times. Probability that all three outcomes are the same.
5.
61 coins are placed at random into 15 boxes. Probability that some box holds more than 4 coins.
6.
A bag holds 10 red, 10 yellow and 10 blue balls. Balls are drawn uniformly at random without replacement until one colour is used up (only two colours remain in the bag). Expected number of balls left in the bag.
7.
A die is rolled repeatedly, keeping a running sum, until the sum exceeds 100. Probability that the last roll was a 2.
8.
Two particles start at opposite vertices of a regular octagon. Every second each particle independently flips a fair coin and moves one edge clockwise or anticlockwise. Expected number of seconds until they are on the same vertex, and the corresponding number of coin flips (two per second).
9.
A particle sits on a vertex of a regular decagon. Every second a die is rolled: faces 1-2 move it clockwise, faces 3-4 anticlockwise, faces 5-6 leave it in place. Expected number of seconds until it first reaches the vertex opposite its start.
10.
A particle starts at lattice point (5, 5) of the grid of points (x, y) with 0 &lt;= x, y &lt;= 10. Each step it moves up, down, left or right with probability 1/4 each. Expected number of steps until it first reaches a point with x or y equal to 0 or 10.
11.
Start with USD 10 and wager USD 1 per independent round, stopping at USD 0 or USD 20. Find the probability of reaching USD 20 for win probabilities (a) 0.5 and (b) 0.6.
12.
Six fair coins are flipped. Probability of (a) more heads than tails, (b) at most 3 heads.
13.
A machine outputs integers uniformly from 1 to 10. You and a friend each add two outputs. Probability that the two sums are equal.
14.
An integer is chosen uniformly from 1 to 1,000,000. Probability that it contains the digit 7 at least once.
Output
For each question, a number (probability or expectation) as an exact fraction or a decimal to 3 significant figures, precise enough to pick the closest option. Questions 8, 11 and 12 have two values each.
Constraints
About 90 seconds per question; a calculator and scratch paper are usually allowed.
Recognise the family (counting, symmetry, first-step analysis, closed forms) rather than computing from scratch.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. 1: P(second die differs)=5/6. 2: after unseen discards, any remaining position is exchangeable with an original card, so red probability=1/2. 3: sums 11/12 have 3 ordered outcomes, giving 1/12. 4: HHH or TTT gives 2/8=1/4. 5: 15 boxes with at most 4 each hold at most 60 coins, so the 61st forces the event with probability 1.
2. 6: Let E(a,b,c) be expected remaining balls until a color empties. Base E= a+b+c when any component is zero; otherwise E=[aE(a-1,b,c)+bE(a,b-1,c)+cE(a,b,c-1)]/(a+b+c). Memoization gives E(10,10,10)=310/77≈4.025974.
3. 7: Let u(0)=1 and u(s)=sum_(d=1..6,s>=d)u(s-d)/6 for reachable sums through 100. Last roll 2 has probability [u(99)+u(100)]/6≈0.0952381. 8: relative position divided by two is a four-cycle lazy walk; first-step equations give 8 seconds and 16 total flips. 9: a ten-cycle walk moves with probability 2/3, so hitting the opposite vertex takes 5*(10-5)/(2/3)=37.5 seconds.
4. 10: Solve h(x,y)=1+mean(neighbor h), with boundary h=0; the center of the 0..10 grid gives about 29.239374 steps. 11: gambler’s ruin gives i/M=1/2 for the fair case and [1-(q/p)^i]/[1-(q/p)^M]≈0.982954 for i=10,M=20,p=0.6.
5. 12: Binomial(6,1/2) gives P(H>3)=11/32 and P(H<=3)=21/32. 13: sum-count frequencies 1,2,...,10,9,...,1 give matching-sum probability sum(count²)/10000=0.067. 14: six-digit zero-padded counting gives 1-(9/10)^6=0.468559; exchanging 0 for 1000000 leaves this count unchanged.

### Small example / sanity check

The numerical checks solve the bag recurrence and 81 interior-grid hitting-time equations, rather than guessing a “nice” closed form for the grid. A repeated title remains a separate question entry so neither 14-part practice set is lost.

### Common trap

Treating discarded unseen cards as biased, assuming the last die is uniform at a threshold crossing, or counting particle seconds as just one flip per second.
