## Question

Vanta's behavioral loop runs in two flavors: a Principles round that hands you a hypothetical scenario and watches how you reason, and a Manager/Leadership round that digs into a project you led and how you make decisions. Modeled loosely on Amazon-style values.

### Requirements

Principles round: rather than STAR stories, you're given a *hypothetical situation* and asked what you'd do — they grade your reasoning and how it maps to Vanta's stated values. Example: during implementation you hit an unforeseen bug that, if left, creates tech debt, but fixing it delays launch — how do you decide, and when would you instead prioritize paying down the debt?
Manager / Leadership round: walk through a project you led; the interviewer cares about your thought process, decision-making, and trade-offs more than the outcome.
Recurring prompts: resolve a conflict with a teammate; communicate a setback to stakeholders; what do you do when there isn't enough time; mentoring others.
Notes
The behavioral bar is run against an internal set of values/principles (Amazon-LP-style "tenets"), so prepare values-aligned stories as well as on-the-spot scenario reasoning.
Feedback here is frequently vague and can feel arbitrary — one candidate was dinged for "not giving concrete cases" in a round the recruiter had framed as hypothetical-scenario, and another was failed in the leadership round for a project judged "not impactful enough." Lead with clearly-scoped, high-impact examples and make your decision-making explicit.

## Worked solution

### Reason about the hypothetical; use a real project for leadership

For the unforeseen bug, first classify impact: data loss/security/incorrect customer outcomes versus a contained maintainability issue. Reproduce and bound it, then compare a fix, a scoped launch without the affected feature, or a temporary workaround with owner/expiry. Take a stance: block or reduce scope for a correctness/security defect; for a low-risk internal refactor, ship only with tests, monitoring, a documented debt item and a funded deadline. Technical debt is not a label that excuses an unknown risk.

Tell stakeholders promptly what changed, the evidence, the options and the recommended trade-off. If repeated workaround cost or a near-term roadmap dependency exceeds the value of shipping sooner, prioritize paying the debt down. A concrete hypothetical: a retry path can create duplicate external jobs, so I would disable that feature or add stable idempotency before launch; postponing a naming cleanup would not have the same risk. These are explicit judgments, not claims about an unpublished internal Vanta rubric.

For the leadership round, no personal history is supplied. Prepare a **real** project with problem, your decision rights, rejected alternative, an actual outcome and reflection. An illustrative candidate is a staged migration where you negotiated a smaller first release, used crash/retry evidence to resolve a peer disagreement and mentored a colleague to own the consumer. Explain the concrete next action when time ran out—cut optional scope and reset dates, not “work harder.” If a setback occurs, own the assumption, preserve the user's safe path and show what changed in planning/tests afterward.

Distinguish the two round modes: do not dodge a hypothetical by reciting STAR, and do not answer a request for a real event with a fictional scenario. Ask for the current published values/role criteria if needed; align evidence honestly rather than guessing secret tenets or exaggerating impact.
