Notes

The expected answers are conceptual but precise.

Convex loss surfaces are the clean case where any local minimum is global.
SGD has noisier gradient estimates than full-batch GD; that noise can help escape some sharp or shallow basins.
Full-batch uses the exact empirical gradient but may converge to sharper minima and is often less scalable.
Generalization gap appears when training loss and population loss diverge; flat minima are less sensitive to small distribution shifts.
Calibration asks whether predicted probabilities match empirical frequencies. Temperature scaling, Platt scaling, isotonic regression, and reliability diagrams are standard tools.
Drift detection can monitor feature distribution shift, prediction distribution shift, calibration decay, performance on delayed labels, and business KPI deltas.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Why use AdamW, warmup and gradient clipping? They address different training problems: adaptive update scale, early-step instability and unusually large gradients.
2. Adam maintains first and second moment estimates, with bias correction after zero initialization.
3. AdamW applies decoupled weight decay rather than treating decay exactly as another adaptively scaled loss gradient.
4. Warm up learning rate over initial steps and clip the global gradient norm when appropriate. Inspect logs and ablations; these are tuning tools, not correctness fixes.
5. Distinguish data drift, concept drift and label delay. A production metric can change because inputs changed, the input-to-label relationship changed, or the labels are not yet mature.
6. Check pipeline health, feature distributions and missingness before assuming the model changed.
7. When mature labels exist, compare conditional performance and calibration on matched slices and periods.
8. Separate delayed outcome windows from true degradation; use operational proxies cautiously and validate them against final outcomes.
9. Check calibration and choose a decision threshold. A probability estimate and the action taken from it are separate design choices.
10. Measure reliability on a validation set using bin plots and proper scoring rules such as log loss or Brier score.
11. Fit a calibration transform only on held-out predictions if needed, then choose a threshold using explicit costs or operational capacity.
12. Evaluate the entire calibrated-and-thresholded pipeline on untouched test data and important slices.

### Small example / sanity check

A gradient [3,4] has norm 5. Clipping its norm to 1 yields [0.6,0.8], preserving direction before the optimizer's own transformation.

### Common trap

Using clipping to hide exploding activations, bad data or incorrect loss scaling.
