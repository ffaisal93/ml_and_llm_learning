## Question

Amazon FAR (Foundational AI Research) / premium-tier paper-read round. The paper arrives ~48 hours before the interview; the slot is ~30–35 min technical discussion plus 20–25 min behavioral.

Read and internalize the assigned paper end-to-end (one recent assignment: the DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report).
Discuss in depth: problem framing, key idea, architecture, design rationale, evidence, limitations, and how you'd translate it to production.
Recent DeepSeek-V3 probes: MLA (Multi-head Latent Attention), Aux-Loss-Free MoE, MTP (Multi-Token Prediction), DualPipe. Recurring: "Why this design choice?" and "How would you use this in production?"

## Worked solution

### Give a technical argument, not a memorized abstract

The assigned paper may differ, so begin by stating its problem, proposed mechanism, strongest evidence and main limitation in about two minutes. Then trace one training example and one inference request through the design. Separate measured claims from your own production hypotheses. Do not invent personal project outcomes for the behavioral portion.

### Explain the named mechanisms precisely

For the named report, the central theme is reducing useful-compute and serving costs through coordinated architecture and systems choices:

- **MLA:** cache compressed latent key/value information plus the positional component instead of a conventional full per-head KV representation. The benefit is lower cache memory; compare the exact cache tensors and attention kernels before claiming a speedup.
- **Auxiliary-loss-free balancing:** expert-selection biases respond to overload/underload while gating weights use the original affinity. The report still includes a small sequence-wise balance loss, so “no auxiliary loss anywhere” is inaccurate.
- **MTP:** training predicts additional future tokens through sequential prediction modules. Distinguish the training objective from optional speculative decoding at serving time.
- **DualPipe:** bidirectional pipeline scheduling overlaps computation and communication. Its value depends on hardware topology and the compute/communication balance, rather than being a universal instruction to double pipeline throughput.

### Critique the evidence

For every claimed gain ask which baseline changes, whether the comparison controls data/token budget and hardware, and whether an ablation isolates the mechanism. A complete-system benchmark cannot establish that one routing choice caused all quality gains. Examine metric definitions, variance, evaluation contamination risk, missing domains and whether the same conclusions hold at your workload's context length and batch size.

For a balancing method, inspect expert-load distribution and quality together: perfect balance can harm specialization, while good average quality can hide overloaded devices and tail latency. For a memory-saving method, distinguish resident model weights, KV memory, activations and communication buffers. Active parameters per token do not determine the total weight memory required to serve an MoE.

### Translate it into a production decision

Propose a controlled pilot on the intended workload. Record task quality, first-token latency, inter-token latency, throughput, peak memory, cost per successful task and failure recovery. Match numerical precision, batching and hardware across baselines where possible. Test short and long contexts plus skewed traffic; a large balanced benchmark batch can conceal small-batch routing bottlenecks.

Roll out only the component that solves a measured bottleneck and is supported by the serving stack. Keep a simpler baseline, shadow traffic, versioned artifacts and rollback. For a project/behavioral discussion, prepare an actual example with problem, your decision, rejected alternative, evidence, failure and what changed afterward. If asked about a result you did not run, explain the proposed experiment rather than presenting a hypothetical as an accomplishment.
