## Worked solution

### Vary samples, not parameters

Choose a fixed p=80 linear model and generate Gaussian X with a fixed ten-coordinate signal plus noise std .5. For each seed, use nested training prefixes n=10 through 200, including 78–82, and a separate 2,000-example test set. Fit the minimum-norm least-squares solution and ridge with the same SVD implementation as [W224](W224.md); call `double_descent("outputs_201_250", seeds=30, sample_wise=True)`. This is a runnable notebook-equivalent experiment, with [sample-wise figure](../assets/review_201_250_sample_wise.png) and [all seed-level measurements](../assets/review_201_250_sample_wise.json). It is distinct from W224's varying-p experiment. The 30-seed median test MSE is 1.158 at n=10, .941 at n=40, 49.648 at n=80 and .391 at n=200, demonstrating both descents and the interpolation spike on the actual fixture.

The expected excess prediction error for isotropic Gaussian features clarifies the mechanism. For n<p-1, minimum-norm risk contains a bias term $(1-n/p)\|w^*\|^2$ and noise variance $\sigma^2 n/(p-n-1)$. For n>p+1, OLS excess risk is $\sigma^2p/(n-p-1)$. Add irreducible test noise $\sigma^2$ to compare against observed test MSE. Expectations diverge at the interpolation boundary, although each nonsingular finite draw usually has a finite numerical error. With p=80, $\|w^*\|^2=1$ and $\sigma^2=.25$, the approximate total risk at n=20 is $0.75+5/59+.25\approx1.085$; at n=60 it is $.25+15/19+.25\approx1.289$, and near 80 it becomes much larger. Far above 80 the variance decreases again. The initial descent may be shallow or absent at other signal-to-noise ratios; show the actual curve and uncertainty.

Ridge solves $\min_w\|Xw-y\|^2/n+\lambda\|w\|^2$ and suppresses small singular values with $s/(s^2+n\lambda)$. It trades interpolation and some bias for lower variance. The provided experiment plots a fixed lambda=.02 comparison; a final model would tune it on validation rather than selecting from test curves. Explain why early stopping is another form of spectral regularization, but does not produce exactly the same estimator as ridge.

A four-hour submission can use six notebook sections: hypothesis/controls; data and solver; numerical checks; train/test curve with seed bands; spectral/risk explanation; ridge ablation and limits. Verify minimum-norm residuals, inspect singular values near n=p, repeat seeds and retain the raw measurements. Tests in this batch check exact interpolation in the overparameterized regime and that the recorded threshold peak exceeds both neighboring broad regimes; they do not assert every random realization has the same shape.
