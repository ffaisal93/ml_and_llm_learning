## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">K-means is the standard algorithm for splitting points into K groups by distance. Implement it from scratch for a given set of starting centers, so that the result is deterministic.

What to implement

kmeans(points, centroids, max_iters=100)

points: a list of n tuples of numbers, all of the same dimension d.
centroids: a list of k initial centers, each a tuple of d numbers.
max_iters: the maximum number of rounds, at least 1.
Return (centroids, labels): the final centers as k lists of d floats, and labels, a list of n integers where labels[i] is the cluster index of points[i].
Rules

Repeat at most max_iters times:

1.
Assignment: give each point the index of the centroid with the smallest squared Euclidean distance. If several centroids are equally close, use the lowest index.
2.
If the labels equal the labels of the previous round, stop. The first round never stops here.
3.
Update: set each centroid to the mean of the points assigned to it. A centroid with no points keeps its current value.

The returned labels are those of the last assignment, and the returned centroids are the values after the last update. Do not modify points or centroids.

Examples
1.
kmeans([(0, 0), (0, 1), (10, 10), (10, 11)], [(0, 0), (10, 10)]) returns ([[0.0, 0.5], [10.0, 10.5]], [0, 0, 1, 1]): two clear groups; the centers move to the group means.
2.
kmeans([(0,), (1,), (2,), (3,), (10,)], [(0,), (1,)], 1) returns ([[0.0], [4.0]], [0, 1, 1, 1, 1]): after one round the labels come from the starting centers and the centers have been updated once.
3.
kmeans([(0,), (4,)], [(2,), (2,)]) returns ([[2.0], [2.0]], [0, 0]): both centers are equally close, so index 0 wins and the empty cluster keeps its center.
Constraints
1 &lt;= k &lt;= n &lt;= 1000, 1 &lt;= d &lt;= 10.
Coordinates are integers or floats of moderate size.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Implement k-means with convergence and empty clusters. Alternate assigning points to their closest centers and moving each center to the mean of its assigned points.
2. Initialize k distinct data indices under an explicit random seed, requiring 1<=k<=N.
3. Compute squared distances, assign closest centers and recompute non-empty means. For an empty cluster, retain its center or explicitly reseed it.
4. Stop on a center-shift tolerance or iteration cap, then recompute labels for the returned centers. Compare objective values, not center ordering, across implementations.
5. Choose the number of k-means clusters. There is rarely one objectively correct k; the useful resolution depends on geometry and the downstream task.
6. Compare objective curves across k, using several initialization seeds.
7. Inspect cluster stability, silhouette behavior, sizes and representative examples, and standardize features if appropriate.
8. Validate usefulness for the real decision; do not choose a visually neat elbow without checking whether clusters are meaningful.

### Small example / sanity check

For [0,1,9,10] with k=2 and sensible initialization, centers converge to 0.5 and 9.5.

### Common trap

Taking the mean of an empty slice, stopping with labels from old centers, or assuming a local optimum is globally best.
