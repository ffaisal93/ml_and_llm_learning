## Question

How do you manage your team member's stress?

## Worked solution

### Change the workload and uncertainty, not only the mood

Start privately: “I noticed the workload seems heavy; how is it feeling, and what would help?” Listen without diagnosing or requiring personal disclosure. Distinguish an urgent overload, unclear priorities, conflict, missing skills/support and broader personal circumstances; the employee chooses what to share. Ask what can be changed now and agree a follow-up.

A **hypothetical example** is an engineer handling on-call incidents while committed to a major launch. I would review the actual queue with them, identify the truly urgent work, reduce or defer scope with stakeholders, redistribute on-call fairly and provide a backup/mentor. Make escalation and “done” criteria explicit so they do not feel personally responsible for every unresolved issue. Protect private details; communicate the team's capacity and delivery plan rather than the person's health story.

Do not praise repeated overtime as the solution. Offer available leave, flexibility or employee-support resources without acting as a clinician or forcing their use. If there is an immediate workplace safety concern, use the organization's appropriate support/escalation process. Check whether the agreed workload/interruptions actually decreased and whether the person feels supported; do not turn a private stress conversation into a performance score.

Longer term, rotate burdens, reserve incident capacity, clarify ownership and make it safe to flag risks early. A manager cannot promise zero stress or fix every external factor, but can remove avoidable pressure and uncertainty. This is a management approach and illustrative scenario, not a claim about a specific employee or a medical assessment.
