## Question

Hiring-manager and project-lead conversations centered on resume/project deep-dive plus a recurring AI theme: your view of AI, how it changes your daily work, and how you collaborate with AI tools.

### Requirements

One or more conversational rounds with the hiring manager and/or a project lead. Two recurring threads:

Project / resume deep-dive — your background, the projects you owned, and the engineering decisions behind them.
AI philosophy — your view of AI, how it affects your day-to-day work, and how you collaborate with AI tools. This comes up across recent loops, not just in the OA.

For senior/Staff candidates the surrounding conversation also covers on-call schedules, security/infrastructure incident response, sprint process, roadmap planning, and org/team structure.

### Notes

The AI conversation is a real signal, not small talk — a candidate who chatted about "views on AI and its impact on daily work" with a project lead was nonetheless cut, so bring a concrete, opinionated stance: where AI genuinely accelerates you, where you still own the judgment, and how you keep quality high while using it.
These rounds are friendly and free-flowing; lead with ownership and specific outcomes rather than waiting to be interrogated.

## Worked solution

### Project ownership and an AI stance

Use a real project in an interview. Here is an **illustrative answer**, not a claim about the reader: “I owned the migration of a document-search API from batch-only indexing to incremental updates. The team had four engineers; my scope was the update protocol, API and rollback. We chose versioned documents and an outbox over dual writes, since a crash between database and index updates otherwise creates silent divergence. The launch gate was search freshness and error rate at a matched query load, with old-index fallback.” Supply your actual scale, dates and measured outcomes instead of borrowing example numbers.

Prepare the implementation-level follow-up: each edit commits the new document version plus an event in one transaction; workers upsert only if the incoming version is newer; deletion tombstones prevent an old event resurrecting removed text. Index lag is observable and the API communicates freshness. If the interviewer challenges the extra infrastructure, compare against periodic rebuilding: a rebuild is simpler when daily freshness suffices. This demonstrates a choice, rather than a technology list.

A concrete AI philosophy is: “AI reduces the cost of exploring alternatives, but evidence still determines whether a change is correct. I ask it to propose tests and small patches, then read the diff, check contracts and run an independent regression. I keep confidential data out of unapproved tools. I would not let generated text authorize an access grant or deploy an unreviewed migration.” Discuss an actual accepted and an actual rejected suggestion, and the evidence for each. Human accountability without a validation workflow is only a slogan.

For senior/Staff scope, connect delivery to operations. A version mismatch or stale-delete alert should have a named owner, runbook, severity and rollback. A security incident needs containment, preserved audit evidence, scoped credential rotation and communication through the incident lead. Explain how on-call load feeds the roadmap: recurring index-lag pages can justify backpressure work before new features. Ask about rotation size, escalation expectations, deployment ownership, planning horizon and who resolves cross-team priorities. Friendly conversation still benefits from a concise opening, a concrete decision and a clearly bounded claim of impact.
