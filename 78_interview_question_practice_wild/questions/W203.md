## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Hiring-manager and project-lead conversations centered on resume/project deep-dive plus a recurring AI theme: your view of AI, how it changes your daily work, and how you collaborate with AI tools.

Requirements

One or more conversational rounds with the hiring manager and/or a project lead. Two recurring threads:

Project / resume deep-dive — your background, the projects you owned, and the engineering decisions behind them.
AI philosophy — your view of AI, how it affects your day-to-day work, and how you collaborate with AI tools. This comes up across recent loops, not just in the OA.

For senior/Staff candidates the surrounding conversation also covers on-call schedules, security/infrastructure incident response, sprint process, roadmap planning, and org/team structure.

Notes
The AI conversation is a real signal, not small talk — a candidate who chatted about &quot;views on AI and its impact on daily work&quot; with a project lead was nonetheless cut, so bring a concrete, opinionated stance: where AI genuinely accelerates you, where you still own the judgment, and how you keep quality high while using it.
These rounds are friendly and free-flowing; lead with ownership and specific outcomes rather than waiting to be interrogated.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain your most technically complex ML project. A strong answer demonstrates decisions and evidence, not a list of technologies.
2. State the user problem, constraints and your own contribution separately from the team's work.
3. Describe the data, baseline, hardest technical failure, alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
4. Quantify the result using a valid comparison, explain limitations and say what you would change next. Use real experiences rather than invented metrics.
5. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
6. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
7. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
8. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.

### Small example / sanity check

“I reduced p95 latency from 800 to 500 ms on a matched workload while keeping task accuracy within the predefined margin” states both benefit and guardrail.

### Common trap

Saying “we built an LLM system” without explaining your choices or how success was measured.
