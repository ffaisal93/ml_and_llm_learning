## Worked solution

1. Audit factor timestamps and availability, corporate-action handling, survivorship and future-normalization leakage. Define a forecast horizon, baseline, costs/turnover and time-based validation with appropriate overlap controls.
2. IC is a chosen cross-sectional forecast/outcome correlation, often rank correlation. ICIR requires an explicit time aggregation/annualization convention; there is no universal good threshold independent of sample dependence, breadth and costs.
3. Fit a stated prediction/portfolio objective, use nested or held-out selection for many factors, and validate after costs on untouched periods. Track drift, capacity and realized-vs-predicted behavior after deployment.
4. For stability, one possible model is repeated measurements y=S+epsilon with a common latent signal and independent equal-variance errors. rho_1=0.95 implies noise/signal variance=1/19; averaging N repetitions gives rho_N=1/(1+1/(19N)), so N>=ceil(99/19)=6 reaches 0.99.
5. If instead both aggregates average N independent changing daily signals with matching independent noise, signal and noise variances both shrink by N and correlation stays 0.95. The wording alone does not justify a fixed-signal averaging model; state it explicitly.

### Small example / sanity check

Under the repeated-fixed-signal model, N=5 gives 95/96≈0.98958 and N=6 gives 114/115≈0.99130. Under independent daily-signal averaging, increasing N does not improve the ratio.

### Common trap

Quoting N=6 without identifying the noise/signal assumptions, or selecting factors by one favorable in-sample IC.
