## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">A model you trained for a credit-risk task performs worse than expected. Use the bias-variance trade-off to explain why a model can fail in two opposite ways, how you would tell which one you are facing, and what you would change in each case.

Requirements

Answer all five parts.

1.
Definitions. Define bias and variance of a learned model, and state the decomposition of expected squared prediction error into bias, variance and irreducible noise.
2.
Failure examples. Give one concrete example of a high-bias model and one of a high-variance model, and say how each looks in the training error and the validation error.
3.
Capacity. Explain how test error behaves as model capacity grows, and where the best model sits on that curve.
4.
Diagnosis. Describe the experiments you would run (learning curves, comparing training and validation error, varying capacity or training-set size) to decide whether more data, a richer model, or stronger regularisation is the right fix.
5.
Remedies. For each failure type, list at least two changes you would try and explain why each one moves the model along the trade-off, including how L2 regularisation, dropout and early stopping reduce variance at a small cost in bias.
Expected answer

A structured explanation that covers every part, uses the error decomposition correctly, gives one example per failure mode, and ends with a clear decision rule connecting the diagnosis to the remedy.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Diagnose high bias versus high variance. Use training and validation behavior to distinguish insufficient fitting from poor generalization.
2. If both errors remain high, check the pipeline and optimization first, then consider inadequate features or capacity.
3. If training error is low but validation error is high, check leakage-free splitting, distribution shift and overfitting.
4. Use learning curves and controlled changes: more data, regularization, simpler models or better features depending on the diagnosis.

### Small example / sanity check

Training error 1% and validation error 25% suggests a generalization problem, but a group-distribution mismatch could be the cause rather than model complexity alone.

### Common trap

Treating the bias–variance story as a diagnosis without inspecting label noise, split structure or optimization failure.
