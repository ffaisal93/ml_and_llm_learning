## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">A common non-coding assessment for software engineers has two automated modules. In the situational-judgment module you read a workplace scenario with several possible responses and rate each response from 1 (least effective) to 5 (most effective). In the work-style module you see pairs of statements and choose which one is more like you, on a scale running between the left and right statement. Both modules measure whether your instincts match the company&#x27;s leadership values, not technical skill. Explain how you would work through each module.

Questions to answer
1.
Rating a review comment. A senior engineer leaves a review comment on your change that is correct but needs substantial extra work for a longer-term benefit. One possible reply is &quot;Let&#x27;s meet to discuss your concerns and recommendations&quot;; another is &quot;Feel free to make any changes you think are best.&quot; How do you rate each on the 1-5 scale, and which values justify the ratings?
2.
Rating a blocked task. You are stuck on a task and the deadline is close. Order these responses from most to least effective and justify the order: troubleshoot first and then ask for help with a proposed plan, ship without review to hit the date, stay silent and keep trying, ask for help immediately without having investigated.
3.
Consistent work-style answers. In each pair both statements sound positive, and the same trait is probed by several re-phrased pairs (planning versus adapting, checking details versus taking initiative, learning versus executing). How do you answer so that your choices stay consistent, and why is trying to guess a &quot;correct&quot; persona counterproductive?
4.
Avoiding avoidable errors. Name two mistakes that lower the score for reasons unrelated to judgment (for example reading the direction of the 1-5 scale wrongly under time pressure) and how you prevent them.
Expected output

A short written answer (about one paragraph per question) that gives a concrete rating or ordering, the value or reasoning behind it, and the behavior it rewards.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. How do you handle disagreement over an ML design? Make the disputed assumptions testable and connect the decision to the shared objective.
2. Restate the other person's concern fairly and identify whether the disagreement concerns facts, constraints or preferences.
3. Propose a small experiment or decision table with agreed success criteria, cost and deadline.
4. Document the decision, support execution after it is made and revisit only when meaningful new evidence appears.
5. Explain a project failure constructively. Show ownership, evidence and changed behavior, not a story in which someone else is always at fault.
6. State the goal, your responsibility and what failed in observable terms.
7. Explain which assumption or decision was wrong and how you detected it.
8. Describe recovery, what changed afterward and whether the fix actually helped. Keep confidential details appropriately abstract.
9. Handle harsh technical feedback. Separate the useful content of feedback from its delivery and verify what needs to change.
10. Listen, summarize the specific concern and ask for concrete examples when necessary.
11. Check the evidence, acknowledge valid mistakes and propose a bounded correction with a follow-up date.
12. If disagreement remains, explain it respectfully using the shared goal and evidence, and seek the appropriate resolution channel.

### Apply the exact contract

Prepare separate real examples for conflict, failure, leadership, inclusion and feedback. Use situation→your responsibility→specific actions→measured result→lesson. A video round needs concise complete responses; a values round needs evidence of behavior, not a memorized list of slogans. When a prompt asks several questions, do not reuse one anecdote without answering the different decisions it probes.

### Small example / sanity check

A debate over retrieval complexity can become a measured comparison of answer quality, p95 latency and maintenance cost on a fixed query set.

### Common trap

Describing a colleague as incompetent or presenting “I won the argument” as the result.
