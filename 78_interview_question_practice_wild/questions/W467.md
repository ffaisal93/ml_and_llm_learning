## Question

A common non-coding assessment for software engineers has two automated modules. In the situational-judgment module you read a workplace scenario with several possible responses and rate each response from 1 (least effective) to 5 (most effective). In the work-style module you see pairs of statements and choose which one is more like you, on a scale running between the left and right statement. Both modules measure whether your instincts match the company's leadership values, not technical skill. Explain how you would work through each module.

Questions to answer
1. Rating a review comment. A senior engineer leaves a review comment on your change that is correct but needs substantial extra work for a longer-term benefit. One possible reply is "Let's meet to discuss your concerns and recommendations"; another is "Feel free to make any changes you think are best." How do you rate each on the 1-5 scale, and which values justify the ratings?
2. Rating a blocked task. You are stuck on a task and the deadline is close. Order these responses from most to least effective and justify the order: troubleshoot first and then ask for help with a proposed plan, ship without review to hit the date, stay silent and keep trying, ask for help immediately without having investigated.
3. Consistent work-style answers. In each pair both statements sound positive, and the same trait is probed by several re-phrased pairs (planning versus adapting, checking details versus taking initiative, learning versus executing). How do you answer so that your choices stay consistent, and why is trying to guess a "correct" persona counterproductive?
4. Avoiding avoidable errors. Name two mistakes that lower the score for reasons unrelated to judgment (for example reading the direction of the 1-5 scale wrongly under time pressure) and how you prevent them.
Expected output

A short written answer (about one paragraph per question) that gives a concrete rating or ordering, the value or reasoning behind it, and the behavior it rewards.

## Worked solution

### 1. Correct review feedback

I would rate “Let's meet to discuss your concerns and recommendations” **4/5**: it takes feedback seriously and seeks shared understanding, but a better response would first summarize the issue and propose a scoped plan so the meeting has purpose. “Feel free to make any changes you think are best” is **2/5**: it is polite but abdicates ownership and shifts work to the reviewer. Exact internal scoring is unknown; the reasoning rewards learning, accountability and proportionate collaboration rather than agreement with seniority alone.

### 2. A blocked task near deadline

Most to least effective: **timebox troubleshooting, then ask with evidence/proposed plan; ask immediately without investigation; stay silent and keep trying; ship without required review**. Early help can be best when risk/time is critical, so do not interpret “troubleshoot first” as hours of silent delay. Silence hides delivery risk, while unreviewed shipping can transfer unknown harm to customers. Explain the blocker, what you tried, impact and a next step or scope/date alternative.

### 3. Consistent work style

Answer based on how you usually behave, including context, rather than guessing an ideal persona. Planning and adapting are compatible: plan reversible work, then update when evidence changes. Details and initiative also coexist through risk-based checks. Rephrased pairs expose a fabricated extreme identity and can steer you toward a role that does not fit. Read the actual pair and choose the more representative behavior; consistency comes from truthful habits, not memorized left/right answers.

### 4. Avoidable mechanics

First verify whether 1 or 5 means most effective; reversed scale reading can invert sound judgments. Second, do not accidentally rank responses when asked to rate each independently, or select the opposite end of a work-style pair after rereading. Take a short check before submission and preserve time for controls. These are reasoned practice ratings, not a claim of access to an employer's private answer key.
