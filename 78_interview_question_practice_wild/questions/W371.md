## Question

DS Power Day case round. The interviewer presents a concrete credit/lending business scenario and asks you to frame the analysis, decompose the P&L, and propose levers. Mental math is part of the round. Examples reported:

Should we offer line-of-credit increases to a specific customer segment? Forecast revenue and default impact.
A new credit product is launching in a new geography — analyse expected profitability.
A risk-model change has been proposed — what is the P&L sensitivity?

Walk through: (1) clarify the goal (revenue uplift vs default reduction vs combined profit), (2) decompose into a P&L equation, (3) identify levers, (4) do an approximate mental-math sensitivity check on the dominant lever. Do not jump to modelling — the graded signal is business decomposition first.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

The business result depends on decisions, exposure, costs and realized outcomes, not only ranking discrimination.

1. Decompose expected revenue, default loss, funding cost, operating cost and acceptance policy under explicit assumptions.
2. Compare models on time-valid cohorts and use calibration and threshold policies appropriate to the decision.
3. Assess uncertainty, delayed outcomes and relevant operational constraints; avoid presenting an unvalidated toy model as a deployment recommendation.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Write the units and driver equation before the conclusion

Separate arithmetic implied by the supplied numbers from empirical facts or predictions the prompt does not establish. A business decomposition might be

$$
\Pi=V\,(p+s-c_v)-C_f,
$$

where $V$ is visits/customers, $p$ price, $s$ other per-unit revenue, $c_v$ variable cost and $C_f$ fixed cost. State whether a “10% drop” is relative or a ten-percentage-point change in utilization; those give different $V$ values.

For valuation ratios, earnings equal market capitalization divided by a compatible P/E, but EPS additionally requires the share count. Combined earnings are not combined EPS. For forecast reactions, a lower discount rate and worse expected cash flows can push values in opposite directions; the headline alone need not identify a numerical outcome.

For estimation, propagate uncertain input ranges rather than presenting assumptions as measured current data. For a toy wager, expected payoff and outcome risk are different; no incomplete odds table determines an appropriate real stake.

Finish with a sensitivity: change the most influential uncertain input and locate the decision boundary. Explain the assumption that could reverse the answer. These are educational calculations and conditional reasoning, not a recommendation to invest, gamble or execute a transaction.

### Work a small example

A higher approval rate can raise revenue and defaults simultaneously; the net effect depends on amounts and loss severity.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Assuming a small AUC gain translates directly to the same percentage increase in profit.
