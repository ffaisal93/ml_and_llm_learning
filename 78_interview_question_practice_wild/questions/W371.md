## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">DS Power Day case round. The interviewer presents a concrete credit/lending business scenario and asks you to frame the analysis, decompose the P&amp;L, and propose levers. Mental math is part of the round. Examples reported:

Should we offer line-of-credit increases to a specific customer segment? Forecast revenue and default impact.
A new credit product is launching in a new geography — analyse expected profitability.
A risk-model change has been proposed — what is the P&amp;L sensitivity?

Walk through: (1) clarify the goal (revenue uplift vs default reduction vs combined profit), (2) decompose into a P&amp;L equation, (3) identify levers, (4) do an approximate mental-math sensitivity check on the dominant lever. Do not jump to modelling — the graded signal is business decomposition first.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Design a credit-model business evaluation without confusing accuracy and profit. The business result depends on decisions, exposure, costs and realized outcomes, not only ranking discrimination.
2. Decompose expected revenue, default loss, funding cost, operating cost and acceptance policy under explicit assumptions.
3. Compare models on time-valid cohorts and use calibration and threshold policies appropriate to the decision.
4. Assess uncertainty, delayed outcomes and relevant operational constraints; avoid presenting an unvalidated toy model as a deployment recommendation.

### Small example / sanity check

A higher approval rate can raise revenue and defaults simultaneously; the net effect depends on amounts and loss severity.

### Common trap

Assuming a small AUC gain translates directly to the same percentage increase in profit.
