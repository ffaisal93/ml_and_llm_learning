## Question

Applied Research opener. Present a project or paper of your choosing to a panel of three senior researchers. Roughly 5-10 minutes presenting, then 40-50 minutes of Q&A where each researcher follows up on a different axis. The depth probe is the entire round — high-level slide-walks fail. Coverage expectations:

Problem framing — why does this problem matter? What is the gap in prior work?
Method — derive the key equations or training objective on the spot.
Experiments — what were the ablations? What did not work? What is the minimal set of experiments that supports the claim?
Limitations — where does the method break? What failure mode did you observe?
Extensions — what would you do next with three more months?

## Worked solution

### Prepare one deep technical story

No personal project/results are supplied. This is a **fictional research proposal and rehearsal**, not a claim about the user's experience: “Does multimodal intent fusion reduce false removal of legitimate historical/news weapon imagery while retaining prohibited-sale recall?” Use the problem to demonstrate depth rather than present invented metrics as completed research.

For a 7-minute opener, spend one minute on the policy/action and costly false positives, two on data and method, two on discriminating experiments, one on failure modes and one on the next study. State your own role only when adapting this to a real project. The gap/hypothesis is explicit: object recognition alone cannot distinguish depicted objects from prohibited commerce intent; textual/contextual fusion may help, but can fail with obfuscation or missing captions.

### Derive the method at the board

Let v be an image embedding, t a caption/OCR embedding and m modality masks. Define h=MLP([v;t;m]) and p=σ(wᵀh+b). For adjudicated binary policy label y, train

$$L=-\sum_i w_i\{y_i\log p_i+(1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)\},$$

with weights correcting documented sampling, not hiding label ambiguity. For multiple policies use independent sigmoid heads and a mask for unannotated policies. The logistic derivative at a head is ∂L/∂z=w(p-y); calibration and removal thresholds are separate held-out steps. At assumed calibrated probability p, false-removal cost C_FP and missed-violation cost C_FN yield the ideal binary action threshold C_FP/(C_FP+C_FN), while review adds a third action/capacity constraint. Explain all assumptions and why weighted training may require recalibration at production prevalence.

### Minimum experiments that could support the claim

Create train/validation/test splits by time and duplicate/campaign groups, plus expert-adjudicated contextual lookalikes and random production samples. Compare image-only, text-only and fused models with matched encoder/data/compute budgets; add shuffled-caption and removed-OCR ablations. Report action precision at fixed severe-violation recall, false removals per million, calibration, subgroup slices and latency with uncertainty clustered by campaign. A win on a handpicked difficult set alone does not establish production prevalence or aggregate impact.

Fictional outcomes should be phrased conditionally: “If fusion improves overall recall but fails on captionless news imagery, I would inspect whether text availability is a selection confound and test a stronger image-only branch.” Do not invent an observed 20% gain. Track failed runs, labeling disagreements, hyperparameter budget and whether gains survive a stronger baseline.

### Prepare the panel's three axes

For the methods researcher, derive loss/gradients, dimensions, missing-modality behavior, threshold costs and why fusion should help. For the experimentalist, defend split leakage prevention, paired comparisons, prevalence, confidence intervals and matched-budget ablations. For the systems/product researcher, explain feature availability, bounded upload latency, review queue sizing, appeals, stale policy/model versions and rollback. Keep a concrete request/failure trace and error examples ready, rather than extra overview slides.

Limitations include policy ambiguity, reviewer disagreement, multilingual OCR, adversarial text, distribution shift and correlated examples. In three more months: first audit labels and establish reproducible baselines; next collect targeted/random failure data and run the minimal ablations; finally calibrate, shadow and conduct a guarded online study. Freeze manifests/code/model/policy versions and predeclare success/guardrails. The defensible claim is the one these experiments support; a proposal is not a finished result and should be presented as such.
