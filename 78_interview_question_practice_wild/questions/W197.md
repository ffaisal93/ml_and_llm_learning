## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Applied Research opener. Present a project or paper of your choosing to a panel of three senior researchers. Roughly 5-10 minutes presenting, then 40-50 minutes of Q&amp;A where each researcher follows up on a different axis. The depth probe is the entire round — high-level slide-walks fail. Coverage expectations:

Problem framing — why does this problem matter? What is the gap in prior work?
Method — derive the key equations or training objective on the spot.
Experiments — what were the ablations? What did not work? What is the minimal set of experiments that supports the claim?
Limitations — where does the method break? What failure mode did you observe?
Extensions — what would you do next with three more months?</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain your most technically complex ML project. A strong answer demonstrates decisions and evidence, not a list of technologies.
2. State the user problem, constraints and your own contribution separately from the team's work.
3. Describe the data, baseline, hardest technical failure, alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
4. Quantify the result using a valid comparison, explain limitations and say what you would change next. Use real experiences rather than invented metrics.
5. Defend a paper's contribution in a research interview. Separate the claimed contribution from the evidence that actually establishes it.
6. Restate the problem, baseline and proposed mechanism in your own words.
7. Inspect ablations, compute/data parity, uncertainty, benchmark contamination and whether improvements survive meaningful slices.
8. Propose one discriminating experiment and discuss reproduction cost and deployment constraints. Be explicit about what you have not verified.
9. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
10. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
11. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
12. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.
13. Prepare a research experiment with limited compute. Spend the budget on experiments that most clearly distinguish hypotheses.
14. State the hypothesis, expected observable effect and a simple falsifying result.
15. Choose a small faithful setting, strong baseline and controlled ablation before scaling.
16. Track compute, seeds and failures, and reserve budget for confirmation rather than spending everything on a broad unreplicated search.

### Small example / sanity check

“I reduced p95 latency from 800 to 500 ms on a matched workload while keeping task accuracy within the predefined margin” states both benefit and guardrail.

### Common trap

Saying “we built an LLM system” without explaining your choices or how success was measured.
