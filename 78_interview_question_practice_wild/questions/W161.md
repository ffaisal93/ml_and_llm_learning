## Question

Work through the following four derivations in probability and statistics. For each, state your assumptions, write the distributions, constraints and formulas first, and give an exact closed form (not a simulation or approximation).

### Requirements

1. Correlation feasibility. Three random variables x, y, z have all pairwise correlations equal to p. Find the range of p for which such variables exist, using the positive semi-definite condition on the correlation matrix (eigenvalues, determinant, or the cosine-of-angle picture). Then extend the result to n variables with a common pairwise correlation.
2. Order statistics. For n i.i.d. Uniform(0, 1) variables, compute the covariance of the maximum and the minimum, using Cov(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] and their joint density.
3. Omitted-variable bias. In the model y = b1*x1 + b2*x2 + e, x2 is left out of the regression. Derive the limit of the estimated coefficient on x1, say when the bias is nonzero and what sign it has, and prove the result in matrix form.
4. Random graph. In a complete graph on n vertices with i.i.d. continuous random edge weights, a pair (a, b) is *good* if each endpoint is the other's maximum-weight neighbor. Compute the expected number of good pairs.
Notes
Indicator variables and linearity of expectation are the natural tools for any "expected number of" question.
Check each closed form on a small case (for example n = 2 or n = 3).

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Equicorrelation: R=(1-p)I+p*11ᵀ has eigenvalues 1-p (multiplicity n-1) and 1+(n-1)p. Positive semidefiniteness gives -1/(n-1)<=p<=1; for three variables the range is [-1/2,1].
2. For min U and max V of n iid uniforms, the joint density is n(n-1)(v-u)^(n-2) on 0<u<v<1. Integration gives E[U]=1/(n+1), E[V]=n/(n+1), E[UV]=1/(n+2), hence Cov(U,V)=1/((n+1)²*(n+2)); handle n=1 directly as Var(U)=1/12.
3. Omitted-variable bias with intercept/centering and E[x1*e]=0 is b2*Cov(x1,x2)/Var(x1). In matrix form beta_hat1=beta1+(X1ᵀX1)^-1 X1ᵀX2 beta2+(X1ᵀX1)^-1 X1ᵀe; take probability limits under valid moment/rank assumptions.
4. A pair is mutually maximum exactly when its shared edge is the largest among the 2n-3 edges incident to either endpoint. IID continuous weights make each edge equally likely: probability 1/(2n-3), and expected good pairs n(n-1)/(2*(2n-3)).

### Understand why the approach works

#### Name the random experiment and condition on the right event

Write the sample space and independence assumptions before counting. Bayes' rule reverses conditioning:

$$
P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)},\qquad P(B)>0.
$$

A $90\%$ detection rate is not a $90\%$ probability that an alert is genuine. Prevalence and false-positive rate determine the latter. Work expected counts in a hypothetical population when the formula feels abstract.

For waiting-time and random-walk exercises, first-step analysis often gives a solvable recurrence:

$$
h(s)=1+\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s)h(s'),\qquad h(s)=0\text{ at absorbing states}.
$$

Specify what counts as one step: two particles moving once each may cost two coin flips but only one elapsed second. At a forced horizon, all surviving probability mass must be assigned to the final stopping time.

For expected counts, indicator variables and linearity do not require independent indicators. For uncertainty or variance, dependence does matter. Check normalization, limiting cases and a small enumeration. Simulation is useful validation but not a replacement for a requested exact derivation; conversely, a neat-looking fraction should not be asserted merely because a numerical estimate is close to it.

#### Put the result in mathematical notation

An equicorrelation matrix has eigenvalues $1-p$ and $1+(n-1)p$, hence
$$-\frac1{n-1}\le p\le1.$$
For iid uniform order statistics,
$$\operatorname{Cov}(U_{(1)},U_{(n)})=\frac1{(n+1)^2(n+2)}.$$
Omitting $x_2$ from a centered two-feature model gives coefficient bias
$$b_2\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(x_1,x_2)}{\operatorname{Var}(x_1)},$$
under the stated orthogonality/rank assumptions. A mutual-maximum edge competes with $2n-3$ incident edges, giving expected count $\binom n2/(2n-3)$. The shared edge makes the endpoint events dependent.

### Work a small example

At n=2 the order-statistics covariance is 1/36 and the graph has exactly one good pair in expectation, matching the formulas.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Assuming the two endpoint-max events are independent; they share an edge and overlapping comparisons.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Use linearity of expectation for random-graph edges.** With 10 vertices and p=0.2, there are 45 possible edges and an expected 9 realized edges.

How would degree expectations work? Sum the n-1 incident edge indicators, giving (n-1)*p under equal marginals.

**Separate correlation, prediction and causation.** Umbrella use predicts rain but making people carry umbrellas does not cause rain.

Can a noncausal predictor still be useful? Yes for prediction, subject to availability, stability and policy constraints.
