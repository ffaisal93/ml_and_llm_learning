## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Work through the following four derivations in probability and statistics. For each, state your assumptions, write the distributions, constraints and formulas first, and give an exact closed form (not a simulation or approximation).

Requirements
1.
Correlation feasibility. Three random variables x, y, z have all pairwise correlations equal to p. Find the range of p for which such variables exist, using the positive semi-definite condition on the correlation matrix (eigenvalues, determinant, or the cosine-of-angle picture). Then extend the result to n variables with a common pairwise correlation.
2.
Order statistics. For n i.i.d. Uniform(0, 1) variables, compute the covariance of the maximum and the minimum, using Cov(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y] and their joint density.
3.
Omitted-variable bias. In the model y = b1*x1 + b2*x2 + e, x2 is left out of the regression. Derive the limit of the estimated coefficient on x1, say when the bias is nonzero and what sign it has, and prove the result in matrix form.
4.
Random graph. In a complete graph on n vertices with i.i.d. continuous random edge weights, a pair (a, b) is *good* if each endpoint is the other&#x27;s maximum-weight neighbor. Compute the expected number of good pairs.
Notes
Indicator variables and linearity of expectation are the natural tools for any &quot;expected number of&quot; question.
Check each closed form on a small case (for example n = 2 or n = 3).</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Equicorrelation: R=(1-p)I+p*11ᵀ has eigenvalues 1-p (multiplicity n-1) and 1+(n-1)p. Positive semidefiniteness gives -1/(n-1)<=p<=1; for three variables the range is [-1/2,1].
2. For min U and max V of n iid uniforms, the joint density is n(n-1)(v-u)^(n-2) on 0<u<v<1. Integration gives E[U]=1/(n+1), E[V]=n/(n+1), E[UV]=1/(n+2), hence Cov(U,V)=1/((n+1)²*(n+2)); handle n=1 directly as Var(U)=1/12.
3. Omitted-variable bias with intercept/centering and E[x1*e]=0 is b2*Cov(x1,x2)/Var(x1). In matrix form beta_hat1=beta1+(X1ᵀX1)^-1 X1ᵀX2 beta2+(X1ᵀX1)^-1 X1ᵀe; take probability limits under valid moment/rank assumptions.
4. A pair is mutually maximum exactly when its shared edge is the largest among the 2n-3 edges incident to either endpoint. IID continuous weights make each edge equally likely: probability 1/(2n-3), and expected good pairs n(n-1)/(2*(2n-3)).

### Small example / sanity check

At n=2 the order-statistics covariance is 1/36 and the graph has exactly one good pair in expectation, matching the formulas.

### Common trap

Assuming the two endpoint-max events are independent; they share an edge and overlapping comparisons.
