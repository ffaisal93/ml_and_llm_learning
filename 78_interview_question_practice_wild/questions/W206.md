## Question

A no-coding HM technical screen for an ML Systems Engineer role that turns into a deep review of one recent project and its codebase. Be ready to defend your implementation, covering:
1. Overall architecture and deployment path.
2. How data is collected, cleaned, evaluated, and compared across alternatives.
3. What database / storage layer the project uses, and why that choice fits the workload.
4. Why an agentic architecture was chosen instead of a simpler pipeline.
5. Backend framework details, including FastAPI-style API boundaries and how state is carried through a LangGraph-style workflow.
6. Retrieval: how PDFs are embedded, how similarity is computed, and how the system selects useful passages/documents.
7. Failures and tradeoffs: what broke, what you changed, what you would redesign with more time.

The round may stay on a single project for the full 30 minutes.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Explain your most technically complex ML project.

A strong answer demonstrates decisions and evidence, not a list of technologies.

1. State the user problem, constraints and your own contribution separately from the team's work.
2. Describe the data, baseline, hardest technical failure, alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
3. Quantify the result using a valid comparison, explain limitations and say what you would change next. Use real experiences rather than invented metrics.

#### How do you debug a production latency regression?

Locate the changed stage and workload before rewriting the model or adding hardware.

1. Compare request mix, prompt/output lengths, concurrency and cache hit rates against the baseline period.
2. Trace queueing, preprocessing, retrieval, model execution and network time separately, including tail percentiles.
3. Check deployment changes, batching, memory pressure and retries; validate a targeted fix under matched load and keep rollback available.

#### Estimate an LLM serving capacity budget.

Capacity is constrained by memory, computation, memory traffic and arrival patterns, so one parameter count is insufficient.

1. Estimate weight bytes, KV bytes for the live-token budget, temporary buffers and runtime overhead.
2. Benchmark prefill and decode at representative lengths and concurrency; measure throughput and tail latency together.
3. Set admission limits below the measured saturation point and handle overload with bounded queues, rejection or a stated fallback.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Give evidence for a decision, not a polished but untestable story

Use an actual experience and separate your responsibility from the team's. State the problem and constraint, the alternative you considered, your action and the observable result. If you do not have a defensible metric, say what evidence you do have instead of inventing a percentage.

For a technical project, explain the data/split, baseline, hardest failure and the experiment that changed your mind. For conflict or feedback, represent the other person's concern fairly and show how you tested assumptions or changed behavior. “I won the argument” is not the project outcome.

For a research claim, distinguish mechanism from evidence. A bigger training dataset or compute budget can confound an architecture comparison. Ask which ablation separates the proposed explanation from a plausible alternative and whether uncertainty is large relative to the claimed gain.

A useful spoken structure is problem → constraint → decision → evidence → limitation → next step. Keep a longer derivation or failure trace ready for follow-up rather than hiding weak reasoning behind jargon. A motivation/timeline question needs a direct truthful answer, not a generic success story; protect confidential details and never fabricate offers or deadlines.

### Work a small example

“I reduced p95 latency from 800 to 500 ms on a matched workload while keeping task accuracy within the predefined margin” states both benefit and guardrail.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Saying “we built an LLM system” without explaining your choices or how success was measured.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**How do you debug a production latency regression?** If median latency is stable but p99 worsens, a small fraction of long requests or queueing spikes may dominate the regression.

Why can throughput improve while user latency worsens? Larger batches can increase queueing and per-request waiting.

**Estimate an LLM serving capacity budget.** Ten requests at 4000 cached tokens consume the same idealized KV tensor space as twenty at 2000, assuming identical models and no block rounding.

What happens if traffic shifts toward longer outputs? Cache residency and decode work rise even at unchanged request arrival rate.

Close the answer. State the main invariant or equation, work the example with changed inputs, and explain which assumption would make your solution fail. For a design, walk through a retry or partial failure; for a derivative, check a shape and a finite-difference case.
