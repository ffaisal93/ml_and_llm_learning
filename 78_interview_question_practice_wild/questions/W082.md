## Question

What are L1 and L2 regularization? What are the differences between them?

## Worked solution

### Both penalties discourage an unnecessarily complex fit

Given data loss $L(w)$, L1 adds $\lambda\sum_j|w_j|$; L2 commonly adds $(\lambda/2)\sum_jw_j^2$. The precise factor is convention-dependent. Usually the intercept is left unpenalized. Choose lambda on validation data, and standardize numerical features using training statistics so feature units do not arbitrarily decide which coefficient is expensive.

L2 has gradient $\lambda w$: large coefficients receive stronger shrinkage. It usually keeps coefficients nonzero and spreads weight among correlated predictors. For linear regression it changes the normal matrix from $X^\top X$ to $X^\top X+\lambda I$, improving conditioning for penalized coefficients.

L1 has a corner at zero. Its subgradient is the sign away from zero and any value in `[-1,1]` at zero. This permits exact zero coefficients at the optimum, giving sparse feature selection. Ordinary gradient descent with an arbitrary derivative at zero does not by itself guarantee exact sparsity; coordinate descent or proximal updates are common.

### A scalar example makes the difference clear

Minimize $\tfrac12(w-z)^2$ plus a penalty. For L2, $w^*=z/(1+\lambda)$. For L1,

$$w^*=\operatorname{sign}(z)\max(|z|-\lambda,0).$$

With $z=0.4$ and lambda 0.5, L2 gives about 0.267 while L1 gives zero. With two nearly identical predictors, L1 may select either unpredictably; L2 tends to share their contribution. Sparse does not necessarily mean a stable causal explanation.

| Property | L1 | L2 |
|---|---|---|
| Typical coefficients | Some exactly zero | Mostly shrunk, nonzero |
| Correlated features | May select one unstably | Often shares weight |
| Main use | Sparse models/feature selection | Stable shrinkage/conditioning |
| Smoothness | Nondifferentiable at zero | Differentiable |

Both can reduce variance at the cost of additional bias. Too large a penalty underfits. Elastic net combines them when correlated groups and sparsity both matter. For adaptive neural-network optimizers, decoupled weight decay is not generally identical to adding L2 to the loss; specify which update is intended rather than treating the terms as universally interchangeable.
