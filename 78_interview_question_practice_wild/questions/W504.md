## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Market-Making Interval Estimation Game

A QR/QT interview game: across five rounds the interviewer asks a numeric estimation question and you respond with a closed interval [L, U]. You score 0 if the true answer falls outside your interval and L/U if it falls inside — so tighter honest intervals score higher. The target is reaching at least 2.0 total points.

Requirements
Five rounds; the goal is to accumulate at least 2.0 total points.
Each round: the interviewer poses a numeric question and you report a closed interval [L, U] you believe contains the answer.
Scoring: if the true answer is outside [L, U], you score 0 for that round; if inside, you score L/U. A wider interval is safer but scores less; a tighter interval scores more but risks missing.
Examples
True answer = 16: reporting [20, 40] scores 0 (16 is outside); reporting [10, 30] scores 10/30 ≈ 0.33.
Notes
The game directly tests calibrated estimation: you must balance the probability of containment against the L/U reward, which pushes you to the tightest interval you still trust.
It is typically preceded by the classic HR &quot;eight big questions&quot; round (English/communication screen) and followed by a structured-problem (SPD) round.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. For an abstract wager with win probability p and net payoff b per unit staked, expected profit per unit is p*b-(1-p); distinguish net odds from total payout. Shared outcomes make bets correlated, so summing expected profits does not establish low risk.
2. For interval scoring, assume 0<L<=U and a positive target. Expected score is P(L<=X<=U)*(L/U); optimize that under an explicit predictive distribution rather than always widening or narrowing. Nonpositive intervals require a clarified scoring rule.
3. For price quotes, define the uncertain payoff, information available and transaction/inventory constraints. Report uncertainty and avoid treating one realized gain/loss as proof of a good/bad expected-value calculation.
4. The prompt supplies no concrete per-round probabilities/odds, so there is no unique numerical stake or interval. Practice with explicitly hypothetical distributions and bounded exposure; “guaranteed” arbitrage requires every relevant execution/counterparty assumption to hold.

### Small example / sanity check

If p=0.6 and net odds b=1, expected unit profit is 0.2, but a losing outcome remains possible. For a true value 16, interval [10,30] scores 1/3 and [20,40] scores zero.

### Common trap

Recommending an all-in real stake from a toy expected-value number, or confusing a tight interval’s conditional score with its expected score.
