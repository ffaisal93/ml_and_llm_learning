## Question

QR Data Analysis Prediction Case

Open-ended data-analysis round: define features, targets, models, and evaluation for a prediction problem such as housing prices, CitiBike demand, rental price, stock price from search/social data, or alpha testing.

### Requirements

You receive a prediction problem and must reason through the full modeling plan. Reported domains include:

Predict housing prices from historical data.
Predict CitiBike demand or station inventory at a specific location.
Predict rental price.
Use six years of Google search data with timestamp, device id, location, query, and URL to predict a company's stock price at arbitrary times.
Use Twitter / social data to predict stock price.
Test whether a momentum alpha works.

Interviewers probe:

Feature and outcome definition.
Model choice: linear regression, random forest, nonlinear alternatives, and why each may work.
Missing values, outliers, data cleaning, and special shocks such as COVID.
Nonlinear relationships, heteroskedasticity, multicollinearity, seasonality, data drift, and variance drift.
Data leakage and overfitting.
Train / validation / test split.
Evaluation metrics such as MSE, MAE, accuracy, ROC-AUC, or domain-specific performance metrics.
Notes
This is not a coding-heavy round. It is a structured statistical reasoning interview.
Candidates who only give high-level ML names struggle; the interviewer asks for assumptions, failure modes, and exact evaluation logic.
Several candidates observe that traditional statistics and linear-model reasoning are valued more than generic ML framing.

## Worked solution

### Define a forecast at an information cutoff

The domains are alternatives, not one interchangeable target. No actual dataset is supplied. For housing/rent, define sale/monthly rent known after a specified listing date, currency, geography and property unit; use log-price if proportional errors matter, comparing median/linear models to trees. Include size, age, location and information available at listing, not future sale negotiations. Group repeated properties and use future-time/geographic holdouts. MAE is interpretable in dollars; RMSE emphasizes expensive misses, and log metrics require honest back-transformation.

For CitiBike demand, predict arrivals/departures for station s over the next chosen 30 minutes, using counts/lags, hour/day/holiday, nearby stations and weather **forecast** available now. Count models or boosted trees with lagged seasonal baselines can handle nonlinearities; use Poisson deviance/MAE and operational empty/full-station cost. Observed rentals are censored when the station is empty; actual unconstrained demand is a different target. Predicting inventory also needs current stock, capacity, arrivals and rebalancing, with bounded outputs.

### Search/social to returns: timestamp discipline matters

Define a future log return $r_{t,h}=\log(P_{t+h}/P_t)$ from an executable decision time, rather than an undefined “stock price at arbitrary times.” Aggregate queries/URLs/posts by availability timestamp into lagged volume, growth, topic/sentiment and geography/device mix; normalize against total observed traffic to reduce changing platform volume. Deduplicate bots/reposts, protect identifiers, and control publisher/retrieval lag. Prices at closed markets or unavailable snapshots need an explicit carry-forward/trading-calendar policy. Six years is a limited number of regimes, not six years of independent per-device samples.

Use a zero-return/market benchmark and regularized linear return model first; include market/sector and lagged volatility controls. A nonlinear model is justified only by stable walk-forward gains. Avoid contemporaneous search spikes caused by a price move being labeled as predictive before that move. Revised search datasets, survivorship-selected companies and post-hoc chosen hashtags leak information even with a chronological split.

### Statistics, cleaning and honest comparison

Audit missingness, duplicates, units, time zones and impossible values; fit imputation and any clipping thresholds within each training period. Retain genuine shocks such as COVID as regimes, report pre/during/post performance and stress results; deleting them merely for high residuals hides risk. Use splines/interactions for nonlinear relationships, ridge/SVD for multicollinearity, residual-versus-fit/time plots for heteroskedasticity and volatility shifts, and robust/cluster/HAC uncertainty when justified. Correlated predictors can leave predictions stable while coefficients vary; a coefficient is not a causal effect.

Walk forward using expanding or rolling training windows, a later validation block, and an untouched final period. Purge training labels whose forward outcome windows overlap validation/test and account for publication lag. Tune only inside the chronological training procedure. Report MAE/MSE/out-of-sample R² for continuous outcomes, classification accuracy/ROC/PR only for an explicitly discretized direction/event target, and per-regime stability. Report confidence intervals using time blocks rather than treating high-frequency rows as independent.

### Momentum alpha is an economic test

Specify lookback, skip period, holding horizon, tradable universe, rebalance time and position rule before testing. Form lagged momentum using prices available at formation; compare future excess returns to zero and risk-factor benchmarks. Report out-of-sample information/Sharpe ratio, drawdown, turnover, exposure and **net** returns after transaction costs, spread, slippage and borrow/market-impact assumptions. Correct for trying many variants and inspect capacity/period sensitivity. A predictive t-statistic or an attractive backtest is insufficient if fees erase the edge. A defensible recommendation states exactly where the signal survives and what additional prospective data would change the decision.
