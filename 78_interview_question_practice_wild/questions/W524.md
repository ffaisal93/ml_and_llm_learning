## Question

A set of nine general interview-preparation questions for job candidates (new-grad / intern leaning). Several questions reference the job description (JD) and the Dallas job market, so answers are expected to be tailored to the specific role and location.

1. Why should we hire you?

Please explain in detail your skills and experiences and how they align with the requirements of the candidate we are looking for. Make sure to provide specific examples of your past achievements and how you can add value to this position by referring to the job description (JD).

2. Describe a course or project you took part in and how it relates to this position.

Please introduce a course you took or a project you participated in, and how it prepared you for this position. Provide specific details, such as the objectives of the project, your particular involvement, and the outcomes.

3. Tell me about a failure you've experienced and how you dealt with it.

Please share a failure experience from your career or academic life, describe how you handled and faced this failure, and what lessons you learned from it.

4. What do you know about the Dallas job market?

Please introduce your understanding of the Dallas job market and your enthusiasm for working in this area. You might need to conduct some market research in advance to provide some data or facts supporting your viewpoint.

5. If we hire you, what kind of work would you like to do?

Please state the main work content you expect to engage in and the experiences and growth you hope to gain from this position. Elaborate based on the job description when answering.

6. When does your summer internship start and end?

Please provide the specific timeline for your summer internship and explain whether you can begin employment with our company on time.

7. When do you graduate, and are you planning to advance your education or enter the industry?

Please specify your graduation date and whether your future plan is to pursue further studies or enter the workforce.

8. What school are you currently attending?

Please provide the name of the school you are currently attending.

9. What skills do you have?

Please list your main skills, especially those related to the applied position, and provide relevant experience or certificates to support your statements.

Example
Input
Why should we hire you?

## Worked solution

### Tailor all nine answers to facts you can defend

The JD, your school, graduation date and availability are missing. Do not fill them with invented credentials. Prepare the following nine answers using your own facts; this is an answer worksheet with specific content requirements, not a fictional candidate biography.

1. **Why hire you?** Select two or three responsibilities from the JD. For each, give a demonstrated skill, one example and its result. Finish with what you could contribute first. For example: “The role needs [requirement]. In [real project] I personally [action], verified by [evidence]. That prepares me to [role contribution].” Avoid repeating the entire resume or promising expertise you lack.
2. **Course/project relevance.** State the problem, data/users, your individual role, technical decision and outcome. Explain one direct transfer to the role and one limitation. A team project's success must not be described as if you built every component.
3. **Failure.** Give a genuine decision or assumption that failed, its consequence, what you did to repair it and the prevention you now use. A disguised strength such as “I care too much” does not show accountability. Distinguish a lesson from a measurable result.
4. **Dallas market.** Use one dated local fact and connect it to this employer/role; do not equate a large labor market with guaranteed hiring. For research, examine the [BLS Dallas–Fort Worth data tables](https://www.bls.gov/regions/southwest/data/employmentandunemploymentandwages_dallasfortworth_table.htm), selecting the exact metro, reference month, sector and seasonal-adjustment definition. Record those fields with any number you quote, then explain your genuine location preference. No unsupported current growth claim is needed for a good answer.
5. **Work you want.** Name the role's actual outputs and a learning goal: “I would like to contribute to [responsibility], beginning with [realistic task], while developing [skill].” Do not ask for a different job hidden inside this one.
6. **Internship dates.** Give exact start/end dates, any exam/travel/work-authorization constraints and whether the published program dates fit. If a date is uncertain, state when you will know. Do not promise availability you cannot honor.
7. **Graduation/plans.** State month/year and your actual industry/further-study plan, including any uncertainty relevant to employment. An aspirational plan is not an accepted enrollment.
8. **Current school.** Give the official institution, degree/major and expected completion date. Add only one relevant academic detail if asked.
9. **Skills.** Group the role-relevant skills into demonstrated strengths, working familiarity and current learning. Attach a project, assessment or certificate to each important claim; a certificate alone does not prove production experience.

### Make the set coherent

Use the same dates, role ownership and results across all answers. Prepare thirty-second screening versions and deeper project detail. Verify any local-market fact near the interview, since this study page is meant to remain useful offline and current labor-market numbers change. A recruiter should finish knowing what you can do, why this role/location fits and when you can start.
