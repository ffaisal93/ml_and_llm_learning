## Question

Key Financial Ratios for Technology Company Analysis

What are the most important ratios to look out for in a technology company?

Example

### Input

```text
Company: XYZ Tech, P/E Ratio = 30, Revenue Growth = 25%, Gross Margin = 60%
```

## Worked solution

### Choose ratios for the business model and life stage

A profitable mature software company and a capital-intensive hardware startup need different comparisons. Begin with growth, unit economics, cash generation, balance-sheet resilience and valuation; use peer groups with similar recurring revenue, margins and reinvestment needs.

| Area | Useful measure | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Revenue growth; recurring-revenue growth where applicable | Organic versus acquisition/FX effects; customer concentration |
| Product economics | Gross profit/revenue | Hosting/hardware/content costs and consistent classification |
| Operating leverage | Operating profit/revenue | Whether sales/R&D spending creates durable future growth |
| Cash | Free cash flow/revenue; cash runway for loss-making firms | Working-capital timing, capex and stock-compensation dilution |
| Recurring revenue | Net revenue retention, churn, CAC payback | Cohort definitions and gross-margin-adjusted acquisition recovery |
| Capital structure | Net debt, liquidity and coverage | Debt maturities, restricted cash and financing needs |
| Valuation | P/E, EV/revenue, EV/operating profit or FCF yield | Profitability, growth durability, accounting and enterprise/equity consistency |

### Interpret the given company

P/E 30 means the equity market value is thirty times the specified annual earnings, equivalent to an earnings yield near 3.33%. Ask whether earnings are trailing or forward, GAAP or adjusted, and whether one-off items distort them. A 25% revenue growth rate is promising only if durable and economically valuable. A 60% gross margin leaves 60 cents per revenue dollar before operating expenses, interest and tax; it is not a 60% net margin.

Do not divide P/E by revenue growth and label the result PEG: the conventional PEG uses earnings growth and still has substantial limitations. P/E may be meaningless for negative or very small earnings, making enterprise-value multiples and a cash-flow valuation more useful. EV/revenue comparisons without margin differences can be equally misleading.

For a subscription business, examine retention, efficient acquisition and whether cash generation depends on annual prepayments. For hardware, emphasize inventory turns, working capital, capex and supply-chain exposure. Adjust any non-GAAP reconciliation transparently and inspect share-count growth. The three supplied figures alone do not establish whether XYZ is cheap, expensive or financially healthy; they start a comparison whose definitions and risks must be explicit.
