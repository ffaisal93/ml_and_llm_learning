## Question

Key Financial Ratios for Technology Company Analysis

What are the most important ratios to look out for in a technology company?

Example

### Input

```text
Company: XYZ Tech, P/E Ratio = 30, Revenue Growth = 25%, Gross Margin = 60%
```

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Write a driver equation before selecting a narrative: revenue=customers*usage*price; operating profit=revenue-variable costs-fixed costs. Separate total market size from obtainable share and distinguish accounting profit from cash generation.
2. For valuation, estimate standalone cash flows, growth, reinvestment and discount/risk assumptions. Synergy is combined value minus standalone values, net of integration costs; a purchase premium is a negotiated allocation of value, not automatically the same number.
3. Choose ratios that fit the business stage: gross margin, operating/free-cash-flow margin, growth, retention/unit economics and valuation multiples with compatible denominators. P/E is unhelpful when earnings are negative or distorted.
4. Run base/upside/downside sensitivities and identify the dominant driver. No actual business, current financial statements or discount assumptions are supplied for the open-ended pitch, so do not invent a verified investment recommendation.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Write the units and driver equation before the conclusion

Separate arithmetic implied by the supplied numbers from empirical facts or predictions the prompt does not establish. A business decomposition might be

$$
\Pi=V\,(p+s-c_v)-C_f,
$$

where $V$ is visits/customers, $p$ price, $s$ other per-unit revenue, $c_v$ variable cost and $C_f$ fixed cost. State whether a “10% drop” is relative or a ten-percentage-point change in utilization; those give different $V$ values.

For valuation ratios, earnings equal market capitalization divided by a compatible P/E, but EPS additionally requires the share count. Combined earnings are not combined EPS. For forecast reactions, a lower discount rate and worse expected cash flows can push values in opposite directions; the headline alone need not identify a numerical outcome.

For estimation, propagate uncertain input ranges rather than presenting assumptions as measured current data. For a toy wager, expected payoff and outcome risk are different; no incomplete odds table determines an appropriate real stake.

Finish with a sensitivity: change the most influential uncertain input and locate the decision boundary. Explain the assumption that could reverse the answer. These are educational calculations and conditional reasoning, not a recommendation to invest, gamble or execute a transaction.

### Work a small example

The synergy example gives 130B-50B-70B=10B before integration costs. A park with capacity 5000, 200 days and 70% utilization has 700000 visits/year; profit=visits*(ticket+in-park spend-variable cost per visit)-fixed cost. A 10-percentage-point utilization drop means 600000 visits, unlike a 10% relative drop to 63% utilization.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Equating revenue growth with value creation or using made-up current company figures as evidence.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Design a credit-model business evaluation without confusing accuracy and profit.** A higher approval rate can raise revenue and defaults simultaneously; the net effect depends on amounts and loss severity.

Why evaluate policy and model together? Changing the threshold changes who is accepted and which outcomes become observable.

**Separate correlation, prediction and causation.** Umbrella use predicts rain but making people carry umbrellas does not cause rain.

Can a noncausal predictor still be useful? Yes for prediction, subject to availability, stability and policy constraints.
