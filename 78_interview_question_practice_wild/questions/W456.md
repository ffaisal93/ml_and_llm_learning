## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Atlassian&#x27;s later-stage behavioral loop is usually split into a values round and a management / hiring-manager round. Questions repeatedly target mentorship, helping teammates, feedback, ownership, ambiguity, sudden changes, mistakes, and alignment with Atlassian&#x27;s five values.

Requirements
Prepare for a values-focused round and a management / hiring-manager round.
Values prompts include: Have you been a mentor or mentee? Have you helped a team member improve? What does an effective team look like to you? How did you handle feedback? How did you handle a case where you missed the mark?
Management prompts include: Describe a case where you owned the outcome. How did you handle unclear requirements? How did you handle sudden changes (leadership or direction)? Have you helped a teammate become better?
Other repeated themes: pushback, change and improvement, ambiguity, mistakes and recovery, role clarity, and learning.
Notes
The management round can carry heavy leveling weight; candidates have been downleveled even when technical rounds were stronger.
Interviewers keep probing until they get the signal they need. Answer with role, action, conflict, impact, and learning rather than a generic story.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. How do you handle disagreement over an ML design? Make the disputed assumptions testable and connect the decision to the shared objective.
2. Restate the other person's concern fairly and identify whether the disagreement concerns facts, constraints or preferences.
3. Propose a small experiment or decision table with agreed success criteria, cost and deadline.
4. Document the decision, support execution after it is made and revisit only when meaningful new evidence appears.
5. Explain a project failure constructively. Show ownership, evidence and changed behavior, not a story in which someone else is always at fault.
6. State the goal, your responsibility and what failed in observable terms.
7. Explain which assumption or decision was wrong and how you detected it.
8. Describe recovery, what changed afterward and whether the fix actually helped. Keep confidential details appropriately abstract.
9. Handle harsh technical feedback. Separate the useful content of feedback from its delivery and verify what needs to change.
10. Listen, summarize the specific concern and ask for concrete examples when necessary.
11. Check the evidence, acknowledge valid mistakes and propose a bounded correction with a follow-up date.
12. If disagreement remains, explain it respectfully using the shared goal and evidence, and seek the appropriate resolution channel.

### Apply the exact contract

Prepare separate real examples for conflict, failure, leadership, inclusion and feedback. Use situation→your responsibility→specific actions→measured result→lesson. A video round needs concise complete responses; a values round needs evidence of behavior, not a memorized list of slogans. When a prompt asks several questions, do not reuse one anecdote without answering the different decisions it probes.

### Small example / sanity check

A debate over retrieval complexity can become a measured comparison of answer quality, p95 latency and maintenance cost on a fixed query set.

### Common trap

Describing a colleague as incompetent or presenting “I won the argument” as the result.
