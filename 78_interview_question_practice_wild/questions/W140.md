## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Research-track take-home. You get a self-contained ML problem to solve in ~4 hours in your own environment (local IDE or Colab), immediately followed by a 40-minute live review where you present results and field questions.

Recruiter guidance: &quot;It&#x27;s mostly coding work, but pen and paper could be useful. The problem involves exploratory research, obtaining results, and showing them numerically. You should be comfortable training and evaluating basic models, with some knowledge of ML and visualization libraries (pandas, numpy, matplotlib...). Treat it like an open-book exam: look up documentation, but don&#x27;t look up the whole problem, and ensure all work is your own.&quot; Open-book on docs; no AI tools. The materials are sent at the start of the window, so you cannot prep on the actual problem.

How should you approach this to maximize your score?</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Plan a four-hour ML take-home exercise. Budget the work so a reproducible baseline and honest evaluation exist before optional improvements.
2. Spend an initial block clarifying the target, split, metric, data quality and a minimal runnable pipeline.
3. Build and evaluate a baseline, then run one or two prioritized improvements with tracked seeds and configurations.
4. Reserve time for error analysis, clean reruns and a short write-up of assumptions, results and remaining limitations.
5. Fit a baseline on messy tabular data without leakage. Build a trustworthy split and simple baseline before sophisticated feature engineering.
6. Define the target, prediction time and unit of independence, then split by time or group if required.
7. Fit imputation, scaling and category encodings on training folds only. Start with a dummy baseline and a regularized linear or small tree model.
8. Evaluate slices, missingness patterns and calibration; document features unavailable at decision time and remove them.

### Small example / sanity check

A simple model with a valid temporal split and reproducible commands is stronger evidence than an elaborate model evaluated on leaked data.

### Common trap

Using the final hour for one unvalidated architecture change and leaving no runnable result.
