## Question

Short-Run Stock Price Impact of Merger Announcement

Suppose A announces its merger with B. How will the stock prices of A and B change in the very short run?

Example

### Input

```text
Company_A_SharePrice = 100, Company_B_SharePrice = 50
```

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Separate an unexpected news component from information already in prices. State the valuation channel and what else the announcement reveals; the prompt does not determine a unique numerical price change.
2. For a cash acquisition, the target often moves toward expected deal proceeds discounted for time, financing and failure risk; the acquirer’s reaction depends on premium, synergies, financing and signaling. A stock deal links target value to the exchange ratio and acquirer price.
3. For a surprise rate cut, a pure lower-discount-rate effect raises fixed cash-flow present value, but news about growth, credit risk, currencies and future policy can reverse some reactions. A policy-rate change is not automatically an equal shift in every yield.
4. Work a conditional numerical scenario, then explain which assumption would change its sign. This is an educational mechanism exercise, not a trading recommendation.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Write the units and driver equation before the conclusion

Separate arithmetic implied by the supplied numbers from empirical facts or predictions the prompt does not establish. A business decomposition might be

$$
\Pi=V\,(p+s-c_v)-C_f,
$$

where $V$ is visits/customers, $p$ price, $s$ other per-unit revenue, $c_v$ variable cost and $C_f$ fixed cost. State whether a “10% drop” is relative or a ten-percentage-point change in utilization; those give different $V$ values.

For valuation ratios, earnings equal market capitalization divided by a compatible P/E, but EPS additionally requires the share count. Combined earnings are not combined EPS. For forecast reactions, a lower discount rate and worse expected cash flows can push values in opposite directions; the headline alone need not identify a numerical outcome.

For estimation, propagate uncertain input ranges rather than presenting assumptions as measured current data. For a toy wager, expected payoff and outcome risk are different; no incomplete odds table determines an appropriate real stake.

Finish with a sensitivity: change the most influential uncertain input and locate the decision boundary. Explain the assumption that could reverse the answer. These are educational calculations and conditional reasoning, not a recommendation to invest, gamble or execute a transaction.

### Work a small example

With an assumed parallel yield drop of 0.005 and modified duration 5, a bond’s first-order price change is about +2.5%. With an assumed cash offer 60, failure value 45 and close probability 0.8, undiscounted expected target value is 57; neither assumption is supplied by the two current share prices alone.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Giving deterministic stock moves or a unique price from incomplete deal/risk information.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Separate correlation, prediction and causation.** Umbrella use predicts rain but making people carry umbrellas does not cause rain.

Can a noncausal predictor still be useful? Yes for prediction, subject to availability, stability and policy constraints.

**Design a Monte Carlo estimate and quantify its error.** Reducing Monte Carlo standard error by a factor of two generally requires four times as many independent samples.

What if the distribution has infinite variance? Ordinary square-root-N error reasoning may fail.
