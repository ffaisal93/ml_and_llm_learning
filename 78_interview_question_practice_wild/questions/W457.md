## Question

Lyft behavioral rounds are standard but broad. Candidates are asked for many short examples across conflict, communication, feedback, failure, decisions, project challenges, and sometimes diversity support.

### Requirements

Prepare examples for conflict or disagreement with a teammate.
Prepare an example of receiving feedback and changing behavior.
Prepare a failed project or difficult time and what you learned.
Prepare a challenging project and how you handled ambiguity.
Prepare a decision-making story where trade-offs mattered.
Prepare a diversity/supporting-inclusion story for senior loops.
Be ready for project-plus-BQ hybrids where the interviewer asks about resume projects and then pivots into behavioral follow-ups.
Notes
The senior behavioral round can ask a sequence of unrelated "tell me about a time" questions rather than drilling one project deeply.
Some BQ rounds feel conversational; others are serious and structured.
For frontend and intern loops, manager rounds stay close to normal collaboration, disagreement, and feedback themes.
Lyft-facing stories should emphasize collaboration, learning, accountability, and inclusion. For DEI prompts, prepare a concrete example where you changed a process, created access, or improved team participation rather than giving a generic values statement.

## Worked solution

### Prepare six short examples, not one stretched story

No personal history is given. Use these **illustrative situations** to identify real counterparts, each with your role, action, result and reflection:

- **Conflict:** a peer wants direct database reads while you favor a read model. Agree freshness/latency goals, test one failure/load case and choose the simpler option that meets them. Represent the peer's operational-cost concern fairly.
- **Feedback:** a colleague says your design reviews are hard to enter. Send a short decision memo beforehand, invite written concerns and measure whether unresolved questions surface earlier.
- **Failure:** a launch missed a partner approval dependency. Communicate early, offer a narrower scope and add explicit dependency checkpoints; own the planning assumption.
- **Ambiguous project:** turn “make search better” into a labeled query set, baseline relevance/latency, two candidate changes and a phased experiment. Explain which uncertainty you resolved first.
- **Trade-off:** defer personalization to ship reliable access filtering and rollback. Describe the cost of the deferred benefit as well as the risk you reduced.
- **Inclusion:** change a meeting-only decision process to an accessible written pre-read plus rotating facilitation, solicit input across timezones and check whether previously unheard concerns enter decisions. Do not claim to speak for a colleague's identity or reveal private circumstances.

In a real interview, replace the examples with facts and actual evidence; no fabricated impact numbers are required. For senior scope, explain how another team's behavior or the decision process changed, not merely that you attended meetings. A project/BQ hybrid can reuse a concrete event from a resume project, but be ready with unrelated examples when requested. Keep the initial answer under two minutes and reserve implementation or interpersonal detail for follow-up.
