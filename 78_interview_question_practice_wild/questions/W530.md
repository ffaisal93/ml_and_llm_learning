## Question

Why did you choose to apply for this company? Please explain your reasons in detail, and include your personal views related to the company's background, culture, or products.

## Worked solution

### Make the answer specific enough that another company's name would not fit

Use three connected points: a product/customer problem you understand, a responsibility that fits your demonstrated skills, and a working environment you have evidence to value. The company and role are not specified here, so supply those facts before writing a final first-person answer.

### Response structure

“I applied because [specific product or customer problem] is work I want to understand deeply. When I [real product use, research or conversation], I noticed [concrete observation], which made [challenge] interesting to me.

“The role's focus on [actual JD responsibility] fits my experience with [real project and your contribution]. I would hope to contribute by [credible early responsibility], while learning more about [relevant gap].

“I also value [genuine working preference]. From [verifiable interaction or published practice], I see a possible fit, and I would like to understand how the team handles [specific question].”

### Build and verify the evidence

Read the actual product/user flow and JD, then prepare one observation beyond the homepage slogan. If you use a company announcement, note its date and distinguish a plan from a completed launch. Do not state that you know the internal culture from marketing copy; explain what you observed and what you still want to ask.

For an ML role, connect your interest to the specific problem—relevance, trustworthy evaluation, inference cost or data quality—rather than saying only that the company “uses AI.” Discuss one trade-off a real team would face. Avoid unverifiable superlatives and invented personal connections. A good answer can acknowledge a learning goal without pretending immediate expertise in every system.

Keep the main version around sixty to ninety seconds. Be ready for “Why this team rather than a competitor?” and “Which part of the role would be hardest for you?” Your motivation should remain consistent with the projects and priorities you discuss elsewhere in the interview.
