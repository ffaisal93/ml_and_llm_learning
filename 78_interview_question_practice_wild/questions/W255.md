## Question

Data Engineer / Cell Integrity & Quality case: five randomly assigned battery groups each undergo three pass/fail tests; identify which group has the best overall quality and justify the statistical method.

### Requirements

There are five groups of batteries.
Batteries are randomly assigned to the five groups.
Each battery goes through three different inspections or tests.
Each test result is pass or fail.
The data has already been stored.
Determine which battery group has the best overall quality.
Notes
Treat this as an experiment-design and statistical-comparison case, not a pure SQL exercise.
Clarify sample sizes, whether the three tests have equal importance, whether failures are independent, and whether a battery-level pass requires all three tests to pass.
Reasonable approaches include group-level pass-rate estimates with confidence intervals, multiple-comparison-aware hypothesis testing, and a composite quality score if the tests have different severity.
If each battery has three binary outcomes, define the unit of analysis before ranking: per-test pass rate, all-tests-pass battery rate, or weighted defect severity score can produce different winners.

## Worked solution

### Define quality at the battery level

Actual counts/results are not supplied, so no numerical winning group can be identified. Ask sample sizes, missing-test rates, randomization unit, severity of each failure and whether every battery truly received all three tests. Assume overall quality means all three pass. For battery i define Yi=I(test1=test2=test3=PASS). Estimate p_g=mean(Yi in group g), with a binomial/Wilson confidence interval. Do not multiply three marginal pass rates unless within-battery independence is justified; the tests share a battery and can be correlated.

Store/use Battery(id,assigned_group,assignment_time) and Inspection(battery,test_id,result,measured_at), unique by battery/test/version with correction provenance. Produce one complete battery row before aggregation. Report missing outcomes by group; investigate differential missingness and give sensitivity bounds, rather than silently dropping defective batteries unable to finish testing. Random assignment supports a causal comparison of group **treatments** only if there is an actual differing treatment and assignment/compliance is sound; random partitions receiving identical treatment may show only sampling noise.

### Comparison and decision

Predeclare a practically meaningful pass-rate margin δ and the primary endpoint. Use a five-group binomial/logistic model or randomization/permutation test on battery-level outcomes for an omnibus equality test. If inspecting pairwise winners, there are ten comparisons; use Holm-adjusted tests/intervals or a planned best-versus-control procedure, accounting for selecting the largest observed rate. Include effect sizes and uncertainty. A largest estimate can be called the observed leader without proving it is uniquely best. If differences are within uncertainty, report insufficient evidence and plan more samples with power based on δ, baseline rate and multiplicity.

Illustration only: group A has 9/10 all-pass batteries and B 880/1,000. A's 90% is larger than B's 88%, but its Wilson 95% interval is roughly 60%–98%, so this does not establish superiority. The remaining groups' results and pairwise uncertainty are still required. This example is not the case's dataset or declared winner.

If failures have unequal severity, define a preapproved weighted defect cost Li=Σw_j I(fail_ij) and compare mean loss (lower is better) with battery-level bootstrap/randomization, while still reporting every test and catastrophic-defect rate. A composite can hide a dangerous failure, so enforce critical-test limits separately. Validate the aggregation on hand-checked batteries, duplicated inspection records and missing tests; check randomization balance, blinded inspection and correction logs. The output should name the endpoint, denominators, estimates/intervals, adjusted comparisons and whether any group is convincingly best, not merely return the group with MAX(pass_rate).
