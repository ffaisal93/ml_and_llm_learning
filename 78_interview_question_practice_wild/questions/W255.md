## Question

Data Engineer / Cell Integrity & Quality case: five randomly assigned battery groups each undergo three pass/fail tests; identify which group has the best overall quality and justify the statistical method.

### Requirements

There are five groups of batteries.
Batteries are randomly assigned to the five groups.
Each battery goes through three different inspections or tests.
Each test result is pass or fail.
The data has already been stored.
Determine which battery group has the best overall quality.
Notes
Treat this as an experiment-design and statistical-comparison case, not a pure SQL exercise.
Clarify sample sizes, whether the three tests have equal importance, whether failures are independent, and whether a battery-level pass requires all three tests to pass.
Reasonable approaches include group-level pass-rate estimates with confidence intervals, multiple-comparison-aware hypothesis testing, and a composite quality score if the tests have different severity.
If each battery has three binary outcomes, define the unit of analysis before ranking: per-test pass rate, all-tests-pass battery rate, or weighted defect severity score can produce different winners.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

1. Choose the battery as the analysis unit and define quality before inspecting winners: all-three-tests pass, per-test rates or a predeclared severity-weighted score are different objectives.
2. Compute each group’s sample size, missingness and outcome distribution. Estimate battery-level pass rates with uncertainty; retain the correlation among three outcomes from the same battery.
3. Compare randomized groups under a suitable binomial/logistic or permutation approach, accounting for five-group multiple comparisons and any predeclared covariates. Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only the largest sample percentage.
4. If sample sizes or actual outcomes are absent, no numerical winning group is identifiable. State what data would establish a practically meaningful advantage and how missing outcomes are handled.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Establish what the number actually estimates

Write the population, unit of analysis, outcome window and denominator next to the metric. A per-user mean and a per-impression mean answer different questions. A global mean over unequal batches is

$$
\bar\ell=\frac{\sum_b\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i}{\sum_b n_b},
$$

not generally the unweighted mean of batch means. Workers should reduce numerators and denominators separately when reporting that metric.

Use a comparison with matched examples, mature labels and unchanged definitions. For uncertainty, resample independent units; repeated events within a user or prompt are not automatically independent. A confidence interval measures sampling uncertainty under assumptions, not protection against leaked data or an inappropriate target.

Calibration asks whether predictions around probability $p$ are correct about fraction $p$ of the time. It is not the same as accuracy. A reliability plot should show bin populations, and a calibration-error formula must say whether bins or examples receive equal weight.

When quality regresses, trace data availability, feature freshness, label delays and cohort mix before automatically retraining. For example, a fraud model may look worse on immature labels even when its true behavior has not changed. Report missing/time-out outputs explicitly: dropping them from the denominator can turn an unreliable system into an apparently perfect one.

### Work a small example

A group with 9/10 all-pass batteries has a higher point estimate but much wider uncertainty than a group with 880/1000; ranking by percentage alone hides that difference.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Multiplying the three marginal pass rates as if the tests on a battery were independent.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Estimate a confidence interval for a paired model comparison.** If the same difficult examples fool both models, treating the two accuracy estimates as independent discards useful covariance information.

What if the final metric is nonlinear? Recompute that metric on each paired bootstrap sample rather than averaging inappropriate per-example surrogates.

**Interpret a confidence interval that includes zero.** An estimated lift of 0.2 percentage points with interval [-0.1,0.5] allows both a small loss and a useful gain.

How do you establish equivalence? Use a prespecified equivalence margin and an appropriate test or interval decision rule.
