## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">You are joining a company that builds AI software for commercial insurance underwriters. Explain how commercial underwriting works, from the moment a broker sends a new risk to the moment a policy is bound. Then decide which steps of that workflow an AI product should automate first, which steps it should not try to replace, and how you would know the product is working. Finish with the questions you would ask an underwriter before building anything.

The question checks whether you understand the customer&#x27;s workflow and business problem, not only the engineering.

What to deliver
A spoken walkthrough of the underwriting workflow in five to seven steps, each in one or two sentences.
A recommendation: the first step to automate, with the reason, and one step you would deliberately leave to the underwriter, with the reason.
The metric you would use to judge the product, stated in the customer&#x27;s terms.
Three questions for an underwriter that the company&#x27;s website could not answer.
What a strong answer covers
1.
The workflow: submission from a broker, clearance, appetite triage, risk assessment, rating and pricing, terms, quote and bind.
2.
Where the value is: which steps are mostly data handling and which are expert judgement, and why turnaround time matters commercially.
3.
The hard sub-problem: why extracting data from broker documents (applications, loss runs, schedules of values) is difficult and why its errors are expensive.
4.
The limits: why replacing the underwriter&#x27;s risk judgement meets resistance, and how pricing regulation pushes toward systems that explain their output.
5.
Measurement: a customer unit such as time from submission to quote, submissions handled per underwriter per day, or the share of declines issued the same day.
6.
Curiosity: specific questions about the customer&#x27;s worst step, where a human stays in the loop, and what the customer measures.
Examples
&quot;Walk me through how a commercial insurance submission becomes a policy.&quot; The answer lists the steps in order and says which ones are data handling and which are judgement.
&quot;Which part of underwriting would you automate first, and why?&quot; One defended choice, for example triage before full extraction, with the condition that would change your mind.
Constraints
1.
Describe commercial (business) insurance, not personal car or home insurance.
2.
You do not need exact regulations, but you must state that pricing decisions are regulated and what that implies for the system.
3.
Give one recommendation with a reason; a list of everything that could be automated is not an answer.
4.
Keep the walkthrough to about three minutes spoken, leaving time for discussion.
Follow-up 1: what would make it fail

What would make this product fail even if the models were accurate?

Follow-up 2: training on past decisions

A colleague proposes training a model on historical accept and decline decisions. What is the risk?

Follow-up 3: what you do not know

What do you not yet know about this industry, and how would you find out?</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Start with the user action and business outcome, then map the funnel into observable stages and possible interventions. Separate a prediction target from the action the system will take.
2. Pick a simple baseline and one high-value, feasible ML use case using label availability, latency and error costs. For onboarding, instrument activation/retention and avoid optimizing a single click in isolation.
3. Define time-valid labels and a comparison design; consider whether human review, a rule or better instrumentation should come before ML. Consequential decisions require appropriate governance and validation.
4. Propose a small reversible experiment with guardrails and quantify expected impact using explicitly assumed rates, not invented business data.

### Small example / sanity check

If onboarding abandonment is mainly a broken form, a recommender is not the first fix. If comment prediction is the task, define whether the label is comment occurrence, text content or ranking utility.

### Common trap

Choosing a sophisticated model before defining a measurable intervention and reliable feedback.
