## Question

(Verbal-only onsite simulating a live production incident.) The interviewer gives a scenario — e.g. 'orders complete but customers report missing items', 'payment-service latency has doubled', 'a percentage of dasher payouts are stuck in PENDING' — and you must walk through investigation, mitigation, recovery, and post-mortem. No coding. The interviewer adapts the scenario based on your responses; one known variant rejects every infrastructure fix you propose to force an operational-playbook answer.

## Worked solution

### Drive a concrete incident, without inventing observations

No live telemetry or exact scenario is supplied. Use this **fictional rehearsal**: “A fraction of dasher payouts are stuck PENDING.” First establish start time, affected fraction/regions/payment rails, total dollars, whether funds actually moved and whether the backlog is growing. Declare an incident owner, timeline and communication cadence. Protect customers and evidence before experimenting; do not batch-retry money movement with new IDs.

Compare affected/unaffected payout IDs end to end: earning ledger→payout intent→queue→processor request→provider status/webhook→internal status. Ask for recent deploy/config changes and stage-specific rates/latencies. If the provider already reports paid but internal status is PENDING, the problem may be webhook/reconciliation rather than transfer execution. If no provider request exists, inspect durable outbox/queue progress. If provider response timed out, query by the existing payout/idempotency key; a timeout is not proof no money moved. Each next probe follows the answer, not a preselected root cause.

### Mitigation and operational-playbook variant

Freeze only unsafe retries/affected rails or rollout, preserve normal payouts where isolation is possible, and use a documented provider fallback only with clear deduplication/authorization. Roll back a correlated bad release when evidence supports it and compatibility permits. If all infrastructure fixes are disallowed, switch to operations: segment the backlog, reconcile with provider ledgers, create an approved exception queue, prioritize oldest/high-impact cases, give support a consistent status message and timetable, and arrange approved manual remediation with dual control and audit. Staff the queue and communicate affected-customer expectations; an operational answer still must not invent paid status or duplicate transfers.

Recovery replays only missing idempotent transitions, verifies totals/counts and samples customer-visible states. Define stop/rollback conditions and compare ledger/provider balances before draining the whole backlog. Track oldest PENDING age, completion rate, duplicate-transfer rate and unresolved amounts; a falling error rate is insufficient if old payouts remain stuck.

### Other given scenarios and postmortem

For “orders complete but items missing,” separate ordering/menu snapshot, fulfillment/picking, handoff and delivery evidence; compare item-level requested/packed/refunded records. Immediate support credits/replacements follow policy while operations correct picking/menu availability; a server restart cannot restore a physically missing item. For “payment latency doubled,” split queueing, connection pools, application work and provider time; compare request mix/retries and recent changes, then use a bounded rollback/routing/load-shed mitigation supported by the bottleneck evidence.

After stability, write a blameless timeline, trigger/root/contributing causes, why detection/mitigation lagged, customer/financial impact and owners/dates for prevention. Add reconciliation/alert/runbook tests and verify actions completed. Distinguish observations from hypotheses throughout: “If provider-paid/internal-pending is confirmed, I would…” rather than fabricate incident facts. A useful answer demonstrates investigation ordering, safe mitigation, customer operations and verified recovery even when a technical fix is unavailable.
