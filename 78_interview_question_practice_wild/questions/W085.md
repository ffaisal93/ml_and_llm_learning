## Question

Understanding Linear Regression and Multicollinearity Issues

Describe linear regression and explain why variables should not have multicollinearity.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Explain multicollinearity and stable least squares.

Correlated predictors can make coefficient estimates unstable even when predictions remain useful.

1. Write the least-squares objective ||Xw-y||²; inspect rank or singular values of X.
2. Use QR or SVD-based least squares rather than explicitly inverting XᵀX. Centering and scaling can help conditioning.
3. Use ridge if shrinkage is appropriate, and distinguish predictive stability from interpretation of individual coefficients.

#### Derive ridge regression and its effect on conditioning.

Ridge penalizes large weights and reduces sensitivity along weakly identified feature directions.

1. For objective ||Xw-y||²+lambda*||w||², set its gradient to zero.
2. Solve (XᵀX+lambda*I)w=Xᵀy, keeping intercept treatment explicit.
3. Use a numerical solve rather than an explicit inverse, and tune lambda using held-out data.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Prefer projections and stable solves to memorized inverses

Least squares finds the projection of the response onto the feature space:

$$
\hat\beta=\arg\min_\beta\|X\beta-y\|_2^2,
\qquad X^\top X\hat\beta=X^\top y.
$$

The normal equations explain the mathematics, but explicitly forming $(X^\top X)^{-1}$ is usually a poor numerical implementation. Squaring the condition number can amplify instability. QR or SVD-based least-squares routines expose rank and near-dependence more reliably.

For two datasets with the same feature definitions, sufficient statistics add:

$$
S=X_1^\top X_1+X_2^\top X_2,\qquad
 t=X_1^\top y_1+X_2^\top y_2.
$$

Solving the pooled problem is not equivalent to averaging the two coefficient vectors. Rows carry different directional information, not only different sample counts.

State centering, intercept and regularization conventions. With ridge, the penalized directions satisfy $(X^\top X+\lambda I)\beta=X^\top y$, but an unpenalized intercept needs separate treatment. Distinguish coefficient identification from predictive usefulness: correlated columns can make individual coefficients unstable while their combined predictions remain fairly stable. Validate forecasts on independent data; impressive training fit, including interpolation of noise, is not evidence of a real predictive relation.

### Work a small example

If one column is exactly twice another, several coefficient pairs produce the same predictions. The individual coefficients are not identifiable without an extra convention.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Taking a numerical inverse and treating enormous coefficients as strong evidence of real effects.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Derive ridge regression and its effect on conditioning.** A zero singular value becomes regularized by a positive lambda, making the penalized system invertible for the penalized directions.

Does improved conditioning remove bias? No; ridge deliberately trades coefficient bias against variance and stability.
