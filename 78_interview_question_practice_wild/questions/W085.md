## Question

Understanding Linear Regression and Multicollinearity Issues

Describe linear regression and explain why variables should not have multicollinearity.

## Worked solution

### Linear regression models a conditional mean

Write $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$ and estimate coefficients by minimizing $\|y-X\beta\|_2^2$. Predictions are linear in the chosen features, which may themselves include interactions or transformations. With a full-rank design, ordinary least squares has solution $(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y$, but practical code should use QR/SVD or a least-squares solver rather than explicitly inverting the matrix.

Multicollinearity means one predictor is exactly or nearly a linear combination of other predictors. For example, including house size in both square meters and square feet contributes the same information twice. Exact collinearity makes $X^\top X$ singular and coefficients nonunique. Near-collinearity produces a poorly conditioned problem: small data changes can move individual coefficients substantially.

### Why this matters—and when it matters less

Under homoscedastic uncorrelated noise,

$$\operatorname{Cov}(\hat\beta\mid X)=\sigma^2(X^\top X)^{-1}.$$

Small eigenvalues of $X^\top X$ imply large coefficient variance. Signs and p-values can become unstable, making “the independent effect of feature j” hard to defend. However, multicollinearity does **not** by itself bias OLS under the usual exogeneity assumption. Predictions within the observed feature manifold may remain good even while coefficients are unstable. Extrapolating off that manifold is riskier.

For two identical columns, only the sum of their coefficients is identified. Coefficients `(2,0)` and `(1,1)` yield the same prediction. More optimization iterations cannot recover a unique individual effect from information the data does not contain.

### Diagnose and remedy

Inspect the design rank, singular values/condition number, correlation structure and coefficient stability across resamples. Pairwise correlations alone can miss a dependency involving several columns; VIF is another diagnostic, not a universal pass/fail rule. Remove duplicate measurements, combine redundant features based on domain meaning, or collect data that separates their effects. Ridge regularization stabilizes coefficients; PCA can give stable predictive components but changes interpretation. Validate any choice on held-out data.

The interview answer is that avoiding redundant predictors helps identification and stability, not that correlated features automatically invalidate every predictive model.
