## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Understanding Linear Regression and Multicollinearity Issues

Describe linear regression and explain why variables should not have multicollinearity.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain multicollinearity and stable least squares. Correlated predictors can make coefficient estimates unstable even when predictions remain useful.
2. Write the least-squares objective ||Xw-y||²; inspect rank or singular values of X.
3. Use QR or SVD-based least squares rather than explicitly inverting XᵀX. Centering and scaling can help conditioning.
4. Use ridge if shrinkage is appropriate, and distinguish predictive stability from interpretation of individual coefficients.
5. Derive ridge regression and its effect on conditioning. Ridge penalizes large weights and reduces sensitivity along weakly identified feature directions.
6. For objective ||Xw-y||²+lambda*||w||², set its gradient to zero.
7. Solve (XᵀX+lambda*I)w=Xᵀy, keeping intercept treatment explicit.
8. Use a numerical solve rather than an explicit inverse, and tune lambda using held-out data.

### Small example / sanity check

If one column is exactly twice another, several coefficient pairs produce the same predictions. The individual coefficients are not identifiable without an extra convention.

### Common trap

Taking a numerical inverse and treating enormous coefficients as strong evidence of real effects.
