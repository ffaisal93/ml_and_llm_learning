## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Calculate Merged Company EPS from PE Ratio and Market Cap

Given company A&#x27;s PE ratio and company B&#x27;s market cap, and assuming there is no synergy after they merge, what is the EPS of the merged company?

Example

Input

PE_A = 12, MarketCap_A = 10B, PE_B = 20, MarketCap_B = 20B</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Use market capitalization divided by P/E to infer earnings under a compatible trailing/forward convention.
2. The example gives earnings_A=10B/12≈0.833333B and earnings_B=20B/20=1B. With no synergy or financing effects, combined earnings are about 1.833333B.
3. EPS is combined earnings divided by the merged share count, which is not supplied. Cash financing, issued acquisition shares and exchange ratio can change that denominator and financing can change the numerator.
4. Therefore a unique EPS is not identifiable. If one additionally assumes combined equity value 30B with no premium/value effects, an illustrative combined P/E is 30/1.833333≈16.36, but that is not EPS.

### Small example / sanity check

If the post-merger share count were 1 billion under additional assumptions, EPS would be about 1.8333 currency units/share; a different share count gives a different EPS with the same market caps and P/Es.

### Common trap

Reporting a P/E ratio or total earnings as earnings per share.
