## Question

Calculate Merged Company EPS from PE Ratio and Market Cap

Given company A's PE ratio and company B's market cap, and assuming there is no synergy after they merge, what is the EPS of the merged company?

Example

### Input

```text
PE_A = 12, MarketCap_A = 10B, PE_B = 20, MarketCap_B = 20B
```

## Worked solution

### Earnings can be combined; EPS still needs shares

The title's limited inputs (A's P/E and B's market cap) cannot determine either company's earnings or merged EPS. Even the richer sample gives earnings but still omits merged share count and deal financing/exchange terms. For positive comparable earnings,

$$E_A=MC_A/PE_A,\quad E_B=MC_B/PE_B,\quad EPS_{merged}=\frac{E_A+E_B-\text{after-tax financing/other incremental costs}}{N_{merged}}.$$

“No synergy” removes an assumed operating gain; it does not specify the number of shares issued or eliminate debt interest, transaction costs or accounting adjustments. For a simplified all-stock/no-additional-cost model, the numerator is just E_A+E_B.

In the sample, firm A earns $10/12=0.833333$ billion USD and firm B earns $20/20=1$ billion USD, giving combined earnings of about 1.833333 billion USD before incremental costs. With one billion merged shares, EPS would be about 1.8333 USD; with two billion shares, it would be about 0.9167 USD. Both are compatible with the given market values and P/E ratios under different share prices/exchange structures. Thus the supplied information does not determine a unique EPS.

If one additionally assumes market values add with no premium/cost/revaluation, the implied combined P/E is $30/1.833333\approx16.36$. That is a weighted earnings multiple, **not** EPS and not the arithmetic mean of 12 and 20. To finish an actual merger EPS calculation, ask for A/B share counts or prices, the exchange ratio/new shares, cash/debt mix and incremental costs, all on a consistent earnings basis. This is an algebraic interview example, not a forecast of a real merger.
