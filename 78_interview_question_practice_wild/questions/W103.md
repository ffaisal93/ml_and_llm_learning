## Question

Capital One DS Power Day round 3, 45 minutes, role-play. The interviewer plays a non-technical Product Manager who asks why a model that performed well in validation is regressing in production. Explain the likely causes in plain English without technical jargon, and propose a diagnostic plan. The PM applies light pushback ("but we tested it before launch — what changed?") to test composure.

## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Explain a regressing model to a product manager.

Translate a metric change into affected decisions, user impact and a concrete next action.

1. State what changed, how reliable the evidence is, which users or slices are affected and the estimated cost.
2. Check deployment differences, feature freshness, label windows and threshold changes; distinguish observations from hypotheses.
3. Recommend rollback, a limited canary or targeted repair with clear success criteria and an owner.

#### Distinguish data drift, concept drift and label delay.

A production metric can change because inputs changed, the input-to-label relationship changed, or the labels are not yet mature.

1. Check pipeline health, feature distributions and missingness before assuming the model changed.
2. When mature labels exist, compare conditional performance and calibration on matched slices and periods.
3. Separate delayed outcome windows from true degradation; use operational proxies cautiously and validate them against final outcomes.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Establish what the number actually estimates

Write the population, unit of analysis, outcome window and denominator next to the metric. A per-user mean and a per-impression mean answer different questions. A global mean over unequal batches is

$$
\bar\ell=\frac{\sum_b\sum_{i\in b}\ell_i}{\sum_b n_b},
$$

not generally the unweighted mean of batch means. Workers should reduce numerators and denominators separately when reporting that metric.

Use a comparison with matched examples, mature labels and unchanged definitions. For uncertainty, resample independent units; repeated events within a user or prompt are not automatically independent. A confidence interval measures sampling uncertainty under assumptions, not protection against leaked data or an inappropriate target.

Calibration asks whether predictions around probability $p$ are correct about fraction $p$ of the time. It is not the same as accuracy. A reliability plot should show bin populations, and a calibration-error formula must say whether bins or examples receive equal weight.

When quality regresses, trace data availability, feature freshness, label delays and cohort mix before automatically retraining. For example, a fraud model may look worse on immature labels even when its true behavior has not changed. Report missing/time-out outputs explicitly: dropping them from the denominator can turn an unreliable system into an apparently perfect one.

### Work a small example

“Recall dropped from 80% to 70% on the same mature-label cohort, adding about 100 missed cases per 1000 actual positives” is more actionable than “the model drifted.”

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Blaming an algorithm before checking data and operational changes, or hiding uncertainty behind technical vocabulary.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Distinguish data drift, concept drift and label delay.** A fraud model's observed precision may look low immediately after deployment because confirmed fraud labels arrive weeks later.

Can input distributions stay stable while performance changes? Yes; concept drift can alter P(y|x) without an obvious marginal shift in x.
