## Worked solution

### Solution outline

#### Explain PCA from centered data to reconstruction.

PCA finds orthogonal directions that capture the greatest variance under squared reconstruction error.

1. Fit a training-data mean and center X; use SVD X=U*S*Vᵀ.
2. Project onto the first k right singular vectors and reconstruct with the same mean and directions.
3. Choose k using validation needs or explained variance; scale features first only when that matches the intended geometry.

#### Scale dimensionality reduction to large or sparse data.

Choose a factorization that fits the data's size and storage structure rather than forming a dense covariance matrix automatically.

1. Use randomized/truncated SVD when only a small number of leading components is needed.
2. Use incremental PCA for bounded-memory dense batches, with its approximation and batch effects understood.
3. For sparse count matrices, truncated SVD can avoid densification from centering; explain that this changes the centered-PCA problem.

#### Interpret clustering completeness and homogeneity.

External clustering metrics compare assignments with labels but reward different behaviors.

1. Homogeneity asks whether each predicted cluster contains one label; completeness asks whether all members of a label stay in one cluster.
2. Inspect both, plus a combined measure or task-specific evaluation, under the same data and label conventions.
3. Remember that the reference labels may not represent the only useful clustering structure.

### Understand why the approach works

#### Choose the geometry before the update rule

An algorithm's center update follows its objective. Squared Euclidean clustering uses means:

$$
J_2=\sum_i\|x_i-\mu_{c_i}\|_2^2.
$$

For fixed assignments, differentiating with respect to a center sets it to its members' arithmetic mean. Manhattan distance instead uses coordinatewise medians:

$$
J_1=\sum_i\|x_i-m_{c_i}\|_1.
$$

For points $0,2,10$, the squared-error center is $4$, while the absolute-distance center is $2$. Swapping these updates silently solves a different problem.

Alternating nearest-center assignment and an exact fixed-cluster update cannot increase the chosen objective, but it need not reach a global optimum. State initialization, empty-cluster handling, deterministic ties and a stopping rule. Recompute assignments for returned centers after the last update.

For retrieval, state normalization, similarity and relevance labels. Cosine and raw dot product rank differently when embedding norms vary. Index approximation must be evaluated using recall and downstream quality, not latency alone. Negative examples can actually be valid alternative matches; audit this before interpreting a lower contrastive loss as better semantics. Evaluate cluster/retrieval stability across seeds and important slices rather than trusting a visually attractive plot.

### Edge cases and common mistakes

Fitting PCA on all train and test data before evaluating a model.

### Check your reasoning on related cases

**Scale dimensionality reduction to large or sparse data.** A million-by-100000 sparse matrix may be manageable in sparse form but impossible to materialize as a dense array.

When would random projection fit? When approximate distance preservation is enough and interpretability is less important.

**Interpret clustering completeness and homogeneity.** Putting every observation in one cluster gives perfect completeness but generally poor homogeneity for multiple classes.

What does splitting every sample into its own cluster do? It gives perfect homogeneity but typically poor completeness.
