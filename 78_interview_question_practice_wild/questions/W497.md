## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Fee Calculation with Boolean Flag and Numeric Parameter

Suppose there are only two parameters: one can be a T/F flag and the other can be any number X. Under this condition, how would you design a mechanism to calculate three different fee schedules? The fee per share increases with longevity; let&#x27;s say 0.2, 0.3, 0.4. The cap is 1.5.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Clarify whether X is quantity, holding duration, an encoded tier or another variable, and whether the 1.5 cap is total or per-share. The two inputs alone do not uniquely identify an unstated fee contract.
2. Under a declared example, let X be nonnegative holding duration, thresholds t1<t2 select rates 0.2/0.3/0.4, and the boolean choose standard versus a documented alternative schedule. Represent rates/thresholds as a table, not deeply nested duplicated formulas.
3. For a total-fee convention fee=min(1.5,base_rate(X)*quantity), quantity must be fixed or separately known; it cannot be recovered from an X already used solely as duration. For a per-unit convention cap the rate instead.
4. Validate boundary equality, negative/nonfinite X and rounding. Explain exactly what policy information is missing rather than silently inventing thresholds or overloading one scalar with multiple meanings.

### Small example / sanity check

If X is explicitly declared to be quantity, a selected rate 0.3 and total cap 1.5 produce fee min(1.5,0.3*X); X=10 gives 1.5. This example still needs a stated schedule-selection rule.

### Common trap

Claiming a boolean alone can select three independent schedules, or applying a cap to the wrong unit.
