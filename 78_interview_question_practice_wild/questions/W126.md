## Question

(MLE onsite, open-ended, no coding.) An interviewer-driven applied-ML discussion. Common threads: A/B test fundamentals (hypothesis, power analysis, variance reduction / CUPED, confidence intervals); ranking metric design (CTR vs CVR vs revenue; offline NDCG/AUC vs online conversion); multi-objective ranking (combining CVR, delivery time, freshness, ad slots into one ranker); marketplace experimentation (switchback vs A/B, novelty effects, guardrail metrics); the offline-online gap (why an offline win didn't ship — leakage, serving skew); plus the methods used in your own recent work. Marketplace flavor (switchback, supply-demand elasticity, per-region effects) is heavier here than at peers.

## Worked solution

### Start with a marketplace decision

Suppose the ranker chooses which store/item to show for an order. Specify whether the business goal is incremental fulfilled orders, contribution margin or retention; CTR alone rewards curiosity and can lower purchase quality. Define eligibility, attribution window, unit (user/order/region-time) and guardrails: cancellations, late deliveries, user satisfaction, supplier imbalance and latency. A CVR score is meaningful only with a declared denominator (per impression versus per click). Offline NDCG measures ranked relevance under its label/gain definition, AUC pair ranking; neither automatically estimates conversion under a new exposure policy.

### Experiment hypothesis, power and variance reduction

Predeclare $H_0:\Delta=0$, a meaningful alternative $\delta$, alpha, power, duration and stopping rule. For a two-arm difference in means with equal per-unit variance $\sigma^2$, approximate samples/arm by $2(z_{1-\alpha/2}+z_{1-\beta})^2\sigma^2/\delta^2$ under independent units. Use historical user/cluster-level variance and account for repeated outcomes, delayed labels, seasonality and minimum business cycles. Check sample-ratio mismatch and logging before reading effect estimates.

CUPED uses a pre-treatment covariate X correlated with outcome Y: $Y_{adj}=Y-\theta(X-E[X])$, $\theta=\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)/\operatorname{Var}(X)$. Under appropriate randomization/estimation, variance becomes $\operatorname{Var}(Y)(1-\rho^2)$. Never use a covariate altered by treatment. Report effects and confidence intervals at the randomization unit; use a predeclared sequential method if monitoring continuously, rather than stopping at the first p<.05.

### Build and test a multi-objective ranker

One initial utility can be expected margin from fulfillment minus calibrated penalties for delay/cancellation, plus a bounded freshness/exploration value. Normalize units: add dollars to dollar-equivalent penalties, not raw seconds and probabilities. Learn separate calibrated heads for CVR, time and cancellation, then optimize weights on validation/policy simulations and test online. Apply inventory and ad-slot constraints explicitly; paid placement must respect quality and the product's exposure policy. Inspect the Pareto frontier and slice harms instead of hiding trade-offs in one unexplained score. For cold start use content/location features and controlled exploration.

### Interference changes the experimental design

Individual A/B is suitable when users do not materially change one another's outcomes. A shared courier/supply pool violates that assumption: one treatment changes wait times and availability for controls. Use randomized balanced region-time switchbacks with blocks/washout justified by fulfillment/carryover duration, enough independent blocks and analysis clustered by assignment. Region-level variation and supply-demand elasticity can make an aggregate gain mask a regional loss. Persistent effects may require longer blocks, geo clusters or a different design; switching quickly does not remove carryover automatically.

Separate novelty from durable effects by running through normal weekly cycles, predeclaring day-since-exposure analysis and monitoring supplier adaptation. If an offline improvement does not ship, replay feature values available at decision time, check train/serve schema and calibration, label leakage, selection bias from historical exposure and p95 latency. Validate against randomized traffic rather than assuming logged rankings are unbiased counterfactuals. Use actual recent work to support your method choices; no candidate project results are supplied here.
