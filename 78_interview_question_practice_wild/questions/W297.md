## Question

Two customers each buy two cups of coffee from the same shop. Compute the probability that one customer's cups are better than the other's, under several precise meanings of "better".

### Setup

Customer A buys cups A1, A2; customer B buys cups B1, B2.
Each cup has a quality score. The four scores are independent and identically distributed.
Parts 1 to 4 use a continuous distribution, so two cups never have exactly the same score. Do not assume any particular distribution.
Tasks
1. Clean sweep. Find the probability that every one of A's cups is better than every one of B's cups, i.e. min(A1, A2) &gt; max(B1, B2).
2. Best cup. Find the probability that A's best cup is better than B's best cup.
3. Total. Find the probability that A1 + A2 &gt; B1 + B2.
4. Head-to-head. Compare each of A's cups with each of B's cups (four comparisons). Give the full distribution of the number of comparisons A wins, and the probability that A wins strictly more than half.
5. n cups each. Generalise Task 1 to n cups per customer. Give a closed form, and evaluate it for n = 3, n = 5 and n = 10.
6. Discrete ratings. Now each score is an integer rating drawn uniformly from {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}, so ties are possible. Recompute Tasks 1, 2 and 3 as exact fractions (strict inequality wins; give the tie probability for Tasks 2 and 3). Explain why the answers move away from the continuous ones.

### Requirements

Justify each continuous answer with a symmetry or counting argument that does not depend on the distribution.
Give exact fractions, not decimals only.
Describe how you would check every answer with a short simulation.

## Worked solution

### Continuous iid scores: rank symmetry

For A to beat B in the stated direction, distinguish clean sweep, best and total; “either customer sweeps” would double the clean-sweep probability. All six choices of A's two rank positions among four are equally likely under iid continuous scores.

1. **Clean sweep:** only A occupying ranks 3 and 4 (ascending order) works, so P=1/C(4,2)=**1/6**. Both customers' sweep events together have probability 1/3.
2. **Best cup:** the unique highest score belongs to A with probability **1/2**.
3. **Total:** A1+A2 and B1+B2 are iid continuous random variables, so ties have probability zero; by exchangeability P(A total>B total)=**1/2**. No particular score distribution or finite mean is required.
4. **Four head-to-head comparisons:** choosing A ranks {1,2},{1,3},{1,4},{2,3},{2,4},{3,4} gives win counts 0,1,2,2,3,4 respectively. Thus P(W=0,1,2,3,4)=(**1/6,1/6,1/3,1/6,1/6**) and P(W>2)=**1/3**. The four comparisons share cups and are not independent Bernoulli trials.
5. **n cups each:** A must occupy the top n of 2n positions, so P=**1/C(2n,n)**. For n=3,5,10 this is **1/20,1/252,1/184756** respectively. If the intended wording means either customer, multiply by two because the strict sweep events are disjoint.

### Discrete uniform ratings 1 through 5

There are 5⁴=625 equally likely ordered quadruples. Strict inequalities matter:

* **Clean sweep:** sum over A's minimum m. There are (6−m)²−(5−m)²=11−2m ordered A pairs with minimum m and (m−1)² B pairs below it. Therefore Σ[m=1..5](11−2m)(m−1)²/625=70/625=**14/125**.
* **Best:** the maximum of two ratings has counts 1,3,5,7,9 for values 1..5. Tie probability is (1²+3²+5²+7²+9²)/625=165/625=**33/125**. By symmetry strict A-win probability is (1−33/125)/2=**46/125**.
* **Total:** two-rating sum counts are 1,2,3,4,5,4,3,2,1. Tie probability is (1²+2²+3²+4²+5²+4²+3²+2²+1²)/625=85/625=**17/125**. Strict A-win probability is (1−17/125)/2=**54/125**.

Ties remove probability mass from strict best/total wins. For sweep, tied/interleaved boundary scores also make strict separation less likely; continuous rank orderings are no longer all distinct. Replacing “>” by “≥” would be another problem.

### Checks, including simulation

The runnable checker enumerates all 625 discrete quadruples and all six continuous rank placements. For simulation, use a fixed seed, generate independent continuous uniforms (valid because continuous tasks depend on ranks/exchangeability), evaluate each stated inequality and average indicators; repeat with four uniform integers 1..5. For n=10 the sweep is rare, so ordinary small Monte Carlo has high relative error; use the exact combinatorial answer, much larger sample size or simulate rank subsets directly. Report a binomial standard error sqrt(p_hat(1−p_hat)/M) rather than treating agreement from a few successes as proof. Total-sum symmetry works across all iid continuous score laws, while simulating one law is only a check, not the justification.
