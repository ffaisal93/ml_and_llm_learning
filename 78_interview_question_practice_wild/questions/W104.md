## Question

Discuss your experience with large language model training and explain how you decide whether training data is suitable for a target capability. Your response should connect concrete work you performed with a rigorous evaluation plan, then show how you approach technical ownership and learning.

Questions to answer
1. Describe your past work on LLM training. Identify the stages you worked on, your personal ownership, the scale, and a measurable or observable outcome.
2. Explain how you assess a dataset before training. Cover duplicate removal, contamination, language and encoding checks, quality filtering, and manual inspection.
3. Design an experiment to determine whether a dataset improves instruction following, mathematics, coding, truthfulness, or factuality. Explain the baseline, controlled variables, capability-specific evaluations, uncertainty, regression checks, and decision rule.
4. State whether your current center of gravity is research, engineering, or a deliberate mix. Support the choice with one example of working effectively in the other mode.
5. Walk through one difficult technical problem: why it mattered, failed attempts, the eventual resolution, evidence that it worked, and what you learned.
Example response shapes
A strong data-quality response might explain a near-duplicate and contamination pipeline, then propose equal-token-budget ablations with paired evaluations and confidence intervals. The explanation should connect each check to a concrete failure it prevents.
A strong challenge response might describe diagnosing a training loss spike, ruling out data and numeric causes in a deliberate order, validating the fix through a reproducible rerun, and changing the monitoring process afterward.

### Constraints

Use genuine projects and clearly distinguish your work from the team's work.
State assumptions and scale with numbers you can substantiate; do not invent outcomes.
Distinguish truthfulness from factuality and explain how their data and evaluations differ.
Evaluate each capability separately rather than relying only on an aggregate score.
Keep the complete spoken response to roughly 15 minutes, with the dataset-quality experiment receiving the most detail.

## Worked solution

### Use this as a truthful speaking guide

The question requires the candidate's real history; no personal projects or measured outcomes were supplied. Do not memorize an invented success story. Spend about two minutes on ownership, seven on data and experiment design, two on working style and four on a difficult problem. Replace the evidence requests below with facts you can defend; the experimental plan itself is fully specified here.

For your opening, identify the actual stage you owned: pretraining, supervised instruction tuning, preference training, data curation, evaluation, or serving. State model size, tokens/examples, hardware and elapsed time only where recorded. Say “I implemented the deduplication job; another engineer owned training” if that is the true division. Explain the observable outcome with its comparison and uncertainty; if no controlled result exists, say that the pipeline shipped or passed its acceptance check rather than claiming a capability gain.

### Audit data against the intended capability

Freeze a source manifest with permissions, collection dates, hashes, languages and lineage. Remove exact normalized-document duplicates with hashes; find near duplicates using token-shingle MinHash candidates followed by measured text overlap. Group the entire duplicate family before assigning splits. Compare training documents against evaluation prompts, reference solutions and close paraphrases; quarantine matches rather than only deleting evaluation-looking keywords. Audit false positives, especially boilerplate and short mathematical statements.

Decode UTF-8 with error accounting, inspect replacement characters and mojibake, check language identification by script and human samples, and retain intentional multilingual/code-switching examples. Filter spam, broken templates, truncated answers, PII and task-specific quality defects. Keep reason codes and before/after counts by source/language. Inspect stratified random examples plus high-loss, filter-boundary and rare-capability examples; do not review only the model's favorites. Double-label a subset with an adjudicated rubric and report agreement and error rates. A clean encoding does not prove a correct answer.

### An equal-budget experiment

Start two arms from the exact same checkpoint: control uses the existing mixture; treatment replaces a fixed token fraction with the candidate corpus. Hold total training tokens, optimizer, schedule, batch sizes, context length and evaluation decoding fixed. Include a candidate-with-quality-filter arm if the question is whether cleaning helps. Run multiple training seeds when feasible; separate seed variability from prompt-sampling uncertainty. Keep all evaluation families untouched by curation.

| Capability | Independent evaluation and failure check |
|---|---|
| Instruction following | Held-out constraints, multi-turn corrections and machine-checkable formatting; human scoring for semantic compliance |
| Mathematics | Novel or decontaminated problems, exact answers plus checked derivations; include difficulty and topic slices |
| Coding | Hidden unit tests in a restricted runner, pass@1 at fixed budget and pass@k with identical sample counts; include insecure solutions and regressions |
| Truthfulness | Misconception/adversarial questions, uncertainty and false-premise handling; reward rejecting an unsupported premise |
| Factuality | Atomic claims verified against dated authoritative evidence, citation entailment, coverage and abstention on unavailable facts |

Truthfulness concerns resisting misleading beliefs and stating uncertainty rather than confidently repeating a misconception. Factuality concerns whether claims match evidence/the world at the relevant date. They overlap, but a fresh fact corpus is not by itself an evaluation of resistance to false premises. Judges must be blinded, randomized and calibrated against human labels; a training-model judge is not independent evidence.

Score paired prompts and bootstrap by problem/document family rather than treating paraphrases as independent. Report per-capability differences with intervals, compute cost, refusals and safety/language/long-context regressions. Before running, set a minimum meaningful improvement and regression tolerances. A possible *chosen* rule is: promote only if the target capability's lower confidence bound exceeds zero and its point improvement exceeds the agreed margin, while no critical regression exceeds tolerance. Otherwise collect more evidence or reject; an aggregate average cannot override a failing safety slice. Those are decision rules, not results from an experiment already run.

### Working style and a difficult problem

Choose “research,” “engineering,” or “deliberate mix” based on your actual work. Support it with one contrasting example: an engineer may have designed a controlled ablation; a researcher may have built restartable training and production monitoring. Explain the trade-off you personally made.

For the challenge, use a real incident with dated logs or artifacts: impact → initial hypotheses → failed attempts → decisive observation → repair → rerun → lesson. For a loss spike, a defensible diagnostic order is to preserve the batch/checkpoint/seed, check data corruption and masking, inspect nonfinite activations/gradients and optimizer state, then isolate a minimal reproducible cause. Mention which checks you actually ran. Validate using a reproducible rerun and unaffected evaluation slices; explain the monitoring or test added afterward. If you have not trained an LLM, state that directly and map a relevant smaller-model/data project to these responsibilities without claiming experience you lack.
