## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Discuss your experience with large language model training and explain how you decide whether training data is suitable for a target capability. Your response should connect concrete work you performed with a rigorous evaluation plan, then show how you approach technical ownership and learning.

Questions to answer
1.
Describe your past work on LLM training. Identify the stages you worked on, your personal ownership, the scale, and a measurable or observable outcome.
2.
Explain how you assess a dataset before training. Cover duplicate removal, contamination, language and encoding checks, quality filtering, and manual inspection.
3.
Design an experiment to determine whether a dataset improves instruction following, mathematics, coding, truthfulness, or factuality. Explain the baseline, controlled variables, capability-specific evaluations, uncertainty, regression checks, and decision rule.
4.
State whether your current center of gravity is research, engineering, or a deliberate mix. Support the choice with one example of working effectively in the other mode.
5.
Walk through one difficult technical problem: why it mattered, failed attempts, the eventual resolution, evidence that it worked, and what you learned.
Example response shapes
A strong data-quality response might explain a near-duplicate and contamination pipeline, then propose equal-token-budget ablations with paired evaluations and confidence intervals. The explanation should connect each check to a concrete failure it prevents.
A strong challenge response might describe diagnosing a training loss spike, ruling out data and numeric causes in a deliberate order, validating the fix through a reproducible rerun, and changing the monitoring process afterward.
Constraints
Use genuine projects and clearly distinguish your work from the team&#x27;s work.
State assumptions and scale with numbers you can substantiate; do not invent outcomes.
Distinguish truthfulness from factuality and explain how their data and evaluations differ.
Evaluate each capability separately rather than relying only on an aggregate score.
Keep the complete spoken response to roughly 15 minutes, with the dataset-quality experiment receiving the most detail.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
2. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
3. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
4. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.
5. Audit human-labeled training data before scaling it. More labels help only if they carry the intended signal and match the task definition.
6. Inspect the sampling frame, instructions, class balance, duplicates, annotator disagreement and gold-label checks.
7. Separate ambiguous examples from clear errors; compare disagreement across languages, subgroups and content types.
8. Run a controlled data-quality intervention against a fixed model and evaluation set before attributing improvements to architecture.

### Small example / sanity check

Improving annotation guidelines while holding the model fixed can show a clearer causal contribution than changing data and architecture together.

### Common trap

Calling the dataset “clean” without saying what was checked.
