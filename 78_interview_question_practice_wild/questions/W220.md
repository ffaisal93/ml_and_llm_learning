## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Analyze how ordinary least-squares slopes combine across two disjoint samples, then reuse a fitted model to select and add one new regressor. The goal is to distinguish what can be inferred from fitted slopes alone and to avoid repeatedly fitting the full expanded model.

Given
Sample 1 contains X1 and Y1 with M observations; sample 2 contains X2 and Y2 with N observations. In parts 1 through 3, each X and Y is a single column and both within-sample predictor sums of squares are positive.
In parts 4 and 5, X1 is an N by k full-column-rank matrix, X2 is an N by (f-k) matrix, e is the residual from regressing Y on X1, and X1 = QR is an available thin QR factorization.
Treat M and N as known in part 3, and also state what can be guessed if only the two slopes are known.
Questions
1.
Fit without intercepts on both samples and their concatenation. Given the two sample slopes beta1 and beta2, derive the combined slope beta and give its exact possible range, including beta1 = beta2.
2.
Repeat with an intercept in every regression. Given only the two within-sample slopes, determine the possible pooled slope. Assume the attainable set has no gaps.
3.
Return to regressions without intercepts and assume all (x, y) pairs are i.i.d. from one zero-mean bivariate Gaussian distribution. Give a reasonable estimate of beta using beta1, beta2, M, and N, and state the equal-size or unknown-size approximation.
4.
Compute which column of X2 produces the largest reduction in residual sum of squares when added to X1. Derive the reduction formula and give the cost of scanning all candidates using the existing QR factorization.
5.
For the selected column, derive the new coefficient and the update to the k existing coefficients without a full refit.
Efficiency target

Do not fit f-k separate augmented models or refit the full design from scratch, which can cost O(N f^2). The candidate scan should use matrix projections in O(N k (f-k)) time, followed by an O(k^2) triangular solve for the coefficient update.

Constraints
M, N &gt;= 2; 1 &lt;= k &lt; f &lt;= N.
Ignore a candidate column whose component orthogonal to X1 has zero norm, because it adds no identifiable direction.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Pool two disjoint ordinary least-squares datasets correctly. The pooled solution depends on sufficient statistics, not a simple average of coefficients.
2. For each dataset retain S_i=X_iᵀX_i and t_i=X_iᵀy_i using the same feature and intercept conventions.
3. Sum S=S1+S2 and t=t1+t2, then solve S*beta=t when well-posed, or use stable factorization-based alternatives.
4. Inspect conditioning and rank; for raw data, QR/SVD avoids unnecessary normal-equation instability.
5. Add a feature to an existing least-squares regression efficiently. Only the part of the new feature unexplained by current features can explain additional response variation.
6. Residualize the new column z against X to get z_perp, and residualize y to get y_perp.
7. If z_perp has nonzero squared norm, its incremental coefficient is z_perpᵀy_perp/(z_perpᵀz_perp).
8. Update old coefficients consistently using the projection of z onto X, or update a QR factorization directly.

### Small example / sanity check

One dataset with 10 rows and another with 1000 generally should not contribute equally by averaging their coefficient vectors.

### Common trap

Averaging coefficients without their design information or ignoring different feature encodings.
