## Question

1. What is functional programming?
2. What does NP-complete mean?
3. What is integration by parts?
4. What is mutual information? (Give formula.)
5. What is entropy, and why does the uniform distribution maximize it?
6. What is a moment generating function (MGF)?
7. What is insertion sort?
8. What is the Central Limit Theorem (CLT)?

## Worked solution

### Eight direct fundamentals

1. **Functional programming** organizes computation as function evaluation with explicit inputs/outputs, favoring pure functions, immutable data and higher-order functions. A pure `square(x)=x*x` has no hidden writes and is referentially transparent; I/O/state can still exist through explicit boundaries. The paradigm does not mean “all code has no state” or guarantee speed.
2. **NP-complete** describes a decision problem that is in NP (a yes certificate can be verified in polynomial time) and NP-hard (every NP decision problem reduces to it in polynomial time). A polynomial-time solver for one NP-complete problem would imply P=NP. We do not know that P≠NP. SAT is a canonical example; optimization problems are usually called NP-hard unless converted to decisions.
3. **Integration by parts** follows from (uv)'=u'v+uv': ∫u dv=uv−∫v du. For ∫x e^x dx, choose u=x,dv=e^x dx to get (x−1)e^x+C. Definite integrals include boundary term [uv]_a^b and require suitable differentiability/integrability.
4. **Mutual information** is I(X;Y)=Σx,y p(x,y)log[p(x,y)/(p(x)p(y))], or the analogous density integral when defined. It is KL(joint||product), hence nonnegative and zero exactly at independence under standard conditions. Independent fair bits have I=0; identical fair bits have one bit. Specify log base; differential entropy subtleties do not change the KL definition.
5. **Entropy** for a finite distribution is H(p)=−Σp_i log p_i with 0log0=0. On fixed n-element support, KL(p||uniform)=log n−H(p)≥0, so H≤log n with equality at uniform. Without a fixed support/constraint, there is no universal uniform entropy maximizer on the real line. A fair coin has one bit; a deterministic one has zero.
6. **MGF** is M_X(t)=E[e^(tX)] where finite. If finite on a neighborhood of zero, derivatives at zero give moments and the MGF determines the law. For Bernoulli(p), M=1−p+pe^t, so M'(0)=p. An MGF need not exist away from zero (e.g. a Cauchy); characteristic functions are more general.
7. **Insertion sort** maintains a sorted prefix: take the next key, shift larger prefix values right and insert it. [3,1,2] becomes [1,3,2], then [1,2,3]. With strict “>” shifts it is stable, uses O(1) auxiliary array space, is O(n²) average/worst and O(n) on sorted input. Binary-searching insertion reduces comparisons but not required shifts.
8. **CLT:** for iid observations with mean μ and finite variance σ²>0, √n(average−μ)/σ converges in distribution to N(0,1). Thus average has approximate standard error σ/√n for large n; it does not make the raw data normal. Dependence/heavy tails may invalidate the ordinary statement, and finite-n approximation quality depends on the underlying distribution.

These answers state each definition and the assumption behind its familiar shortcut. In particular NP membership is not a runtime guarantee, entropy maximization needs a constraint and the CLT concerns a standardized aggregate, not individual observations.
