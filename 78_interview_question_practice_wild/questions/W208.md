## Question

Prepare a technical deep-dive on one machine learning project you know well, either a published paper or a shipped system, and be ready to defend it under detailed questioning.

What to prepare

Cover each of the following for your project:
1. Motivation and headline result: the problem, why it matters, and the main quantitative result.
2. Method: the model or system design, the key equation or architecture, and why each major choice was made over the alternatives.
3. Evaluation: dataset construction and its biases, the choice of baselines and why stronger ones were not used, statistical significance of the gains, and ablations.
4. Failure boundaries: what would make the result fail, and how it behaves when the label or input distribution shifts over time (for example with user-generated content).
5. Scaling and transfer: how the approach scales, and how it would transfer to a different product surface such as recommendation, an in-game economy, moderation or creator tooling.
Deliverable

A written outline of about one page for each of the five parts, with the concrete numbers, equations and trade-offs you would cite.

### Constraints

Choose a project you can explain end to end, deep enough to withstand follow-up questions on any part.
Every claim about improvement must name its baseline and how significance was checked.

## Worked solution

### Five-part written rehearsal dossier

No personal project or paper was supplied. Below is an **illustrative shipped-system study plan**, not a factual claim of improvement: ranking relevant help articles for a support query. It provides the five requested outlines; in a real one-page-per-part dossier, attach your actual dataset tables, experiment logs and failure examples. The numerical experiment below is deliberately hypothetical and cannot be cited as a measured result.

#### 1. Motivation and headline result

Users need a correct article near the top of a short list; an irrelevant top result wastes support time. Define the unit as a query session, the primary metric as NDCG@10 and the online guardrails as resolution rate, latency and unsafe recommendations. Example scope: 2 million historical sessions, 100,000 articles, a team of three, with my ownership limited to ranking and evaluation. State which data, infrastructure and labels others owned. A hypothetical result might be NDCG@10 0.42 versus 0.38 for the production lexical baseline, paired-session bootstrap 95% CI for the difference [0.025, 0.055]. These numbers illustrate a report format, not evidence. The result is only credible after specifying dataset dates, exclusions, sample size and how sessions were sampled. Online impact is a separate experiment; offline relevance is not proof of reduced support contacts.

#### 2. Method and alternatives

Use a two-stage architecture: lexical plus dense retrieval generates 100 candidates; a small cross-encoder reranks them to 10. A bi-encoder score is $s(q,d)=u(q)^Tv(d)$ with normalized vectors. Train retrieval with $-\log[\exp(s(q,d^+)/\tau)/\sum_j\exp(s(q,d_j)/\tau)]$; negatives include similar but wrong articles, checked for false negatives. Precompute article embeddings; keep query encoding and reranking within a stated latency budget. BM25 is simpler and better at identifiers; dense retrieval handles paraphrases; the cross-encoder costs one pair evaluation per candidate. Compare against lexical-only, dense-only and a strong tuned hybrid. If cross-encoder reranking of all articles is omitted, explain its cost rather than calling it a weak baseline. Version the index and encoder together to prevent incompatible embedding spaces.

#### 3. Dataset and evaluation

Clicks are exposure-biased and are not relevance labels. Construct an adjudicated query–article set, sample rare languages and failed sessions, and retain unanswerable queries. Use time-based splits with article-family grouping to prevent near-duplicate leakage. Report label guidelines, disagreement and coverage of new articles. A hypothetical ablation table could contain lexical 0.38, hybrid 0.40 and hybrid+reranker 0.42, all evaluated on exactly the same held-out queries; label these hypothetical until run. Bootstrap paired differences by user/session, correct for repeatedly choosing among many variants or reserve a final untouched set, and report uncertainty for tail slices. Online, randomize stable users, predefine sample size and duration, measure resolution with delayed outcomes, and check treatment does not change logging itself.

#### 4. Failure boundaries and shift

A new product name may be absent from training; lexical retrieval can rescue it if the article exists. No retriever can return an unpublished answer. Detect new-entity misses, stale articles, contradictory versions and multilingual gaps. User-generated queries can drift toward slang or adversarial instructions; retrieved text is untrusted and must not execute tools. Monitor query distributions, zero-result rate, article freshness, relevance by language and adjudicated support cases. Retrain only after confirming label semantics have not changed. If all old “resolved” labels become unreliable after a UI change, recalibration alone does not repair the target. Roll back on a slice regression even if the mean metric improves.

#### 5. Scaling and transfer

For 100,000 articles with 768 float32 dimensions, raw vectors need about 307 MB before index overhead. At 10 million articles, that becomes about 30.7 GB; sharding, compressed ANN and incremental index updates become relevant, and recall must be remeasured. Cap reranker candidates and benchmark p99 under realistic lengths. For recommendation, replace query relevance with user–item utility while correcting exposure bias and protecting diversity. For moderation, high-severity false negatives and appeals dominate the objective; a relevance score is not a policy violation score. For creator tooling, prioritize faithful sources and human edits. For an in-game economy, strategic behavior and delayed marketplace effects require simulation and conservative online tests. Transfer the representation only after checking label meanings, action costs and distribution, and keep task-specific evaluation. The key defense is the experiment that would falsify your explanation: equal compute, equal data and a targeted ablation can separate architecture gains from more training effort.
