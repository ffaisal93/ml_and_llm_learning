## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Prepare a technical deep-dive on one machine learning project you know well, either a published paper or a shipped system, and be ready to defend it under detailed questioning.

What to prepare

Cover each of the following for your project:

1.
Motivation and headline result: the problem, why it matters, and the main quantitative result.
2.
Method: the model or system design, the key equation or architecture, and why each major choice was made over the alternatives.
3.
Evaluation: dataset construction and its biases, the choice of baselines and why stronger ones were not used, statistical significance of the gains, and ablations.
4.
Failure boundaries: what would make the result fail, and how it behaves when the label or input distribution shifts over time (for example with user-generated content).
5.
Scaling and transfer: how the approach scales, and how it would transfer to a different product surface such as recommendation, an in-game economy, moderation or creator tooling.
Deliverable

A written outline of about one page for each of the five parts, with the concrete numbers, equations and trade-offs you would cite.

Constraints
Choose a project you can explain end to end, deep enough to withstand follow-up questions on any part.
Every claim about improvement must name its baseline and how significance was checked.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain your most technically complex ML project. A strong answer demonstrates decisions and evidence, not a list of technologies.
2. State the user problem, constraints and your own contribution separately from the team's work.
3. Describe the data, baseline, hardest technical failure, alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
4. Quantify the result using a valid comparison, explain limitations and say what you would change next. Use real experiences rather than invented metrics.
5. Discuss the data strategy behind an ML project. Data choices can explain more of a project's outcome than the architecture name.
6. Describe collection, permissions, label generation, cleaning and the intended deployment distribution.
7. Explain split design, duplicates, hard examples, reviewer quality and versioning.
8. Connect one data intervention to a controlled result and identify unresolved gaps.

### Small example / sanity check

“I reduced p95 latency from 800 to 500 ms on a matched workload while keeping task accuracy within the predefined margin” states both benefit and guardrail.

### Common trap

Saying “we built an LLM system” without explaining your choices or how success was measured.
