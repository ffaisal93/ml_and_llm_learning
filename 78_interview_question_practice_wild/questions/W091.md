## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Scientist onsite round — a reasoning prompt, not coding. Pick a metric (e.g. ETA vs session conversion) and design an experiment to measure a causal effect on Uber&#x27;s marketplace.

Cover: metric definition, choice of experimentation framework (A/B vs switchback), treatment/bucket design and guardrails, rollout strategy, and confidence-interval interpretation for ratio metrics. Be fast and precise in defining the metric.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Design a switchback experiment for a shared marketplace. Time-block treatment assignment can reduce simultaneous cross-treatment interference, but introduces time effects and carryover concerns.
2. Define the interacting unit, such as a region-time block, and randomize a balanced treatment schedule.
3. Choose block length and washout periods based on system response and persistence, not convenience alone.
4. Analyze at the assignment unit with time and region structure, checking carryover and enough independent blocks.
5. Interpret a confidence interval that includes zero. The interval describes uncertainty under a sampling procedure; it does not prove no meaningful effect exists.
6. State the estimated effect and interval in practical units.
7. Compare the entire interval with the smallest meaningful benefit and acceptable harm, not just zero.
8. Check power, design and uncertainty assumptions before deciding to launch, gather more data or reject the change.

### Small example / sanity check

Alternating dispatch policies within one shared fleet by individual rider can contaminate both arms; region-time assignments may fit better.

### Common trap

Treating thousands of rides inside one time block as thousands of independent randomized units.
