## Question

Scientist onsite round — a reasoning prompt, not coding. Pick a metric (e.g. ETA vs session conversion) and design an experiment to measure a causal effect on Uber's marketplace.

Cover: metric definition, choice of experimentation framework (A/B vs switchback), treatment/bucket design and guardrails, rollout strategy, and confidence-interval interpretation for ratio metrics. Be fast and precise in defining the metric.

## Worked solution

### Pick one estimand

Suppose treatment is a new dispatch policy. Define the primary metric as completed trips divided by eligible rider sessions in a market-time block, with eligibility fixed before treatment. State the session boundary and attribution window. Guardrails include pickup ETA, cancellation, driver earnings/utilization, safety signals and platform reliability. If treatment changes the denominator, report numerator and denominator separately so a favorable ratio does not hide fewer useful sessions.

### Choose switchback because the marketplace is shared

Ordinary rider-level A/B assignment works when one rider's treatment does not materially change another's outcome. Dispatch changes shared driver availability, violating that assumption. Randomize sufficiently isolated geographic markets or market-time blocks, alternating control and treatment in a randomized, balanced schedule. Block by market, day-of-week and time-of-day to control predictable demand patterns.

Choose block lengths from the time required for dispatch/supply effects to dissipate, not for convenient dashboard refresh. Include washout periods or analyze carryover explicitly. Adjacent geographic areas can still exchange drivers, so examine spillovers and use larger clusters or buffer regions where needed. Do not describe a deterministic “treatment every evening” schedule as randomized.

### Analyze the actual randomization unit

Estimate the treatment contrast using block-level or appropriately clustered data, accounting for serial correlation and market heterogeneity. The effective sample size is related to randomized blocks/markets, not millions of correlated individual sessions. Precompute power using historical variability and the switchback schedule.

For a ratio $R=\bar X/\bar Y$, the delta-method variance is approximately

$$\operatorname{Var}(R)\approx\frac{\operatorname{Var}(\bar X)}{\mu_Y^2}
+\frac{\mu_X^2\operatorname{Var}(\bar Y)}{\mu_Y^4}
-\frac{2\mu_X\operatorname{Cov}(\bar X,\bar Y)}{\mu_Y^3}.$$

Estimate these terms with the randomization/cluster structure intact, or bootstrap randomized clusters/blocks. Do not take a mean of block ratios when the estimand is a pooled trips/session ratio without explaining the weighting. Unstable near-zero denominators require a different analysis, potentially Fieller-style intervals.

### Interpret and roll out

A 95% confidence interval refers to coverage of a repeated-sampling procedure; it does not assign a 95% probability to a fixed true effect under frequentist inference. An interval crossing zero is inconclusive for a zero-effect test, not proof of no effect. A positive interval can still be too small to matter, while a nonsignificant result can still include harmful effects.

Run an instrumentation/A-A check, ramp a small set of markets, then expand according to predeclared utility and guardrail criteria. Monitor sample-ratio anomalies, demand shocks, logging changes and carryover. Use fixed analysis times or a valid sequential method rather than repeatedly peeking until significance appears. Keep the dispatch rollback available throughout the experiment.
