## Question

Let S be symmetric positive definite and v0 nonzero. Define v_(i+1)=S*v_i/||S*v_i||². Analyze direction, even/odd norms, convergence, scaling, repeated top eigenvalues and nonzero fixed-point stability. Compare with the ordinary unit-normalized power method; treat zero top-eigenspace projection separately.

## Worked solution

### Direction follows the power method; magnitude does not

Let $a_i=\|S^iv_0\|$ and $u_i=S^iv_0/a_i$. Positive scalar normalization does not change direction, so $v_i=r_i u_i$ with $r_i>0$ and

$$L_i=\|Su_i\|=a_{i+1}/a_i,\qquad r_{i+1}=1/(r_iL_i).$$

Expand v0 in an orthonormal eigenbasis. Let lambda* be the largest eigenvalue with a nonzero projection of v0 and let u* be the normalized projection onto its eigenspace. Then $u_i\to u_*$, including repeated lambda*: there is no unique eigenvector independent of initialization. If v0 has zero projection on the global top eigenspace, the exact iteration stays in its invariant active subspace; lambda* is smaller, although numerical perturbations may eventually introduce the true top direction.

For SPD S, $L_i$ increases toward lambda* (the successive power-norm ratios are log-convex), hence $r_{i+2}/r_i=L_i/L_{i+1}\le1$. Both even and odd norm subsequences decrease and converge to positive E and O with $EO=1/\lambda_*$. Thus the generic limit is a two-cycle $Eu_*,Ou_*$, not a unit vector and not necessarily a single point. The finite products of ratios determine E/O from v0; scaling v0 by c>0 scales E by c and O by 1/c. Ordinary unit-normalized power iteration instead has norm one every step and converges directionally to u*.

A nonzero fixed point is any vector v in an eigenvalue-lambda eigenspace with $\|v\|=1/\sqrt\lambda$. Along that eigendirection, every other starting radius alternates exactly between r and $1/(\lambda r)$. Example: S=[4], v0=1 gives 1, 1/4, 1, 1/4,...; v0=1/2 is fixed. These facts rule out a general convergence claim even in one dimension.

At a fixed point, $DF(v)=S/\lambda-2\lambda vv^T$. The radial multiplier is -1, tangential directions in the same eigenspace have multiplier +1, and other eigen-directions have multiplier lambda_j/lambda. A top-eigenvalue fixed point has no expanding linear directions but is not asymptotically attracting: radial perturbations retain a two-cycle and repeated-top rotations are neutral. A lower-eigenvalue fixed point is unstable to any higher-eigenvalue component. SPD excludes zero eigenvalues and ensures the denominator never vanishes for nonzero v. Tests check the direction, radial two-cycle, scaling and repeated-eigenspace examples numerically.
