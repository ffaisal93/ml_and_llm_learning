## Question

<pre class="question-text" style="white-space:pre-wrap;font-family:inherit;line-height:1.55">Explain the CAP theorem and use it to reason about a replicated service during a network partition. The response should distinguish guarantees from common database labels and turn the theorem into concrete design decisions.

Questions
1.
Define consistency, availability, and partition tolerance precisely in the CAP model.
2.
Explain why a system experiencing a partition cannot guarantee both consistency and availability for every request.
3.
For two replicas separated by a partition, trace what a consistency-first design does and what an availability-first design does when reads and writes arrive on both sides.
4.
Give representative consistency-first and availability-first systems, qualifying any configurable behavior rather than assigning a permanent label.
5.
Explain why describing CAP as simply &quot;choose any two&quot; is misleading, and relate CAP to the latency-versus-consistency choice captured by PACELC when no partition exists.
6.
Recommend policies for the two scenarios below, including client-visible errors, conflict handling, and recovery after communication resumes.
Scenarios
A replicated payment ledger must prevent conflicting committed balances, even if one partition cannot serve writes temporarily.
A reaction counter should accept updates during a regional isolation and may converge after replicas reconnect.
Constraints
Assume asynchronous communication: messages can be delayed or lost long enough that a node cannot distinguish a partition from a slow peer.
Use linearizability for CAP consistency and a response from every non-failing node for CAP availability.
Discuss behavior during the partition and reconciliation afterward; normal-operation replication alone is not enough.</pre>

## Worked solution

1. Explain CAP during a real network partition. Under the CAP model, a partitioned service cannot guarantee both linearizable behavior and a successful available response to every request at every non-failing node.
2. State what counts as consistency and availability in this model.
3. Trace conflicting reads and writes on isolated replicas: a consistency-first design may reject or delay some operations; an availability-first design may accept divergence.
4. Explain reconciliation after communication returns and normal-operation latency tradeoffs separately.

### Small example / sanity check

A ledger and a reaction counter can reasonably choose different policies because the consequences of conflicts differ.

### Common trap

Reducing CAP to “choose any two” without discussing an actual partition or the meaning of guarantees.
