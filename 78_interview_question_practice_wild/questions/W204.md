## Question

A conversation with the hiring manager mixing standard behavioral prompts with deep, role-specific domain interrogation. For Ads roles the pivot lands on ad-tech mechanics; for ML Platform on production ML system internals. The round most loops die in despite clean technical performance.

Structure
1. Opening (5-10 min): recent-work walkthrough; a highlight/lowlight per project (need 3+ distinct pairs).
2. Behavioral block (15-20 min): disagreement, scope expansion, ambiguity, biggest failure at L5/L6 depth.
3. Domain interrogation (15-20 min, the differentiator): Ads (auction mechanics, pacing, bid shading, attribution, SKAN/IDFA), ML Platform (feature store internals, serving, training pipeline, monitoring), or Subreddit/Community infra (moderation pipeline, trust & safety, sharding).
4. Closing (5 min): candidate questions that reveal genuine team-specific interest.

Candidates fail here for lack of hands-on domain depth to answer live follow-ups; the Ads pivot is the most-cited trap.

## Worked solution

### Prepare the opening and the domain pivot

No resume or role specialization is given. Use the appropriate branch below, and research changing product policies separately. The following project examples are **illustrative**, not personal accomplishments.

Prepare three highlight/lowlight pairs: (1) a serving migration improved isolation, but a cache-key error caused stale results; (2) a training pipeline made runs reproducible, but late labels invalidated its initial split; (3) an experimentation service sped analysis, but shared traffic allocation caused interference. For each, replace these with real evidence, own a specific decision, and state the corrective action. An L5/L6 story needs the boundary you coordinated across, not an inflated headcount.

For disagreement, state the competing goals fairly: “The product lead wanted a broad launch; I wanted a holdout because feedback was delayed. We agreed to a capped rollout and a prewritten rollback threshold.” For ambiguity, write the objective and non-goals; for scope expansion, identify what you deferred; for failure, describe detection, mitigation, communication and prevention. Use actual results or explicitly acknowledge that no trustworthy measurement exists.

**Ads follow-up.** Distinguish bid, predicted click/conversion probability and ranking score. In a simplified value auction, ranking might use `bid × p(click) × quality`; payment depends on the actual auction rules, so never assume every system is second price. Pacing spreads a campaign budget through time; a controller lowers participation or bids when spend is ahead of target. Bid shading reduces a first-price bid below estimated value while trading win probability against cost. Attribution assigns credit after exposure; delayed conversions and missing identifiers bias naive labels. Privacy-limited aggregate attribution requires delayed/aggregated measurement and uncertainty, rather than reconstructing identities. Explain a budget overspend race: reserve spend atomically before accepting a charge and reconcile actual spend idempotently.

**ML Platform follow-up.** Trace an event through feature computation, offline storage, point-in-time training join, model registry and online feature lookup. The key invariant is identical feature semantics and no future-data leakage. State freshness, missing-feature defaults, model rollback and skew monitoring. A cache hit does not prove the feature is current.

**Community infrastructure follow-up.** Trace content creation to synchronous policy checks, asynchronous classifiers, review queues, decisions and appeals. Partition content by community or content ID with hot-community mitigation, version moderation decisions, and preserve audit evidence with restricted access. Measure false-positive removals and appeal overturns as well as queue time.

Close with domain questions: who owns budget correctness for Ads, freshness SLOs for ML Platform, or appeal quality for moderation? These reveal the actual constraints you would work under.
